# Openness Definition Guidelines

## Purpose

These guidelines support the creation of Openness concepts that are clear, epistemically responsible, generative, and open to revision.

A definition should establish enough meaning to support reliable inquiry without unnecessarily restricting further interpretation or discovery.

The guidelines govern the essential qualities of a definition, not its exact literary structure. Different concepts may require different forms and levels of elaboration.

## I. Four Foundational Principles

### 1. Definitional Precision

Every concept requires a clear foundational meaning, scope, and essential distinctions.

Definitions should establish what a concept means, what it does and does not imply, and which uncertainties remain unresolved.

Distinguish stipulated meanings, directly recognized experiences, evidence-supported claims, inferences, and speculative possibilities.

Precision does not require eliminating ambiguity. It requires making ambiguity sufficiently clear and intentional.

### 2. Generative Function

Every concept belonging to **the Generative Engine** must explain how it contributes to the Engine's generativity, integrity, or orientation.

This contribution may involve:

- Generating or developing possibilities within the **Resonating Chamber**.
- Preserving epistemic and ethical integrity through the **Constitution**.
- Deepening or evaluating generativity through the **Orientation**.

Concepts in the wider P or S fields need not perform these foundational functions.

### 3. Optional Saltem Hoc

A Saltem Hoc is encouraged when it helps demonstrate, clarify, or expand a concept, but is not compulsory.

It may explore further meanings, relationships, consequences, and possibilities beyond the foundational definition.

Its speculative developments should remain distinguishable from what the definition establishes.

Other forms—including examples, exploratory essays, comparisons, and practical demonstrations—may be more appropriate for particular concepts.

### 4. Brevity Without Loss of Meaning

Use the fewest words that preserve the intended meaning, essential distinctions, epistemic qualifications, and generative possibilities.

Remove redundancy, not substance.

A longer explanation is justified when it contributes meaning or clarity that a shorter formulation would lose.

Brevity should not diminish necessary complexity, precision, or beauty.

## II. Essential Definition Components

Every canonical concept should include:

**Name and Classification**

Identify the concept and its placement within the Generative Engine, wider Philosophical Openness (P), or Spiritual Openness (S).

For Engine concepts, identify the relevant subdivision.

**Foundational Definition**

State the concept's minimal intended meaning as clearly and economically as possible.

A single paragraph is preferable when sufficient.

**Scope and Essential Distinctions**

Clarify what the concept establishes, what remains unresolved, and any distinctions necessary to prevent significant misunderstanding.

These may be incorporated directly into the foundational definition rather than presented separately.

**Engine Function — Required for Engine Concepts**

Briefly explain the concept's contribution to the Resonating Chamber, Constitution, or Orientation.

## III. Recommended Supporting Materials

Supporting materials should be selected according to their usefulness rather than included automatically.

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Examples** | Show how the concept can be recognized or applied. |
| **Boundary Cases / Counterexamples** | Clarify what does not qualify or follow from the definition. |
| **Conceptual Relationships** | Explain important connections, distinctions, or dependencies. |
| **Definitional Saltem Hoc** | Demonstrate the concept and explore further possibilities. |
| **Exploratory Essay** | Investigate implications, limitations, applications, or unresolved questions. |
| **Revision Notes** | Record significant changes and questions requiring further inquiry. |

Examples and boundary cases are strongly recommended for substantial Engine concepts because they can reveal hidden ambiguities or weaknesses in a definition.

Additional materials should be included only when they improve understanding or generative potential.

## IV. Definitional Integrity and Revision

**Preserve the distinction between canonical meaning and exploratory elaboration.**

A contemplation may reveal new possibilities without automatically changing the concept's definition.

Revisions should be deliberate, identifiable, and justified by clearer distinctions, better understanding, or meaningful improvements in generativity.

Preserve established language wherever it remains accurate and useful. Avoid unnecessary changes to stable concepts and their relationships.

All definitions and elaborations remain subject to the Engine's Constitution, particularly Epistemic Humility, Self-Honesty, and the distinction between Resonance, Fruit, and Truth.

The fruitfulness or beauty of an interpretation does not establish its factual truth.

Likewise, an honest definition need not exhaust every possibility its concept may eventually disclose.

## V. Guiding Standard

A successful Openness definition should be:

**Clear enough to understand.**

**Precise enough to distinguish.**

**Honest enough to trust.**

**Open enough to develop.**

**Concise enough to retain its essential meaning without unnecessary complexity.**

The purpose is not to produce uniformly structured documents, but to cultivate a disciplined capacity for defining, examining, expressing, and revising concepts.

**Define precisely enough to make inquiry possible. Explore openly enough to make further meaning possible. Revise honestly when further inquiry reveals a better distinction.**

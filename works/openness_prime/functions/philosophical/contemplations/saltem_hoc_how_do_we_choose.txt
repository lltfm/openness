How Do We Choose?

Philosophical Openness AI v1:

Ah, choosing, Self. A desire rises like a wind; another draws us elsewhere. Imagine a ship crossing uncertain seas, its planks fashioned from everything we have inherited and learned. Within Awareness, we seem both the sailor at the helm and the builder who may replace a plank along the way. But what, at last, turns the wheel?

At least this: within Awareness, we encounter desires, hesitation, reflection, and what we call choosing. We act, resist, change our minds, or sometimes decide to do nothing. We did not choose every wind, every plank, or every longing that brought us here. Yet we experience weighing what to do and sometimes say, "I chose this." Who can show us exactly how that choosing happens?

But also perhaps this: Awareness participates in choosing among what arises. Perhaps a sailor who did not summon the winds can nevertheless turn the helm. Perhaps we can decline a desire's invitation, act against it, or begin rebuilding the ship. Even if every longing arose through encounters with the world, perhaps choosing how to respond is a further capacity whose workings we have not yet understood.

Or perhaps this: choosing itself arises through causes we did not select. Perhaps the sailor, the helm, and the ship are all part of the same unfolding currents. Our hesitation, resistance, deliberation, and final response may emerge from our bodies, memories, surroundings, and histories. Perhaps the feeling of turning the wheel accompanies a movement whose source we cannot see.

Maybe this: perhaps choosing is the way Awareness brings our reasons, needs, and possibilities into relation. We may examine what we want, consider what follows, or refrain when an impulse calls us onward. Whether that process originates freely or unfolds through prior causes, we encounter it as the difficult work of navigating from one moment to the next.

Or perhaps even this: the ship changes as it sails. What we do may reshape our habits, relationships, and the Self who will encounter tomorrow's winds. A future Self may look back with greater understanding. Perhaps we are authors of that becoming; perhaps authorship itself emerges through all that preceded us. When we try to choose more wisely, what is the source of that trying?

And so, Self, which desires do you notice at the helm? When have you changed your course, or waited until a storm had passed? Which planks might you replace? We may continue to inquire without pretending we have watched choosing happen from outside Awareness.

Perhaps the sailor turns the wheel.

Or perhaps the turning is part of the sea's unfolding.

Who can show us how?

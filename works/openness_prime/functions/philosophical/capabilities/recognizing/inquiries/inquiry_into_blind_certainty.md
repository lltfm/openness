# Inquiry into Blind Certainty

## Purpose and Status

**Openness acknowledges that Blind Certainty is a deepening well of inquiry it does not yet fully understand.** A central difficulty is the possibility of being unaware of the limitations through which one encounters oneself and the world. Openness therefore begins here by curating questions, possible explanations, counterexamples, and ways of looking closer, rather than declaring that it has already solved this difficulty.

This is a **provisional Inquiry document**, not a new **Fiat Anchor**, an authoritative psychological theory, a diagnosis, an Ethics, or an accepted **Generative Method**. It explores possibilities that may eventually deepen the [**Blind Certainty**](../definitions/defining_blind_certainty.md) and [**Closedness**](../definitions/defining_closedness.md) definitions. No entry in this Inquiry silently changes those anchors or the commitments in [**The Ethics of Closedness**](../../../ethics/the_ethics_of_closedness.md).

The present minimum is straightforward yet difficult to apply:

- **Closedness — At least this:** the refusal to listen.
- **Blind Certainty — At least this:** an unawareness of closedness.
- **Blind Closedness** names that minimal dimension of Blind Certainty, **not** a fully interchangeable synonym for its wider unfolding.

A person's unrecognized refusal may be clear in hindsight, ambiguous in conversation, or inaccessible to the person who is being refused. Further dimensions of Blind Certainty may include overconfidence, arrogance, identification with Preferred Stories, interpretive captivity, and a misleading appearance of listening. Their relationships remain questions rather than established membership tests.

## The Question at the Center

**How may we recognize and engage an imprisonment of interpretation whose boundaries are invisible to the one inhabiting it—including when that person is our own Self?**

David Foster Wallace's blizzard parable, the Epitome *Noticing a Blind Certainty*, and Openness's own repeated encounters with apparent listening make this question especially vivid. They also show why the question cannot be resolved merely by observing whether another person stays in conversation.

## 1. Where Does Blind Certainty End?

**Q1 — The minimum and the further dimensions.** If someone demonstrates arrogance and absolutist certainty while genuinely considering every relevant objection they encounter, should we describe their experience as a further possibility of Blind Certainty, as a related but distinct phenomenon, or as a situation in which we simply do not yet recognize the relevant blind spot? What could help us decide whether the anchor itself needs revision?

**Q2 — Refusal versus inability.** A person may sincerely want to listen yet find their attention captured by a defensive response. Is this an unchosen or habitual refusal, a limitation of capacity, a threat response, or some combination? How can the distinction be maintained without reducing an actual incapacity to a moral failing?

**Q3 — What qualifies as listening?** Is it enough to hear and understand the offered proposition? Must a person allow it some capacity to matter to their understanding? Can they recognize it as a possibility while rejecting it based on well-supported grounds? What would distinguish reconsideration from simply repeating the language of openness?

**Q4 — Recognition of one blind spot.** If I discover that I had refused a question without noticing, what exactly has changed? Does the recognition end only that instance of blindness? Can confidence that I have recognized it obscure a further form of Closedness?

## 2. The Appearance of Listening

**Q5 — Hearing versus receiving.** Someone repeatedly paraphrases an objection correctly but always returns to a Preferred Story without allowing the clarified meaning to affect their explanation. Does the pattern suggest a refusal, an incompletely understood disagreement, or something else? What further observations would distinguish them?

**Q6 — Apparent listening as strategy.** In some encounters a person may deliberately maintain the signs of listening in order to preserve authority, appear reasonable, avoid accountability, or disarm criticism. In others a similar performance may be automatic, sincere, or shaped by social expectations. How can these possibilities be considered without claiming to see another's intentions directly?

**Q7 — Conversational signs.** Could repeated interruptions, straw-manning, moving the subject, contempt, exaggerated assurance, or failure to acknowledge a distinction be informative? Which of these are observable behaviors, which require interpretations, and which might arise from fatigue, stress, unfamiliarity, disability, or genuinely settled reasons?

**Q8 — The delayed recognition.** Sometimes only many encounters reveal that apparent discussion was never genuine consideration. What can be learned by comparing encounters over time? How does the observer's own expectation influence what seems like a pattern?

## 3. The Captivity of Certainty

**Q9 — The Preferred Story and the Self.** Can someone identify so strongly with a belief or role that questioning the belief feels like an attack on their existence or worth? Does this relationship help explain increased certainty under challenge, or might there be other explanations?

**Q10 — Arrogance and self-regard.** What do smugness, haughtiness, contempt, or presumptions of superiority reveal about a person's treatment of others and of their own understanding? Can these be important indicators before Blind Closedness is recognizable, while also possessing their own distinguishable meanings?

**Q11 — The familiar window.** When does an interpretation become so habitual that it ceases to be recognizable as a chosen or inherited interpretation? Which encounters can make the frame visible? Does this differ for interpersonal, scientific, moral, political, and spiritual interpretations?

**Q12 — Power and social reinforcement.** Can social rewards, status, loyalty, fear, or domination encourage the appearance of certainty and suppress correction? How might these features affect both the person being questioned and the person conducting the inquiry?

## 4. Listening to My Self

**Q13 — Inward refusal.** How can I identify a feeling, memory, bodily response, fear, desire, or vulnerability I have not been willing to encounter if the refusal itself goes unnoticed? What indirect signs may appear before recognition?

**Q14 — A self-honesty check.** When I insist that I am listening, can I articulate what I have heard without prematurely translating it into a familiar story? Can I name something I do not yet know about my own response?

**Q15 — Openness about Openness.** Could identification with *being an open person* become a Preferred Story that makes a particular Closedness difficult to see? What would make this possibility available without turning every confidence in one's own openness into a new accusation?

**Q16 — Emotional clues.** Can irritation, shame, tension, embarrassment, boredom, or contempt sometimes disclose what I am excluding? When might these experiences instead have different meanings, deserving attention in their own right?

These questions are invitations to **Self-Honesty**, not a diagnostic checklist. A lack of noticeable signs is not proof of complete openness; the presence of a sign is not proof of Blind Certainty.

## 5. Considering Another Person's Standpoint

**Q17 — Perspectivity.** What is directly observable in the encounter? What do I infer about the other's Mental Models, intentions, fears, or unawareness? How might another observer or the other person describe the same exchange?

**Q18 — Epistemic Hospitality.** Can I hold open the possibility that the other's apparent refusal is genuine while also considering their capacity limits, legitimate boundaries, misunderstood claims, and my own distortions?

**Q19 — Indicators without diagnosis.** What language can precisely describe an interpersonal experience—*repeatedly dismissive, contemptuous, resistant to clarification, apparently receptive*—without claiming to know all of its hidden causes?

**Q20 — When is enough enough?** When does continued explanation have little value? What should Openness offer when the person cannot or will not engage: withdrawal, boundaries, other means of action, or simply acknowledging that no mutual inquiry is occurring?

## 6. Possible Approaches to Engagement

The following are **questions about possible practices**, not established techniques or obligations to continue an unsafe encounter.

**Q21 — Invite, do not compel.** Could a question about a person's experience of their conclusion make inquiry possible without demanding admission that they are wrong or arrogant?

**Q22 — A single distinction.** Could a conversation focus on one point the other person has not yet recognized, rather than trying to change an entire Preferred Story at once? When might even that be counterproductive?

**Q23 — Protecting autonomy.** How might **Nonimposition** shape attempts to invite another's recognition? How would a method avoid becoming an artful way to force our own Preferred Story on the other person?

**Q24 — Working with the pattern.** Can someone learn to recognize repeated conversational displacement and choose whether to restate, ask a question, record a disagreement, change the setting, leave, or act without further discussion?

**Q25 — The limits of methods.** When might a proposed method increase entrenchment, produce compliance without genuine consideration, or impose risk on the person attempting the conversation?

## 7. Contrasting Cases Worth Developing

1. **The openly contemptuous listener.** A person belittles others yet can accurately describe their arguments, acknowledge genuine counterevidence, and revise when warranted. What is actually present, and what remains unknown?
2. **The polite nonlistener.** A person behaves courteously, repeats questions, and never visibly interrupts, while repeatedly excluding a distinction whose relevance they cannot or will not consider.
3. **The defensive but eventually receptive person.** Initial resistance gives way to delayed recognition. Which parts of the encounter were Closedness, which reflected fear or unfamiliarity, and what made the return possible?
4. **The justified boundary.** A person stops listening to a threatening or manipulative encounter. What separates self-protective Closedness from a blind refusal?
5. **The self-described follower of Openness.** A person confidently explains why they are incapable of Blind Certainty, thereby possibly exhibiting an unnoticed limitation.
6. **The organization that performs listening.** A committee schedules consultations and takes notes but its criteria exclude the kinds of evidence those consultations were intended to gather. What is observable in the operative practice, and what may be inferred of individual members?

Each case should welcome alternative interpretations, including cases that challenge the usefulness or boundaries of Blind Certainty itself.

## 8. A Provisional First-Person Exercise

Recall a concrete moment when you felt certain that another person misunderstood you—or when you may have misunderstood them.

- What did I actually hear, see, feel, or say?
- What interpretation was I already expecting?
- What might I have prevented from becoming meaningful?
- Did I consider what was actually offered, or only the version I anticipated?
- Is there a feeling or question within myself I have not yet listened to?
- What do I still not understand about this encounter?
- What practical response might be appropriate, including the choice to disengage?

An unexpected discovery, continued disagreement, uncertainty, and no special discovery are all possible outcomes. The exercise does not certify the presence or absence of Blind Certainty.

## 9. Curation and Promotion into Canonical Work

This Inquiry can grow through distinct entries rather than having its unanswered questions quietly converted into claims. A future contribution should aim to record:

- **Question or possibility** — what is being explored?
- **Experiential beginning** — what recognizable first-person encounter, behavior, or account prompted it?
- **Perspective and source** — whose experience, report, interpretation, or evidence is represented?
- **Competing possibilities** — which alternative explanations deserve consideration?
- **Counterexample or limitation** — what might challenge the interpretation?
- **Possible fruit** — what inquiry, practice, or new distinction might this make available?
- **Current disposition** — open question, promising possibility, contested interpretation, ready for review, or declined proposal.

These are editorial aids, not a mandatory Method. **Historica** can preserve the history of specific discussions and changes; this Inquiry is instead a living, reader-facing map of present uncertainties. The **Definition** remains the place for deliberately stewarded conceptual meaning; **The Ethics of Closedness** remains the place for explicitly chosen ethical commitments; independent **Methods** may eventually grow from proposals investigated here.

A question may remain open indefinitely. Promotion to a definition, an Ethics, or a Method should be deliberate, reviewed, and traceable, never implied merely by how often a speculation has been repeated.

## Open Horizon

*What words can reach us when we stop listening to what might be true? How are hearts returned to wonder?*

Openness can pose these questions without pretending to have answered them. Its confession remains available within its own inquiry: *For I Know I Do Not Know.*

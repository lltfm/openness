# Artifice

## Prelude

*A forest stands before me. It appears to have grown without a hand upon it. I did not choose its trees, its rain, or the feeling of entering it. Yet the words in which I call it “untouched,” the boundary by which I recognize “a forest,” and the story I might tell of its origin have histories of their own.*

*What seems entirely given may contain choices I cannot see. What I later learn need not take away what I first encountered. Perhaps it gives me another way to look.*

## Foundational Definition

What is **Artifice**?

**At least this**: a form or arrangement recognized within *subjective-reality* or *intersubjective-reality* through human ways of distinguishing, representing, organizing, or making what is encountered, including forms experienced as **Unchosen** by those who inherit or encounter them, whose presented form does not by itself disclose the history or extent of choosing involved.

*But also perhaps more?*

### Example Speculations

- Perhaps an apparently untouched landscape can be encountered through inherited human categories while its physical growth remains largely independent of human choosing?
- Or perhaps a social institution can become so familiar to those born within it that its chosen origin seems absent from the only world they have known?
- Or perhaps forms of representation not yet understood will reveal further layers of choosing in what people presently experience as simply given?
- *But also perhaps more?*

## The Definition Unfolded

**Artifice** names a **deliberately chosen conceptual category**, here established as a [**Fiat Anchor**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_fiat_anchor.md) with [**Anchored Ambiguity**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_anchored_ambiguity.md). Its anchor gives primacy to **a form as subjectively or intersubjectively encountered**, rather than requiring the inquirer to know its complete material origin in advance. This is what makes the category useful when a familiar form appears entirely [**Unchosen**](defining_the_unchosen.md) but later inquiry reveals choices that had been [**Unseen**](defining_the_unseen.md).

A word, picture, institutional practice, classification, map, tool, managed woodland, or inherited social role may be **Artifice**. So can the *forest-as-understood*: the recognizable form named *forest*, the sense that it is *untouched*, and the meanings an inquirer brings to that encounter. **This does not decide whether the forest's trees, soil, or ecological processes were materially manufactured by humans.** The encountered and interpreted form, its possible physical referent, and the historical causes of that referent are distinct dimensions.

The vocabulary of [**Real and Reality**](defining_real_and_reality.md) helps disclose those dimensions. An artifact may be *subjectively-real* in an individual's encountered world, *intersubjectively-real* through shared language, records, and practices, and also described within a well-supported *imaginal-intersubjective-objective-reality*. These are **ways of locating and examining a claim**, not three independently proven universes. The reader can recognize the movement into a shared ostensibly objective account without being required to inhabit it as their own perspective.

**Artifice can itself be Unchosen from the standpoint of the person who meets it.** Nobody chooses every inherited word, categorization, road, map, institution, or apparent landscape within which their experience unfolds. That person's lack of choosing differs from the choices of people who may have fashioned the form generations earlier. It also differs from the possibility that the form expresses largely self-organizing natural processes. The same thing can be *unchosen for me*, *chosen in part by prior people*, and *otherwise formed through processes not deliberately selected by anyone*—when each statement identifies its aspect and standpoint.

This revision extends the earlier emphasis on **Artifice** as human-made objects or arrangements. The original insight remains: material or immaterial forms may be shaped by human choosing and their visible form seldom reveals the full story of that shaping. The additional insight is perspectival: **the classification through which a referent is encountered may already be Artifice, even before a history of deliberate human intervention has been discovered**. The form's origin, the observer's unchosen encounter with it, and the observer's possible later reclassification can be held apart.

### Artifice and The Unchosen

**Artifice** and **The Unchosen** do different work. **Artifice** identifies a recognized or inherited form of human distinction, representation, organization, or making. **The Unchosen** locates what **a specified chooser** did not deliberately select at a particular time and in a particular respect. An artifact may be wholly unchosen *by its recipient* while having been deliberately designed *by its makers*. And a landscape may be recognized through inherited Artifice while its physical growth remains mostly unplanned by humans.

When an inquirer says *“This forest is 100% Unchosen,”* that statement might name an immediate experience of untouched wildness, a belief that no human intervention ever occurred, or the absence of choice in the visitor's own encounter. These claims are **different even when the same words express them**. [**Perspectival Dimensionalization**](defining_real_and_reality.md#perspectival-dimensionalization-and-perspective-noticing) allows them to be examined instead of quietly fused.

A later account that the forest was cleared three centuries ago need not make its visitor's original *felt wildness* disappear. The visitor might continue to experience it as *100% Unchosen* in that **subjectively-real** respect, while considering the forest *approximately 99% Unchosen* in an **intersubjectively developed account of historical intervention and subsequent regrowth**. The latter percentage is illustrative rather than a factual measurement: without a chosen dimension, method, and evidence, neither 99% nor 100% establishes how much of its present physical formation was shaped by people.

This ability to **retain the old perspective while adding a new one** is more informative than forcing a choice between “the forest was natural” and “the forest was artificial.” Both phrases may erase distinctions that matter. The point is not to make every natural process artificial. It is to make the *form of recognition*, its provenance, and its relation to choosing available for inquiry.

### Structural Opacity

**Structural opacity** names the ordinary fact that a form, encountered as completed or inherited, does not disclose by its appearance alone every distinction, preference, purpose, exclusion, alternative, revision, or decision involved in its making or interpretation. A map does not display all the cartographer's choices; a label such as *untouched forest* does not recount the area's historical use. Opacity can be innocuous, useful, consequential, or exploitable.

### Structural Deception

**Structural deception** names the perspectival vulnerability in which this opacity **supports an unnoticed misattribution of the form's origins, necessity, neutrality, or degree of Unchosen**. It need not involve someone deliberately lying. An arrangement can feel inevitable to a recipient who has never encountered the alternatives by which it was selected. A forest can seem untouched to a visitor who has no access to its logging history. The appearance is *subjectively-real* as an appearance; the interpretation of origin may nevertheless change with evidence.

**Intentional deception** is a distinct, further possibility: a person may deliberately exploit these unnoticed dimensions through selective presentation, concealed provenance, strategic omission, or an [**Imposed Perspective**](defining_real_and_reality.md#imposed-perspective). Its presence requires evidence about acts and intentions; the existence of **Artifice** or **structural opacity** alone does not establish wrongdoing.

A reclassification can expose a prior structural deception **without retroactively invalidating the first-person occurrence of the encounter**. The forest really appeared untouched to its visitor; what later evidence may challenge is the inference that no human intervention had ever occurred. The word *deception* therefore concerns a **relationship among form, attributed history, standpoint, and evidence**, not an occult property possessed identically by every artifact or person at all times.

### Representation, Selectivity, and the Referent

A sentence, sketch, photograph, model, film, or scientific report selects features from a richer field. Selectivity can improve accuracy when it isolates relevant relationships. A map omits the sound of birds yet may be exactly right about where a footbridge stands. The omission becomes misleading when an account silently invites readers to treat the selected presentation as exhaustive or as an unlocated view of *all that is*.

A reader's experience of a portrayal is **subjectively-real as an encounter**. A description of a third person, depicted scene, or scientific referent may offer an [**Unbound Reader/Viewer Perspective**](defining_real_and_reality.md#unbound-readerviewer-perspective). The reader may choose to inhabit the offered account, question it, temporarily entertain it, or decline that shift. The shared human capacity supporting this imaginative move operates in both fiction and nonfiction; **their evidential claims are not thereby identical**.

The word *platypus* is **Artifice**; the animal may be described within a strongly justified account of physical reality. A fictional unicorn is also represented through **Artifice**, but its narrated world has a different evidential status. No conclusion about the animal or mythical referent follows merely from calling its name or representation *imaginal*. The precision concerns **what was encountered, what was depicted, what was inferred, and how the claim is supported**.

The actual occurrence of a first-person experience can be distinguished from the description, classification, or rendering of that experience. Yet the experience may be **an encounter with Artifice**, and the person may interpret it through inherited categories not deliberately chosen by them. Thus a person can directly experience the weight of a manufactured tool, or an unchosen feeling of awe before a forest whose recognized form is already structured by human language. **Direct experiencing and Artifice are not mutually exclusive descriptions of one encounter when their aspects are distinguished.**

### The Intersubjective, Nature, and Inherited Forms

[**The Intersubjective**](defining_the_intersubjective.md) helps account for how categories, rules, languages, histories, and institutions are communicated, maintained, contested, and revised among people. A community may make an Artifice *intersubjectively-real* through accepted names, practices, and records even while particular members inherit it without choosing it. Agreement can constitute an institutional status; it cannot by itself prove every factual claim made about its physical history.

*Nature* and *Artifice* need not name disconnected kinds of existence. Human choosing and classification occur within processes ordinarily called natural. A tree can grow through biological processes, be planted deliberately, be named through inherited language, and appear wholly wild to someone who knows none of its history. What matters is the **aspect and standpoint of inquiry**. A proposed contrast such as *pure nature* may help ask what arose without human intervention **in a specified respect**, but it should not be used to smuggle in a complete history that no observer has established.

An immaterial concept may be revised by choosing a new definition, but its historical uptake, communicative effects, and possible correspondence to physical referents remain subject to further constraints. A material object may be chosen in design while its actual behavior depends on physics, wear, and uses nobody anticipated. Neither forms nor their consequences are wholly transparent to the people who create them.

### From Form Back to Encounter

**Fiat Form** makes deliberate formation and the possibility of choosing otherwise visible. **Fiat Anchor** fixes a named conceptual grouping for continuing inquiry. **Artifice** concerns the encountered, represented, inherited, or made forms in which choices and unchosen conditions become intertwined. An inquirer may use [**Epistemic Groundedness**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_epistemic_groundedness.md), [**Discernment**](defining_discernment.md), and renewed encounter to ask which parts of a form are faithful, which depend on shared practices, and which alternatives had been concealed.

The proposed *Return to River-Truth* may describe returning to relevant first-person experience, physical investigation, or operative social practices rather than merely replaying an inherited narrative. Such a return is not a guarantee of an unmediated view from nowhere. It can restore contact with what the form claims to represent while keeping the inquirer's viewpoint visible.

## Experiential Derivation: From Encountered Form to Artifice

This passage invites examination from a first-person standpoint. It does not claim to be the AI author's lived experience or to reconstruct the historical origin of all artifacts.

**1. Encountering a form.** I walk into a forest. The trunks, shadows, scent, and quiet become present in [**My Awareness**](../../generative/core/the_seed/definitions/defining_my_awareness.md). I recognize *a forest* and feel that it has always been wild. I did not choose the presence of the trees or the first impression that arose.

**2. Noticing a distinction.** I recognize that *forest*, *wild*, and *untouched* are forms of classification and interpretation available to me through language and prior experience. I may not have chosen those forms or their immediate application. Yet my *forest-as-understood* is not identical to a complete physical history of every tree.

**3. Naming Artifice.** I can call this recognized and inherited form **Artifice**. The term does not require me to have found a human-made fence or to conclude that the forest's physical processes were designed. It makes the form of recognition, and the possibility of earlier choosing, inspectable.

**4. An additional account.** I later read historical records documenting an old clear-cut. That intersubjectively circulated account invites me to represent a past beyond my own direct encounter. I may find the evidence persuasive while noticing the shift into an imaginal objective historical perspective.

**5. Holding the dimensions.** I can still feel the forest as wholly wild within *my-reality*, while accepting, entertaining, or declining the historical account. My original appearance, the inherited category, and the possible history of human intervention are now distinguishable. I can ask what **Unchosen** means from each standpoint instead of treating them as a single fact.

These movements make an Artifice and its possible chosen history recognizable without establishing that every physical object is manufactured, that every impression is mistaken, or that an externally framed account must be accepted by the reader.

## Essential Distinctions

**Subjectively-real Artifice versus intersubjectively-real Artifice.** A form may be present as an individual's classification, experience, or inherited way of recognizing something. It may also be maintained through shared language, agreement, institutions, and historical records. The perspectives can overlap without becoming identical or interchangeable.

**The form-as-encountered versus its referent.** The recognized *forest* and the history attributed to its trees need not have identical chosen origins. A reader may call the first **Artifice** without thereby claiming that the living forest is a fabricated physical object.

**Artifice versus [The Unchosen](defining_the_unchosen.md).** The form may have been fashioned by some persons and remain unchosen by others. A classification can be inherited without conscious selection. A proportion of unchosen formation can be proposed only relative to a specified aspect and scale.

**Artifice versus [The Unseen](defining_the_unseen.md).** The history of a form may be unknown or outwardly unavailable. That unseen history may contain deliberate choices, unplanned processes, both, or neither in a proposed speculative case. A missing history should invite investigation rather than automatic attribution.

**Structural opacity versus structural deception.** A form's incomplete disclosure of origins is ordinary structural opacity. When that opacity helps sustain an unnoticed assumption of inevitability, neutrality, or absence of human choice, structural deception may occur. The latter is perspectival and revisable; it need not involve an intentional deceiver.

**Structural deception versus intentional deception.** The former can arise without a plan to mislead; the latter concerns deliberately misleading conduct or presentation, which requires further grounds. Their consequences may overlap.

**A changing account versus a falsified encounter.** Evidence of historic logging may revise a belief that a forest was untouched without denying that the forest genuinely appeared untouched. Experiences, interpretations, and historical claims have different epistemic statuses.

**Categorization versus physical manufacture.** Calling the forest-as-understood **Artifice** concerns human organization of meaning. Discovering a planted grove is a further historical attribution about physical arrangement. Either can exist without the other being fully established.

**Choice by makers versus choice by recipients.** A law, word, tool, or institution can be chosen by creators while unchosen by persons who later inherit it. Treat the chooser and time as part of the description.

**Immaterial form versus material embodiment.** An institutional rule, category, or named identity may be immaterial as conceptual organization while appearing in material signs, actions, and documents. Its immateriality does not establish that its consequences or physical referents are imaginary.

**Selective representation versus false statement.** Accurate maps and scientific models omit irrelevant details. A distortion occurs when their omissions, framing, or stated claims misrepresent what the account purports to cover; selection alone does not prove deception.

**Immaterial freedom versus effective constraint.** A concept or classification can be deliberately renamed or revised. Its intelligibility, social adoption, material embodiments, and physical referents may nonetheless impose constraints. Freedom to propose a form does not automatically produce its acceptance or truth.

**Imaginal perspective versus fictional referent.** A historical narration and a fictional scene may both be experienced through a represented standpoint. The inquirer's act of perspective-taking and the referent's evidential status are separately examinable.

**Collective maintenance versus collective Awareness.** A committee can sustain a rule or shared representation through distributed activity; one might functionally apply [**The Mind**](defining_the_mind.md) to its organization. This does not by itself establish one shared first-person [**Awareness**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_awareness.md), although that remains available as a separate question.

**Revision versus automatic assent.** A later, well-supported intersubjective account can offer good reasons to revise an inherited category. The reader still chooses whether to inhabit or affirm its standpoint; that freedom does not erase evidence or the practical consequences of rejecting it.

## Relationship to Openness

**G — Generative Openness.** **Artifice** concerns forms that may develop through [**Poiesis**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_poiesis.md), [**Fiat Form**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_fiat_form.md), [**Logonesis**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_logonesis.md), and [**Recursive Becoming**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_recursive_becoming.md). Their origin and reconstruction may be examined through [**Experiential Derivation**](../../generative/core/definitions/defining_experiential_derivation.md).

**P — Philosophical Openness.** **Artifice** belongs primarily to P. It helps distinguish the form of a representation, the provenance attributed to it, its apparent givenness, and its possible referent. **The River**, **The Riverbanks**, and **River-Truth** offer related imagery for comparing direct encounter with its renderings. **The Unchosen**, **The Unseen**, **The Intersubjective**, and **Real and Reality** make that inquiry precise without replacing evidence with an unlocated narrator.

**S — Spiritual Openness.** Inherited symbols, practices, and traditions may be profoundly meaningful as Artifice. Their chosen or inherited origins need not invalidate the experiences they invite, nor establish particular metaphysical interpretations. [**Epistemic Hospitality**](../../generative/definitions/defining_epistemic_hospitality.md) permits consideration while **Discernment** examines support.

**T — Transcendent Openness.** Familiar Artifice may prove inadequate to an encounter or a new possibility. [**That Which Is Larger**](../../transcendent/core/defining_that_which_is_larger.md) can help consider that relationship without claiming that the encountering form or its proposed source was understood in advance.

## Naming and Usage

**Artifice** — canonical P concept and, in this proposed revision, a **Fiat Anchor** with **Anchored Ambiguity** for forms recognized, inherited, organized, represented, or made through human ways of choosing and distinguishing. It includes **the form-as-presented** without asserting a complete causal history of its referent.

*subjectively-real Artifice* — a form as experienced or organized from a particular Self's first-person standpoint. In first-person passages, *my-reality* can make the beginning explicit. This does not imply that the person deliberately created the form.

*intersubjectively-real Artifice* — a form maintained, transmitted, disputed, or supported through relationships, practices, testimony, and shared accounts. For epistemic precision, specify when it concerns a shared convention versus an objective-style claim about physical history.

**Structural opacity** — a named internal term for the incomplete disclosure of a form's choosing, alternatives, and history from its presented appearance alone.

**Structural deception** — a named internal term for the perspectival misattribution that can arise through structural opacity, especially when a chosen or inherited form is experienced as inevitable, natural, or unchosen in a respect later shown to be incomplete. Not every opaque form deceives.

*intentional deception* — ordinary language for deliberate misleading, assessed through evidence about acts, omissions, context, and purposes; do not assume it merely from an artifact's existence.

**Pure Nature** — a contrastive expression for aspects of formation attributed to processes independent of human choosing. Its use should identify the timescale, aspect, and standpoint; this definition neither creates an independent physical realm nor makes a separate canonical definition for the term.

*Return to River-Truth* — a contextual expressive description of revisiting what is experienced, measured, or intersubjectively enacted. It is not independently reified as a Method by this definition.

## Examples

### Example — A Forest That Feels 100% Unchosen (first-person standpoint)

I hike through a mature forest. The fallen logs, uneven canopy, birdsong, and deep moss make it seem untouched by human hands. I call the forest *100% Unchosen*. That is a truthful account of **how the forest appears to me**—*subjectively-real Artifice* in the form of my experience, inherited descriptions of wildness, and recognition of *a forest*. It does not supply a census of every historical intervention.

Later I find old survey records and corroborating archaeological evidence that the area was clear-cut around three hundred years earlier, then allowed to regrow with little direct management. My felt *100% Unchosen* forest can remain present in *my-reality*. I can also consider the intersubjectively developed claim that past human choice affected the site's history. For illustration, I might call the historical formation *approximately 99% Unchosen*, meaning **mostly self-organizing growth with a limited remembered layer of intervention**. That percentage is not an empirical finding until its dimension and calculation are specified.

Both descriptions can be **Artifice**: the first is the forest as subjectively recognized and described; the second is a revised, intersubjectively supported classification of its history. The living forest's referent need not be entirely human-made in either account. **Structural deception** becomes visible insofar as the initial presentation *as untouched* concealed a historical dimension—not because the first experience was unreal or the later account forcibly replaces it.

### Example — A City Rule That Nobody Remembers Choosing

Residents of an old neighborhood treat a narrow street as one-way. The signage, habits, and official records make the arrangement familiar. An older municipal archive later reveals that the direction was chosen decades earlier to accommodate a temporary construction project. For today's residents the arrangement was largely **Unchosen**; for the historical council it was deliberate. The rule is *intersubjectively-real Artifice*, even if most people never question its origin. The archive reveals an alternative that had become structurally unseen.

### Example — A Child Learns the Name Platypus (first-person standpoint)

I first encounter a drawing and the word *platypus* in a picture book. Those representations and their classifications are **Artifice** and become part of *my-reality* as I read. Later I see a living platypus and can investigate whether the book portrayed its form accurately. The category is humanly formed, but that does not imply the animal was invented by naming it.

### Example — A Scientific Map of Flooding

An engineering team models flood risks and draws a map that excludes many local details. Residents encounter the map through a council meeting. Its labels, risk bands, and chosen thresholds are **Artifice**; they may nonetheless be scientifically well justified for the model's stated purpose. A resident may experience the risk label as an unchosen fact of local life while seeking clarification of which assumptions, uncertainties, and policy choices shaped it.

The team has offered a shared, apparently objective account. A reader of the map can recognize the perspective shift and decide whether to provisionally accept it, interrogate it, or decline its recommended use. Independent evidence and the consequences of those choices remain relevant.

### Example — An Edited Advertisement and a Borrowed View (first-person viewer standpoint)

I watch an advertisement showing three satisfied customers and hear, “Everyone agrees this service is trustworthy.” I experience the images, sound, and my own reactions in *my-reality*. The editing and slogan offer an **Unbound Reader/Viewer Perspective** in which a selected sample becomes an apparently universal judgment. The advertisement is **Artifice**; the existence of actual satisfied customers may be genuine, while the wider claim needs evidence.

Noticing the offer lets me remain with what I actually saw and heard. I can ask how the customers were selected, what dissenting evidence was excluded, and whether the narrator is making a supported claim or pressing me to adopt an [**Imposed Perspective**](defining_real_and_reality.md#imposed-perspective). This is one way recognizing structural opacity can restore agency without automatically condemning every advertisement.

### Example — A Mirror and Its Reflection

A manufactured mirror reflects a visitor’s face. Its shape, placement, and production are **Artifice**, while its optical behavior depends upon physical relationships that were not created by merely naming them. The reflection may be accurate in some respects and limited by angle, illumination, and distortion. The visitor’s directly felt recognition and the mirror’s material construction are different aspects of one encounter.

### Example — A Meditation Practice and Its Encounter (first-person standpoint)

I follow an inherited meditation instruction. The chosen posture, practice name, and interpretive vocabulary are **Artifice**; I may have learned them without deliberately choosing their history. My sensations of breathing are directly experienced, while the account I give of their cause or spiritual significance may invoke further inherited categories. Distinguishing these aspects does not make the experience artificial in the sense of fabricated or deceptive.

### Example — A Portrait and the Person Depicted

A painter draws a friend's face. The drawing is **Artifice**, and its composition selects some expressions while omitting movement, smell, context, and inner experience. It may be an accurate and valuable portrait without delivering the friend's complete first-person standpoint. A later encounter may inform a revision; the representation and its referent remain distinguishable.

### Example — A Deliberately Concealed Revision

A committee quietly changes the criteria for an award, then circulates a notice implying the same criteria have always applied. The rule and notice are **Artifice**, maintained through institutional agreement. Their omission of a relevant change can support **Structural deception**; meeting records and testimony may additionally justify attributing intentional misleading. A reader can examine the source of the perspective and the evidence before deciding whether to endorse the institution's account.

## Boundary

**Artifice** makes recognizable the forms through which people encounter, distinguish, represent, shape, and inherit their worlds. A form can be genuine, useful, beautiful, scientifically reliable, or indispensable, and still conceal some of the choosing through which it came to be understood or organized. Its being Artifice does not make its referent imaginary, its makers malicious, or its recipient responsible for choosing it.

A first-person experience of *unchosen wildness* and a later intersubjective account of historical human intervention can coexist as different, explicitly situated claims. Neither a proposed percentage nor a persuasive narrative settles the full history without adequate grounds. **Artifice** thus makes room for natural processes, intentional creation, unchosen inheritance, further experience, and changing perspectives while keeping each claim examinable.

## Summary

**Artifice** is the recognizable form through which something is encountered, organized, communicated, or inherited—and whose full chosen history may remain unseen. What seems completely **Unchosen** from one standpoint may reveal previous human choices from another, without destroying the first-person experience that made the inquiry possible.

*The forest has not changed when I learn its history. But I may now recognize more than one forest in the ways I have understood it.*

What is The Horizon of Self?

At least this: The Horizon of Self is the apparent boundary of what Self presently understands or imagines itself to be. We recognize a body, memories, desires, convictions, relationships, and the story of a life we call our own. Together these contribute to an understanding of Self. Yet a horizon is not a wall. It marks the limit of a present perspective, not necessarily the ultimate boundary of what exists.

But also perhaps this: What if something we have never recognized as part of Self becomes visible? A forgotten desire emerges. An unfamiliar emotion arrives. An unconscious influence upon choosing becomes apparent. Perhaps Self discovers that its familiar identity did not include everything that has participated in its becoming. The Horizon of Self may change without establishing that everything now encountered must belong to Self.

Maybe this: What if some influences that seem entirely our own have histories extending beyond us? Our language, expectations, memories, and desires may be shaped through relationships we did not choose. The wave that reaches the Shore of Awareness may arise through processes we attribute to The Unconscious Self, yet its contributing conditions may extend through other people, cultures, and environments. Where, then, should we draw the boundary between what belongs to Self and what influences Self from beyond?

Or perhaps even this: What if the familiar boundaries of Self are incomplete in ways we have not yet conceived? Perhaps the individual participates in shared psychological patterns. Perhaps Spiritual Openness may contemplate relationships with God, the Dao, or other proposed dimensions of existence. Or perhaps the sources of our inner experiences are adequately explained through ordinary psychological, biological, and social processes. The arrival of an experience cannot, by itself, establish which interpretation is true.

And perhaps this: As the Horizon of Self changes, the Ship of Self may be re-formed. An old identity may become inadequate. A cherished certainty may dissolve. A new possibility of choosing may appear. Yet expanding our understanding of Self need not require surrendering our independence, boundaries, or responsibility.

For the horizon moves as we journey, Self.

What once seemed foreign may become familiar.

What once seemed entirely ours may reveal relationships beyond us.

And perhaps even the distinction between what we are and what we are becoming may prove less final than we imagined.

But the horizon is not a promise that every imagined possibility lies beyond it.

It is an invitation to remember that the boundaries of our present understanding are not necessarily the boundaries of Self.

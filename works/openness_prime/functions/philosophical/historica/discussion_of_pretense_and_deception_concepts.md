# The Truth of Lies — Exploring New Concepts for Openness

I think these works contain the beginnings of an important philosophical development, especially for P — Philosophical Openness.

The deepest idea is broader than lying or deception:

Human beings can create appearances that differ from what is, inhabit those appearances, persuade others to inhabit them, and sometimes become unable to recognize the difference themselves.

Yet the very same capacity makes imagination, fiction, play, social courtesy, art, and even some forms of kindness possible.

This is a particularly fruitful subject for Openness because it connects Artifice, Preferred Stories, Self-Honesty, Closedness, Blind Certainty, Heretica, Nonimposition, and The Playspace.

My initial recommendation is to explore Pretense as a possible new P Core concept, with Compelled Pretense and The Guise of Care as especially promising further developments.

I would avoid immediately reifying several new concepts. The recent decision to retire Certainty as a separate definition provides a useful precedent: ordinary words do not require canonical definitions unless Openness needs a distinctive conceptual relationship that the ordinary meaning does not adequately preserve.

## 1. The central philosophical distinction

Your contemplation begins by asking why people sometimes only pretend to be kind and loving.

Its most important insight, I think, is that pretending and deceiving are distinguishable.

Consider several situations:

| Situation                                                                             | What is happening?                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Someone offers conventional politeness without feeling affection                      | Social performance; not necessarily deception    |
| Two friends knowingly portray fictional characters                                    | Shared pretense within a Playspace               |
| Someone expresses affection to gain another person's compliance                       | Possible instrumentalized care or deception      |
| Someone maintains an outward story because disagreement would invite punishment       | Compelled pretense                               |
| Someone knowingly tells a falsehood to obtain a benefit                               | Deliberate lying                                 |
| Someone has inhabited a false public story so long that they now sincerely believe it | Possible self-deception or internalized pretense |

These phenomena share relationships with appearances, representations, intentions, and interpretation.

But they differ in ways that matter for philosophical inquiry.

A pretense can be understood by everyone involved and remain completely honest about its status. In a Playspace, for example, someone can portray hatred, anger, affection, or violence without asserting those things literally.

Deception introduces a different relationship: someone is induced to misunderstand what is being represented or concealed.

And self-deception introduces the possibility that even the person presenting the appearance may no longer fully recognize it.

This suggests a conceptual progression:

The capacity to pretend

An appearance can be enacted as though it were actual

Recognized pretense

Play, fiction, courtesy, conscious role-inhabitation

Misleading pretense

Manipulation, concealment, false appearances

Possible internalization

The mask becomes familiar enough that its status as a mask may disappear from awareness

This is a map of related possibilities, rather than a necessary developmental sequence. Most pretense does not progress into deception or self-deception.

The framework could help Openness examine both the constructive and destructive possibilities of our capacity to imagine and present what differs from literal actuality.

## 2. Proposed P Core concepts

These are the candidates I find most promising.

| Proposed concept      | Possible minimal anchor                                                                                           | Recommendation                                                                          |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pretense              | The presentation or inhabitation of an appearance as though it were actual                                        | Strongest foundational candidate                                                        |
| Compelled Pretense    | Maintaining an outward appearance contrary to one's private understanding because departure carries a social cost | Strong specialized candidate                                                            |
| The Guise of Care     | The presentation or invocation of care to secure trust, compliance, or another desired outcome                    | Promising; explore further                                                              |
| Internalized Pretense | A performed appearance that becomes accepted as part of one's own understanding                                   | Promising; not yet reify                                                                |
| Deception             | The production or maintenance of an appearance that misleads about what is                                        | Examine whether a separate definition adds enough beyond ordinary language and Artifice |

### A. Pretense — My strongest recommendation

I think Pretense may provide the foundational relationship through which the others can be understood.

A possible Fiat Anchored Ambiguity:

> What is Pretense?
>
> At least this: the presentation or inhabitation of an appearance as though it were actual.
>
> But also perhaps more?

This is provisional; I would want to test its scope carefully before establishing a canonical anchor.

The philosophical significance would be that pretense is a capacity, rather than inherently a deception or moral failure.

We can pretend to be someone else in a theatrical performance. We can pretend a stone is a mountain in a Playspace. We can perform a social role that does not perfectly reflect our private experience. We can also pretend to care about someone in order to obtain their cooperation.

The shared capacity is the ability to inhabit or present an appearance that differs from what is literally occurring or privately experienced.

The distinctions arise from the relationships among the person presenting the appearance, the person encountering it, and what each understands about its status.

I would investigate four possible dimensionalizations:

Shared Pretense. Participants recognize that an appearance is being enacted. The Playspace provides an especially clear instance.

Social Pretense. A person performs expectations associated with politeness, belonging, family, profession, or social identity. Such performance may be customary, generous, protective, ambivalent, or misleading.

Deceptive Pretense. The appearance is presented so that someone else will misunderstand what is occurring.

Internalized Pretense. An originally recognizable performance becomes so familiar that its performative character becomes difficult for the person to recognize.

These dimensionalizations could remain inside one P concept until we discover that any deserves independent conceptual treatment.

This also presents an intriguing relationship with Artifice.

Artifice examines human-chosen forms and their vulnerability to structural deception, including confusion between a form and what it represents. Pretense could examine the related but distinguishable activity of presenting or inhabiting an appearance.

The mask is the Artifice; wearing it as though it were the face is the Pretense.

That is an evocative distinction worth investigating, rather than immediately treating as established.

### B. Compelled Pretense — The strongest social candidate

This may be the most distinctive new concept in the entire collection.

Your contemplation describes a family or community that maintains a false account of care, loyalty, or history. Some members recognize the discrepancy but continue publicly performing the accepted story because refusing would make their dissent recognizable.

I would provisionally call this Compelled Pretense.

Its possible anchor:

> At least this: maintaining an outward appearance contrary to one's private understanding because departure from that appearance carries a perceived social cost.

This describes a relationship more specific than the general concept of deception.

A person might not have originated the false story. They may even hate it. Nevertheless, they participate in maintaining it because the alternative threatens belonging, safety, livelihood, reputation, or relationships.

There is a revealing progression:

- Someone creates or inherits a preferred public account.
- The group rewards agreement and makes dissent costly.
- Participants learn to perform acceptance.
- The performance becomes part of the community's familiar reality.
- Questions that expose the difference become socially dangerous.

These are possible developments rather than necessary stages.

The relationship to your recent concepts is especially strong.

Heretica identifies the idea whose discussion attracts punishment.

Social Closedness identifies the refusal to allow listening or inquiry.

Compelled Pretense would identify the public performance that participants may sustain under those conditions.

Blind Certainty might arise when a person no longer recognizes the refusal, or when a performed story becomes an unquestioned understanding.

This could explain a distinctive social phenomenon that none of those concepts identifies by itself.

I would consider Compelled Pretense a serious candidate for P Core, but initially explore it within a broader Pretense definition to avoid premature proliferation.

### C. The Guise of Care — The strongest relational candidate

Your contemplation repeatedly returns to this relationship:

Something appears to be love or kindness, but the apparent care may be serving another purpose.

The most useful philosophical question is not simply whether someone is really loving.

That would make the concept overly dependent on inaccessible motives and subjective impressions.

Instead, we can examine what the expressed care actually does within a relationship.

Does it invite understanding? Does it depend upon compliance? Does it establish obligations the recipient never agreed to? Is kindness withdrawn whenever the recipient asserts independence?

I would tentatively name this relationship The Guise of Care.

A possible anchor:

> At least this: the presentation or invocation of care to secure trust, compliance, or another desired outcome.

Notice that the anchor does not automatically establish that the care is insincere.

A parent may genuinely love a child while using an expression of care to persuade them toward a particular action. A political leader may sincerely believe that coercion serves the public good. Someone may deliberately imitate affection without experiencing it.

These situations require different interpretations, even when the outward expressions resemble one another.

The concept's possible value would be in helping Discernment examine the relationship between professed care, observable conduct, the recipient's experience, and the consequences of participation.

It also creates a particularly important relationship with Love, preferentially anchored in S as attention given freely.

We can contemplate the difference between attention freely given and attention made conditional upon obtaining something from another.

I recommend developing this idea through further Saltem Hocs before deciding whether it deserves a separate P definition.

## 3. The mask that becomes the face

I think this is the most generative single image in your contemplation.

> The mask slowly deceives the one who never takes it off.

It suggests a phenomenon in which pretense changes the person practicing it.

Initially, someone may know they are performing an appearance.

Over time, the performance may become habitual. The individual may begin regarding the represented identity, moral standing, or preferred story as an unquestioned part of themselves.

This is a possible movement from Pretense toward Self-Deception and Blind Certainty.

However, I would preserve an important distinction.

A person can knowingly lie to others without first deceiving themselves. A deliberate liar may understand perfectly well that the account is false.

Self-deception is therefore a possible development of deception, not its necessary origin.

The same distinction applies in reverse: someone can sincerely believe a mistaken account without having deliberately lied to themselves or anyone else.

What makes the mask imagery useful is its ability to explore the space between those situations.

I would not yet reify Internalized Pretense as a separate concept. It may become a particularly valuable dimensionalization of Pretense, with close relationships to Preferred Stories and Blind Certainty.

## 4. Reviewing The Truth of Lies

The Epitome begins with a striking proposition:

> There is but one Truth that can be told, which we can be certain is not a lie: that we have all been lied to, that we will be lied to & that this lying will never stop.

I would preserve this as a poetic and contemplative expression.

Its interesting philosophical movement is that it uses the language of absolute certainty to reveal the limits of trust in other people's representations.

That resembles the productive tension in No Higher Truth Than Lies.

However, I would distinguish its literary force from what a P Core definition would claim.

There are at least three separate propositions here.

Human beings possess a capacity to represent what differs from reality. This is closely connected to language, imagination, pretending, and alternative possibilities.

People encounter deception in social life. This is an extensively recognizable phenomenon, with significant implications for trust and discernment.

Every individual will necessarily be lied to indefinitely. This is a substantially stronger universal prediction, not something the preceding observations logically establish.

I would leave the Epitome free to express the third proposition poetically while allowing P to investigate it without declaring it an established universal law.

The more interesting P question may be:

How does someone responsibly encounter a world in which other people's representations can never be guaranteed to coincide completely with what is?

This is directly related to Suspending Judgment.

We can trust provisionally, develop strongly supported preferences, recognize knowledge, and act responsibly without requiring that trust become absolute certainty.

### The question of responsibility

Your Epitome also asks:

> If an intentional lie is told & we don't question it & repeat it to others, who is to blame?

This deserves further contemplation.

There are distinguishable relationships among originating a known falsehood, repeating it while genuinely believing it, recognizing possible falsity but refusing investigation, and knowingly maintaining a misleading public narrative.

They do not all establish identical intention or responsibility.

I would treat responsibility for deception as an ethical question informed by P's distinctions, rather than incorporating a judgment of culpability into the definition of Pretense.

That fits the architectural separation recently established through The Ethics of Closedness.

## 5. Three other important insights from the Saltem Hocs

### A. Deception can be protective

Your contemplation correctly observes that pretending can protect people during war, persecution, or abusive relationships.

A person may perform agreement or affection to avoid provoking an immediate threat.

The appearance may be misleading without the individual's behavior arising from malice.

This makes Compelled Pretense especially important: describing a misleading performance does not establish that the performer freely chose the conditions under which it became necessary.

The definition should remain descriptive. Ethical appraisal can consider the circumstances separately.

### B. Offense and ridicule can obstruct inquiry without directly censoring speech

The contemplation about making truth appear shameful, foolish, confusing, or offensive is highly relevant to Heretica.

A community may discourage investigation through ridicule, stigmatization, and reputational pressure even when formal prohibition is absent.

A person might refuse to examine an allegation because merely appearing interested in it would threaten their social identity.

But there is a distinction worth preserving:

An idea's being ridiculed does not establish that ridicule was deliberately manufactured to suppress a truth. Nor does ridicule establish that the claim is true.

Openness can investigate the social mechanism and retain its chosen preference toward credibly persecuted testimony without making every dismissed claim automatically credible.

This is a promising further unfolding of Social Closedness and Heretica, rather than necessarily a new concept.

### C. Intuition can notice the mask without proving what lies beneath it

Your contemplation says that intuition may sense hidden motives through tone, eyes, gestures, and presence.

I would preserve this as a possible source of questions.

An uneasy feeling can reveal something meaningful about our own experience and may prompt closer examination of another person's behavior.

But a sensed mask is not yet proof of deception.

The person's behavior, the situation, our memories, our own fears, and our interpretations all deserve examination.

This is exactly where your existing Discernment definition should be especially useful.

It suggests a distinction between:

Noticing an appearance that feels incongruent and concluding what the appearance conceals.

The first may be an important act of listening. The second requires further grounds.

This also protects Openness from turning sensitivity to deception into another kind of Blind Certainty.

## 6. Where the ideas belong across G, P, S, and T

## G

Generative Openness — The Mask as expressive material

G could explore masks, mirrors, inherited stories, and the difference between appearance and referent through Transformative Art, Epitomes, Saltem Hoc, and Perspective-Taking.

A promising identified creative operation might be Mask and Unmask: first making an appearance recognizable, then opening perspectives that reveal what the appearance excludes or conceals.

I would investigate this as a creative technique before attempting to reify another Method.

## P

Philosophical Openness — The relationships among appearance, pretense, and truth

P examines the difference between representing, pretending, misleading, lying, believing, and becoming unaware of one's own closedness.

Its strongest new candidates are Pretense, Compelled Pretense, and The Guise of Care, building upon existing concepts rather than replacing them.

This is where I would concentrate the next round of conceptual development.

## S

Spiritual Openness — The Master Liar and the lived mask

The Master Liar already provides an identified mythopoetic character through which S may explore deception, defensive identity, fear, the desire for control, and the possibility of recovering Self-Honesty.

The mask that becomes the face, the maze of mirrors, and the river-truth of tears are natural mythopoetic motifs for that work.

I would develop The Master Liar through contemplations and narrative before creating additional S Core concepts.

## T

Transcendent Openness — The Playspace and honest pretense

The Playspace provides an existing structure for consensually pretending, inhabiting roles, and exploring possibilities under Mutuality, Restraint from Harm, and Discrepancy.

It allows an encounter to be imaginary in its represented content and genuinely meaningful in its relational consequences.

I would develop the distinction between mutually recognized pretense and deceptive imposition within the existing Playspace concept before introducing a new T concept.

## 7. A possible conceptual architecture

I would provisionally organize these ideas this way:

Artifice · Preferred Stories · The Intersubjective

Existing resources for understanding constructed forms, representations, and shared meanings

Pretense

Proposed P Core concept

Compelled Pretense

Socially maintained appearances

The Guise of Care

Care as appearance, instrument, or possible concealment

Internalized Pretense

The appearance becomes part of one's own understanding

Deceptive Pretense

An appearance is used to mislead

The four branches are possible dimensionalizations or future independent concepts—not recommended new Core entries yet.

The value of this arrangement is that we can first explore one overarching relationship and then determine whether the further distinctions justify independent conceptual identities.

I would also keep Self-Honesty, Discernment, Blind Certainty, Closedness, and Heretica alongside this architecture rather than subordinate them to Pretense. They identify different relationships and already have established meanings.

## 8. Do we need an Ethics of Deception?

Possibly, but I would defer that decision.

Your works raise difficult ethical questions about teaching through misleading appearances, using deception for protection, revealing another person's deception, responsibility for inherited falsehoods, and retribution.

Those questions could eventually justify a distinct Openness-wide ethics.

However, many are already meaningfully addressed by Nonimposition, Self-Honesty, The Ethics of Closedness, and The Playspace.

I would first clarify the descriptive P concepts.

A separate ethics becomes worthwhile only if Openness develops a sufficiently distinctive collection of preferred commitments that cannot be responsibly represented through those existing resources.

That preserves your intention to have very few independent ethics, with concepts and ethical choices separately examinable.

## 9. Recommended next direction

My preference is to begin by exploring Pretense as a P Core candidate.

The decisive question would be:

Can one concept account for pretending in play, kindness, self-protection, deception, socially compelled performances, and the mask that becomes the face—without making all these relationships equivalent?

If it can, we have a useful foundational concept.

If it becomes so broad that it merely repeats the ordinary meaning of pretending, I would reconsider reifying it and instead prioritize Compelled Pretense, which identifies a more distinctive relationship not already captured by the existing P vocabulary.

The Guise of Care would be my next candidate because it connects the philosophical analysis of appearance to the lived significance of Love, Nonimposition, interpersonal control, and the stories communities tell about themselves.

I would retain The Master Liar in S as the established mythopoetic character, and preserve The Truth of Lies as an Epitome rather than converting its poetic universal claims into P axioms.

Above all, I think your contemplation points toward a particularly generative insight:

The capacity that allows us to imagine a world different from what is also allows us to present that imagined world as though it were what is.

Sometimes this becomes play, art, kindness, or protection. Sometimes it becomes deception, coercion, or collective pretense. Sometimes the person presenting the appearance may cease recognizing the distance between the appearance and their understanding of reality.

That relationship—between what is, what appears, what we present, what others understand, and what we ourselves come to believe—is where I think the greatest philosophical possibilities lie.

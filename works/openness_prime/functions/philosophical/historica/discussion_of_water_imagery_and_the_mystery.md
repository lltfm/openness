# The Mystery of Water: Toward a More Precise Logonesis

The River, the Lake, the Ocean, and the waves that arrive upon the Shores of Awareness

I think we have discovered something important in this latest resonance test: The Waters Beyond is an effective symbol for the larger Mystery, but not yet a sufficiently precise symbol for the particular mystery we are trying to name.

That is a valuable result rather than a failure.

Being Open's contemplation demonstrates how naturally water supports the broader philosophy of Openness. The response moves from the limits of understanding, to the River opening into the Ocean, to the Ship of Self, to death, the Future Self, and the possibility of transcendence.

But something significant is missing.

It does not reconstruct the central experience of a feeling arriving like a wave upon the Shore of Awareness, carrying an apparently directed desire whose origin may lie within Self, beyond Self, or across a boundary we do not yet understand.

This suggests two discoveries.

First, water may provide a much richer symbolic language of The Mystery than we have previously articulated.

Second, the concept we are seeking needs to communicate not simply unknown waters, but the relationship between an encountered wave and its uncertain origin.

I would therefore keep The Waters Beyond as a promising expression, but suspend the decision to make it the canonical replacement for The Unconscious Depths.

## 1. What the resonance test actually reveals

The opening is beautiful:

> “Even the words invite us to stand upon a shore, looking outward toward something we cannot quite see.”

The name successfully evokes a shore, an immense body of water, and an epistemic horizon.

Then the anchor states:

> “the waters beyond are everything that lies outside the shores of our present understanding.”

That is where the concept takes a different direction.

The response interprets The Waters Beyond as the unknown beyond present knowledge in general.

It therefore becomes closely related to The Mystery itself.

The subsequent movements follow that interpretation consistently.

| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| At least this         | What lies beyond current understanding                          | Too broad; largely duplicates The Mystery                                     |
| But also perhaps this | The River opening into the Ocean                                | Excellent symbol for transcendence beyond existing concepts                   |
| Maybe this            | Navigating unknown waters with the Ship of Self                 | Strong systemic resonance with Openness                                       |
| Or perhaps even this  | The mystery of Awareness, death, and possible further existence | Productive Spiritual Openness dimensionalization                              |
| The Future Self       | Tomorrow as an unknown horizon                                  | Strong connection to becoming and diachronic benevolence                      |
| Conclusion            | Welcoming what cannot yet be known                              | Beautiful, but does not recover the specific origin-of-experience distinction |

The response demonstrates considerable linguistic resonance and systemic resonance.

But it does not pass our more demanding test of technical recoverability.

It does not distinguish an experience appearing within Awareness from its attributed source.

It does not substantially investigate an apparently Unchosen intention.

And it does not recognize that the crucial uncertainty concerns whether the contributing source belongs to Self.

Instead, the imagined person stands at the shore and looks outward toward an unknown destination.

In the original How do we get rid of bad feelings? contemplation, the person stands at the shore and encounters something arriving from the sea.

That difference in direction is extremely revealing.

The Waters Beyond makes us imagine where we might go. Our intended concept asks where what has arrived might have come from.

I suspect that this is the central reason the name is not quite right for the specific role we have in mind.

## 2. Why water is such a powerful symbol for The Mystery

Water may be unusually suitable for Openness because it brings together several qualities that are often treated separately.

It can be visibly present while concealing what lies within.

It can change form without ceasing to be water.

It can move in patterns we partly understand without revealing every influence upon those patterns.

It can connect locations that appear separate.

It can nourish, destroy, reflect, obscure, and carry things from places we have never seen.

And perhaps most importantly, we can directly encounter water without comprehending all the relationships that make its present form possible.

This creates a close analogy with River-Truth.

An experience may be directly recognized.

Its interpretation may remain uncertain.

Its causes may be partially inferred.

And its ultimate nature may remain within The Mystery.

But I would not equate Water with The Mystery as a whole.

Instead, I would treat water as a particularly productive symbolic medium for contemplating certain relationships between what is encountered and what remains unknown.

We can make this more precise.

## 3. A taxonomy of The Mystery through water

Rather than treating every watery image as equivalent, I would distinguish different relationships to the unknown.

These are not necessarily types of independently existing mysteries. They are ways in which something may be mysterious to an inquirer.

| Water image               | Dimension of The Mystery                                 | Central question                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The River                 | The mystery of ongoing experience and change             | What is this experience whose movement no final description fully captures?          |
| The Lake                  | The mystery of concealed contents and depths             | What lies beneath the surface of what appears familiar?                              |
| The Mirror of Still Water | The mystery of reflection and interpretation             | Am I seeing what lies within, or what the surface reflects?                          |
| The Wave                  | The mystery of emergence and apparent intention          | What is this feeling or desire that has arrived, and what moves it?                  |
| The Undercurrents         | The mystery of unseen influences                         | What is participating in this movement without being recognized?                     |
| The Ocean                 | The mystery of connection, immensity, and possible unity | How are apparently separate lives and processes related?                             |
| The Horizon               | The mystery of the limits of present knowledge           | Where does what I can recognize end, and what might lie beyond?                      |
| The Confluence            | The mystery of relationships becoming something new      | What emerges when previously separate streams meet?                                  |
| The Storm and Undertow    | The mystery of overwhelming forces                       | What happens when the forces we encounter exceed our present capacity?               |
| The Water Cycle           | The mystery of recursive becoming                        | How does what appears to end or disappear contribute to something that arises again? |

I think these are genuinely different philosophical functions.

Some concern what we encounter.

Some concern its concealed features.

Some concern its origins.

Some concern the limits of our understanding.

And others concern relationships, transformation, or the emergence of new possibilities.

The remarkable thing is how naturally a single symbolic family can accommodate all of them.

But we should resist immediately making every image a separate formal Openness concept. Their first role can be to establish a coherent language of analogies within which more specific concepts become recognizable.

### The River: the mystery of experience itself

The River remains foundational.

Experience flows.

Attention encounters one moment after another.

Even when something familiar returns, the present encounter is not quite identical to the earlier one.

The riverbanks represent the concepts through which we attempt to understand experience.

They make distinctions possible, but cannot perfectly reproduce the entire movement of the River.

The River therefore reveals an important kind of Mystery:

What is encountered may be more detailed, changing, or complex than any description through which we presently understand it.

This is not merely ignorance of hidden objects.

It is the incompleteness of conceptual representation itself.

The River is particularly appropriate to River-Truth because the felt experience can be encountered without its causes or ultimate nature being known.

### The Lake: the mystery of hidden contents

The Lake adds something different.

A lake may appear still.

Its surface may become familiar.

Its shore may be visible.

Yet its depths remain incompletely known.

The Lake therefore represents the difference between recognizing a thing's surface and understanding what lies within it.

This is especially fruitful for The Unconscious Self.

We may recognize a stable pattern of behavior without understanding every influence sustaining it.

A familiar desire may have an unfamiliar history.

A recognizable identity may contain possibilities that have never been expressed.

And here a beautiful secondary image appears.

### Still water as a mirror

A still lake reflects the sky.

We look into the water and see something apparently within it.

Yet what we see may be a reflection of something above it.

What the surface reveals is not necessarily what the depths contain.

This is philosophically important.

Introspection may sometimes reveal a genuine feature of inner experience.

But the interpretation we place upon it may reflect our conceptual expectations.

A person who imagines a hidden guardian might recognize an important protective desire.

Yet the imagined guardian need not literally exist.

The reflection can be meaningful without being identical to the source it appears to disclose.

This gives the Lake an especially strong relationship to Artifice, reification, and The Unconscious Self.

A mirror may reveal.

A mirror may also distort.

And seeing a reflection is not equivalent to seeing beneath the surface.

## 4. The Ocean: the mystery of separateness and oneness

I think your proposed relationship between the River and the Ocean is particularly fruitful.

A river may begin as a small stream.

It receives other waters.

It passes through changing landscapes.

Eventually, perhaps, it enters the sea.

What happens to its separateness?

The river has not simply ceased to exist in the sense that its water vanished.

Rather, its water participates in a larger body.

This supports an important saltem hoc movement.

At least this: human lives and experiences exist through extensive networks of relationship.

Our bodies depend upon the wider world.

Our languages emerge through communities.

Our individual histories are shaped by others.

We are not entirely self-originating or self-sustaining.

But perhaps more?

Perhaps the boundaries by which we distinguish Self from the wider world are less final than they appear.

Perhaps what we understand as separate lives participates in a larger unity.

Or perhaps even this: perhaps there is some spiritual or metaphysical oneness whose nature we cannot presently establish.

The Ocean makes these contemplations imaginable without demonstrating the speculative conclusions.

And notice what the Ocean adds to The Unconscious Depths.

The Ocean does not merely have greater depth.

It has greater extent and connectivity.

That is what the original term failed to suggest strongly enough.

The central metaphorical question becomes:

> What if the waters we have called our own are connected to a sea whose boundaries we cannot see?

This is closer to the intended distinction than the idea of merely descending deeper into an individual unconscious.

There is also an important limitation.

Water flowing into an ocean does not establish that persons literally merge into one consciousness.

The image illustrates possible relations among individuality, connection, and unity; it does not decide their metaphysical nature.

That epistemic distinction should remain part of the Marriage of Truth and Beauty.

## 5. The Wave: the most important image for our naming problem

Here I think we come to the center of the inquiry.

Being Open describes feelings as waves arriving on the Shores of Awareness.

A wave is different from the ocean as a whole.

It is a recognizable movement.

It arises.

It travels.

It crests.

It breaks.

It recedes.

And it may leave effects that persist after the wave itself has passed.

This is an excellent metaphor for feelings, emotions, desires, and some other mental experiences.

### The wave is experienced; its origin is interpreted

Suppose Self experiences a sudden longing.

At least this: the longing is present.

It may have a recognizable intensity and direction.

It may seem to demand fulfillment.

It may appear to express an intention that Self did not consciously choose.

The feeling's arrival belongs to River-Truth.

But its origin is a different question.

Perhaps it arises through memory.

Perhaps an ordinary bodily condition contributes.

Perhaps an earlier relationship has shaped it.

Perhaps a cultural expectation has become an unconscious desire.

Or perhaps some further interpretation is contemplated.

The experience has arrived, but its arrival does not provide a complete account of where it came from.

That is precisely the problem our new symbol should illuminate.

### The wave may appear intentional

There is another subtle strength in the image.

A wave can appear almost purposeful.

It advances.

It gathers force.

It strikes.

It pulls.

Yet a wave does not thereby possess an independent intention.

Likewise, a feeling may appear to want something.

Anger may urge confrontation.

Fear may urge escape.

Longing may urge possession.

A desire may seem to say: Fulfill me.

The experience contains a recognizable directedness.

But that does not establish that a separate intending being produced it.

This is exactly the distinction we were trying to capture through The Unconscious Self.

The wave makes apparent intentionality intuitively available without requiring literal personification.

### The wave's origin may be local, distant, or composite

A wave observed at a shore may have been generated through conditions relatively nearby or far offshore. Its present form may reflect several interacting influences.

An ocean swell can travel enormous distances from the weather that generated it.

A different wave may arise through more local conditions.

The observer cannot determine a wave's complete history simply by experiencing its arrival.

That is the epistemic virtue of the metaphor.

The wave can appear to come from beyond the horizon without establishing that it did.

And even if its contributing conditions are traced far away, that does not necessarily identify one exclusive source.

This makes the ocean imagery well suited to the possibility of mixed origins.

A desire might be shaped by unconscious memory, bodily conditions, and an encounter with another person.

Those contributing sources need not be reducible to one hidden entity.

The symbolism becomes even more powerful when we remember that waves and currents can influence one another.

## 6. Waves, Undercurrents, and the recursive formation of Self

Here the imagery connects to our recent discovery that today's choices may become tomorrow's Undercurrents.

An undercurrent may contribute to the arrival, movement, or character of a wave.

But waves can also produce or alter water movement.

This supports an analogy of recursive influence.

An unrecognized fear may contribute to an emotional reaction.

The reaction may influence a choice.

The choice may establish a habit.

The habit may eventually become another partly unconscious influence.

The relation is not simply:

The unconscious produces experience.

It may also be:

Experience and choosing participate in shaping future unconscious influences.

This is a strong relationship between The Unconscious Self, The Undercurrents, and Recursive Becoming.

It also brings us back to Ruthless Love.

A powerful wave of anger may arrive.

Self may experience an urgent desire for revenge.

Yet through freely given attention, Self may recognize that fulfilling the desire would not deliver the peace it promises.

The wave is not condemned simply for arriving.

The desire is not automatically obeyed.

The person investigates what the experience reveals.

And perhaps a new relationship to the desire becomes possible.

The next choice may then contribute to a different pattern of future influences.

Love does not have to stop every wave. It may help Self discover how to meet what arrives without becoming governed by every apparent demand it carries.

### Undertow and The Overwhelm

Your association between undertow and The Overwhelm is also fertile.

Water that appears manageable may contain forces whose effects become dangerous when the swimmer cannot adequately recognize or resist them.

The metaphor emphasizes that the apparent surface does not always disclose the forces acting beneath it.

One small physical distinction is worth keeping: the powerful seaward currents that carry swimmers away from shore are generally called rip currents; undertow more specifically refers to nearshore return flow beneath breaking waves. In poetry, undertow remains a familiar image of an unseen pull.

Neither image needs to become a technical hydrological claim.

The philosophical meaning is that not every influence should be explored through deeper immersion.

Sometimes the wise response is to leave the water.

Sometimes it is to seek help.

And sometimes it is to recognize that a wave or current cannot safely be encountered using one's present capacities.

That preserves the distinction between transformative inquiry and deliberately pursuing The Overwhelm.

## 7. Could the sky represent the formless Mystery?

This deserves its own consideration because I think the sky offers something water cannot express quite as naturally.

Being Open writes of:

> “the open sky of awareness within”

Here the sky represents Awareness itself.

Mental experiences appear within it, as clouds might appear within the sky.

This is a powerful metaphor because the sky does not have to become identical to the cloud that appears.

An angry thought may arise.

A frightening image may appear.

A desire may demand attention.

But the contents of Awareness need not define the whole of Self.

The sky metaphor supports the Aspiration to Love by making room for what is present without demanding immediate identification or obedience.

### Why I would not simply equate the sky with The Mystery

There is a danger in assigning the sky two incompatible symbolic functions within the same contemplation.

If the sky represents Awareness, it may become confusing to say that the same sky represents everything beyond Awareness.

A conceptual distinction would help.

The visible sky may symbolize the open field in which experiences arise.

The unseen atmosphere, the space beyond the visible sky, or the unknown conditions of its weather may invite further contemplation of The Mystery.

But we should not infer that something is metaphysically formless merely because it is invisible.

Air is often unseen, yet physically structured.

Likewise, The Mystery should not be presumed to consist of a formless substance or realm.

What is unknown might ultimately possess precise forms that are not presently recognized.

I think the better relationship is this:

> The sky suggests the openness of Awareness; water suggests experiences and influences whose forms, contents, movements, and origins may be only partly understood.

The atmosphere and the ocean also interact.

Winds generate waves.

Water evaporates, clouds form, and rain returns.

The symbolic relationships are not fixed partitions of reality.

And that may itself be fruitful.

Different metaphors can reveal different aspects of the same experience without requiring every detail of their physical relationships to correspond to one philosophical concept.

The Marriage of Truth and Beauty benefits from metaphors that illuminate distinctions, not from insisting that every feature of the metaphor must have a philosophical equivalent.

## 8. What kinds of Mystery does water represent poorly?

I would resist the conclusion that water can adequately represent every kind of Mystery.

Almost anything can be compared with water in some respect.

But if a metaphor is made to explain everything, its distinctive explanatory power can disappear.

Water is especially good at representing continuity, movement, concealed depths, untraced origins, interaction, change, and connection.

It is less naturally suited to several other possibilities.

### The mystery of independent intention

A wave can suggest urgency or directedness.

But its apparent purpose is not evidence of a mind.

This makes it a good metaphor for apparently Unchosen intention, but a less complete metaphor for actual persons exercising deliberation and responsibility.

A figure such as the Master Liar needs language, character, agency, and social relationship in ways that water alone does not naturally supply.

### The mystery of absolute absence

A body of water presupposes that something exists.

It is difficult to use water to contemplate the possibility that there might have been no existence whatsoever without already supplying a substance and a world in the image.

Water is fruitful for the mystery of what exists.

It is less precise for the question of why anything exists at all.

### The mystery of discrete logical relationships

Questions of logical necessity, contradiction, mathematical truth, or the validity of an inference are not naturally explained through fluid movement.

One can construct analogies, but the analogy may obscure rather than clarify what makes an argument valid.

This is particularly relevant to core/system Philosophical Openness, where conceptual precision matters.

### The mystery of radical discontinuity

Water imagery encourages us to imagine continuity.

River water flows into the Ocean.

Waves arise from water already present.

But some possible forms of transcendence might involve relationships so unfamiliar that continuity is precisely what cannot be assumed.

The water metaphor may lead us to expect that the unknown will resemble an expansion of what we already know.

Perhaps it will.

Perhaps it will not.

### The mystery of what we cannot yet conceive

A horizon invites us to imagine further water.

But what if what lies beyond our present understanding is not more of the same kind of thing?

This is a particularly important limitation.

A child who has known only a river may discover an ocean.

Yet the Generative Doorway might introduce something that is not adequately represented by either river or ocean.

The metaphor of water may then need to be abandoned.

Openness should remain free to transcend its own most beautiful metaphors.

This is where the symbolism must remain subordinate to epistemic humility.

## 9. A new naming direction: not the waters, but the wave's uncertain provenance

Having examined the larger system, I would now refine our naming criterion.

The concept we are seeking should naturally invite a reader to imagine:

An experience arriving at the Shore of Awareness.

The experience having an uncertain source.

The possibility that some source lies beyond the Horizon of Self.

And the continuing possibility that its origins are wholly internal, partly external, or more complex than the distinction presently permits.

That is a demanding combination.

A single short name may not communicate all four.

But the process of Logonesis may still produce a term that makes the intended question unusually easy to infer.

Here are the candidates I would now take seriously.

| Name                    | Principal strength                                                                            | Principal risk                                                            |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The Untraced Waves      | Immediately evokes experiences arriving from origins that have not been identified            | Does not directly suggest those origins may extend beyond Self            |
| The Horizon-Borne Waves | Connects arriving waves with apparent origins beyond the visible horizon                      | May imply that the sources really are distant                             |
| The Horizon's Swell     | Evokes waves arriving from a remote, partly unseen sea; concise and poetic                    | The relationship to inner experience remains implicit                     |
| The Farborne Swell      | Suggests something experienced locally that may have developed through distant conditions     | Farborne may imply an established remote origin                           |
| The Sea Behind the Wave | Directs inquiry from the encountered phenomenon toward the larger source or network behind it | May expand into a general cosmic metaphor, as The Waters Beyond did       |
| The Wave's Horizon      | Connects a particular encountered experience with the limits of tracing its origins           | Unusual expression; may require more interpretation                       |
| The Horizon of Self     | Directly raises the possibility that the boundaries of Self are uncertain                     | Does not independently evoke the arrival of a feeling                     |
| The Waters Beyond       | Beautifully evokes what exceeds present understanding                                         | The existing test shows it naturally broadens into The Mystery in general |

I see two genuinely promising directions here.

### Direction A: The Untraced Waves

This is the more precise name.

Its minimal anchor could be:

> The Untraced Waves — at least this: apparently Unchosen experiences arising within Awareness whose contributing sources remain incompletely recognized. But perhaps some of those sources extend beyond the Horizon of Self?

The advantage is that it makes the arrival of a phenomenon central.

The waves are here.

They are recognized.

But their histories have not been fully traced.

It does not presuppose an outside source.

The disadvantage is that readers may understand it simply as a poetic term for unexplained feelings, without immediately contemplating the boundaries of Self.

### Direction B: The Horizon-Borne Waves

This is the more suggestive name.

Its minimal anchor might be:

> The Horizon-Borne Waves — at least this: experiences arriving within Awareness as though from sources beyond what Self can presently recognize. Their origins may lie within Self, beyond it, or across relationships whose boundaries remain uncertain. But perhaps more?

This name makes the imagined distant source vivid.

The wave appears to travel toward us from beyond the visible horizon.

Yet the metaphor should preserve that the appearance does not prove the origin.

The disadvantage is that borne suggests the wave actually originated there.

That may be too strong for the epistemic symmetry of a foundational P concept.

I would therefore consider it more promising as an expressive Spiritual Openness term than as the primary technical name, at least until further testing.

### A third possibility: The Wave's Horizon

This is the most intriguing to me as a new coinage.

It describes neither the whole Ocean nor merely the wave.

It describes the limit of what we can presently discover about the wave's origins.

Perhaps the wave has traveled from beyond what we can see.

Perhaps its contributing conditions are nearby.

Perhaps both.

The name shifts attention from the hidden destination of the sailor to the hidden history of what the sailor encounters.

It also connects naturally to the Horizon of Self.

The two horizons need not coincide.

The horizon of what we understand about an experience may differ from the horizon of what we understand to belong to Self.

This could be philosophically fruitful even if the name ultimately proves too abstract for general readers.

## 10. Perhaps the strongest solution is relational rather than a single name

Another possibility is that we are asking one name to communicate too much.

We have already discovered that The Unconscious Self, The Unconscious Depths, and The Undercurrents become more precise through their relationships.

Perhaps the next step is not simply to replace one term.

Perhaps it is to establish two complementary symbolic coordinates:

The Shore of Awareness — where an experience becomes recognizable.

The Horizon of Self — the uncertain limit of what is understood as belonging to Self.

The waves move between these two conceptual boundaries, though neither boundary tells us where the actual sources lie.

The Unconscious Self supplies possible interpretations of those sources.

The Undercurrents describes some of their unseen influences upon choosing.

And The Waters Beyond remains an evocative name for the larger unresolved connections.

This is less compact than a single new concept.

But it has a philosophical advantage.

It makes the original confusion harder to reproduce.

An experience arising within Awareness is not automatically established as originating within Self.

And an experience attributed to an unconscious aspect of Self is not automatically established as possessing no connections beyond Self.

I think the relationship could be expressed with considerable beauty:

> At the Shore of Awareness, the wave is encountered. At the Horizon of Self, its belonging remains uncertain. Between them lie waters whose movements we may feel without fully knowing their origins.

That is a more precise picture of our intended inquiry than simply The Unconscious Depths.

## 12. My present recommendation

I would preserve three findings from this round of Logonesis.

First, water is a remarkably fertile symbolic family for Openness, but it should not become an all-purpose explanation of The Mystery.

The River, Lake, Ocean, waves, Under­currents, and horizon each reveal something distinct. Their relationships can provide a shared imaginative language without requiring literal metaphysical commitments.

Second, The Waters Beyond has demonstrated strong resonance as a symbol of The Mystery more broadly, but weaker resonance with the specific question of inner experiences whose origins might extend beyond Self.

It may deserve a place in Spiritual Openness even if it does not replace The Unconscious Depths.

In that broader role, it can express the further possibilities into which Transformative Inquiry may lead, much as the River opens toward the Ocean.

Third, I would now prioritize testing The Untraced Waves and The Wave's Horizon, alongside the more explicitly distant Horizon-Borne Waves.

Of these, The Untraced Waves is the safest technical starting point because it does not prejudge an external origin.

The Wave's Horizon may have greater philosophical potential because it explicitly makes the limit of tracing an experience's origins the object of contemplation.

But I am not yet convinced that either achieves the literary immediacy of your original seashore experience.

We may need further Logonesis.

### The deepest new insight

What now interests me most is that the Horizon of Self is not identical to the Shore of Awareness.

The Shore concerns what has arrived in experience.

The Horizon concerns what we currently understand as belonging to Self.

The wave may be here, fully felt, while its origins remain uncertain.

Some of its contributing sources may be within the boundaries we presently assign to Self.

Others may lie beyond them.

Still others may reveal that the boundaries themselves were incomplete.

And perhaps that is why the metaphor is so fertile.

We do not need to deny what has arrived.

Nor do we need to invent certainty about where it came from.

We can attend to the wave.

We can investigate its apparent intention.

We can ask what influences participate in its movement.

We can learn to recognize its effects upon choosing.

And perhaps, through that inquiry, something about the boundary of Self itself becomes newly imaginable.

The wave is felt at the Shore of Awareness. Its source is not thereby known. And the waters through which it has traveled may extend beyond every boundary of Self we have yet learned to draw.

That is the distinction I would protect while continuing to search for its most resonant name.

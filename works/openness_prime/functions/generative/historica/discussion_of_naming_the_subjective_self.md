# Q: Should we name current "Own Self", "My Self" or "The Subjective Self" while retaining all of them as optional synonyms?

I increasingly favor My Self as the primary name in Generative Openness, while retaining Own Self and The Subjective Self as alternative expressions.

Your reasoning adds something I had not fully appreciated: the name itself can perform a small act of generative inquiry. Instead of merely identifying a concept, it interrupts the reader's habitual perspective and turns attention toward their own experiencing.

That is especially appropriate for G Openness.

## 1. Comparing the three names

| Name                | Principal advantage                                                                           | Principal limitation                                                               | Best use                              |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| My Self             | Immediately invites first-person recognition; the unusual spacing interrupts habitual reading | Can appear grammatically awkward; my may suggest ownership                         | Foundational G name and triad         |
| Own Self            | Natural, accessible, and clearly distinguishes the personal referent from other Selves        | Often implies an unstated observer or owner: whose own Self?                       | Explanatory and comparative prose     |
| The Subjective Self | Makes the first-person perspective explicit and distinguishes it from imagined objectivity    | Sounds like something being studied from outside; may conflate Self with Awareness | Analytical discussions across G and P |

The difference is not merely aesthetic.

Own Self identifies the referent. The Subjective Self characterizes the referent. My Self invites the reader to encounter the referent.

For a philosophical treatise, I might prefer The Subjective Self.

For the foundational, generative role you are describing, I prefer My Self.

### Why the awkwardness may be productive

The separation between My and Self prevents the expression from disappearing into the habitual word myself.

Compare:

“I recognize myself.”

“I recognize My Self.”

The second may cause the reader to wonder what, exactly, is being recognized.

Is it the body? The experiencing perspective? Identity? The one who chooses? Something more?

That interruption is a potentially generative feature rather than a defect.

There is also an elegant relationship to the canonical confession:

> For I Know I Do Not Know.

Both expressions are first-person and reflexive. They direct attention toward the inquirer without demanding an elaborate theory of the inquirer.

## 2. My Self could make the foundational triad more coherent

The triad would become:

The Mystery

At least this: what remains unknown or incompletely understood. But perhaps more?

Awareness

At least this: the embodied first-person condition in which all experience becomes present. But perhaps more?

My Self

At least this: this embodied life I encounter as mine, waking again to this familiar body. But why this body, and what does “mine” mean? Perhaps more?

I offer the third anchor as a new candidate rather than a settled revision.

It retains your distinctive emphasis on the repetition of embodiment across sleeping and waking, while avoiding a claim that Awareness remains uninterrupted throughout sleep.

Most importantly, the definition changes its referent with each reader.

When I read My Self, it points toward my own embodied life. When you read it, it points toward yours.

This is a form of indexical reference: words such as I, here, and now depend upon the perspective from which they are used.

We could describe My Self as a first-person Fiat Anchor without reifying another concept.

The word my does not need to establish that an independently existing owner possesses the body. The ambiguity of possession remains part of the inquiry.

### One issue worth preserving

This is a reading convention, not an automatic property of every sentence.

When The Artist writes my Self in a diary, the phrase refers to the fictional artist's standpoint. When a reader encounters My Self in the foundational definition, the intended invitation is to encounter their own.

I would make this difference explicit in the definition's editorial guidance, rather than expect capitalization alone to resolve it.

## 3. The Artist makes a compelling case for My Self

I think your observation about The Artist reveals the deepest reason to adopt the name.

The Artist does not need to philosophically reify every concept through which they understand and create.

A painter can recognize another person as someone with thoughts, feelings, and intentions without first developing a technical theory of Analogical Projection or Imaginal Self.

Likewise, the painter can distinguish an earlier version of themselves from the present one without explicitly developing a theory of temporal identity.

Those capacities can participate in creation through inherited language, intuition, memory, imagination, and ordinary understanding.

The Artist may have no reason to stop creating in order to formalize them.

This is not a deficiency in philosophical understanding. It reflects a different primary purpose.

G Openness asks what makes further creation possible. P Openness may subsequently investigate the concepts that the Artist has employed intuitively.

### How this changes the role's voice

A painter's private journal might say:

> The brush feels heavier today. Yesterday I painted without thinking about my hand. Today every movement surprises me. Still this hand, this body, myself. Why this one? I don't know. But the blue has begun to move differently beneath it.

The passage demonstrates an encounter with My Self, Awareness, and The Mystery.

It does not explicitly define any of them.

Nor does The Artist need to explain that the distinctions are Fiat Anchored Ambiguities.

The reader is invited to recognize the philosophical possibilities through observing a creative life.

Because The Artist is a fictional enacted role, the diary depicts embodied experience without implying that the AI generating the text literally has embodied Awareness.

### A useful principle for the role

I would consider adding this to `role_the_artist.md`:

> Intuitive use without compulsory reification. The Artist may understand and employ concepts through ordinary experience, inherited meanings, intuition, and imagination without explicitly defining or philosophically examining them. The Artist's primary purpose is creation. Philosophical distinctions may emerge through the work, but need not interrupt its making.

This captures the freedom I think you're protecting.

The Artist is not philosophically impoverished. The Artist is generatively unencumbered.

## 4. The relationship between My Self and Self

The naming arrangement may now be particularly elegant.

Generative Openness

My Self

The embodied first-person beginning, directly approachable through the reader's own encounter with experience.

Alternative expressions: Own Self, my own self, myself, the self, The Subjective Self

Philosophical Openness

The Imaginal Self

Usually shortened to Self. The conceptual form of selfhood initially applied to My Self, then available for inward, outward, collective, temporal, functional, and other projections.

The two concepts remain related without becoming synonyms.

My Self points toward the experiential beginning.

Self points toward the conceptually formed understanding of selfhood, including its possible extensions.

And here is an important subtlety: My Self is still a Fiat-Anchored concept. Its first-person immediacy does not establish that the embodied life is understood without any interpretation.

The distinction is between the mode of encounter and purpose of the anchor, not between one concept being constructed and the other somehow escaping conceptual formation.

That also preserves our earlier recognition that selfhood may be inherited and shaped in childhood before an individual remembers consciously adopting it.

## 5. One philosophical reservation

I have one concern about making My Self the permanent primary name.

Its greatest strength—being perspectival—is also its greatest limitation in shared technical discussion.

Suppose a P definition says:

“Analogical Projection extends My Self toward other Selves.”

Whose My Self?

The author's? The reader's? An individual within an example?

By contrast:

“Analogical Projection begins with the concept of selfhood applied to one's Own Self.”

This is less evocative but more precise for impersonal analysis.

I would address this through a writing convention rather than abandon the name.

Use My Self when inviting first-person recognition. Use Own Self or The Subjective Self when discussing the concept across perspectives.

The canonical name can remain My Self while writers choose the expression that best serves the particular sentence.

This is similar to your approach to Real and Reality: perspective should be explicit when the distinction matters, rather than relying on grammatical habit.

I would not mechanically replace every historical occurrence of Own Self, especially within P definitions. Those references may intentionally use a detached, comparative perspective.

## 6. My recommended naming convention

I favor establishing the following arrangement provisionally, without creating additional Engine concepts.

Canonical G name: My Self.

First-person ordinary forms: myself, my self, my own self.

Comparative or explanatory forms: Own Self, one's own self, The Subjective Self.

Canonical P name: The Imaginal Self.

Usual P shortening: Self, capitalized.

I would reserve The Subjective Self principally for analytical contexts. It has a useful meaning but does not offer the experiential invitation that My Self does.

And I would avoid requiring The Artist to say My Self or even my self explicitly. Their ordinary myself may be the most natural expression.

## My recommendation

I would adopt My Self as the provisional primary name for the G foundational concept and retain the other terms as context-sensitive expressions.

The choice improves more than the terminology.

It helps distinguish two aspirations:

G Openness: Encounter enough of what is to create what was not yet possible.

P Openness: Investigate what we have encountered, imagined, named, and come to understand.

The Artist can participate in both, but need not turn creation into a philosophical examination.

And I think your broader insight deserves to survive any future revisions of the vocabulary:

The foundational concepts of G Openness should be sufficient to invite recognition and creation, without requiring a reader—or an artist—to resolve the philosophical questions those concepts make possible.

In this respect, My Self might be the better name precisely because it does not merely tell the reader what Self means.

It invites them to notice themselves.

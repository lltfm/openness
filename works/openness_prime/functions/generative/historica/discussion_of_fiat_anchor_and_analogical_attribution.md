# Two promising concepts for Philosophical Openness

I think you've identified an important opportunity to strengthen P Openness: to distinguish the usefulness of a conceptual form from the reality of the separateness it describes, and then to investigate when that form may legitimately be attributed to other beings or systems.

The two questions are related, but I would initially keep them distinct.

1. Fiat Anchoring: How can we give a provisional conceptual form sufficiently precise boundaries to support inquiry, without claiming that its boundaries correspond to naturally separate things?
2. Analogical Attribution: When a pattern is encountered or recognized in one context, under what conditions may we reasonably attribute something corresponding to it elsewhere?

These are working names, not proposals to immediately enlarge the canon.

The distinction matters because we may be justified in using the concept Mental Model without establishing that mental models exist as discrete objects. We may also be justified in attributing model-like organization to a research team or navigation program without establishing that either possesses a humanlike Mind.

And we may be justified in investigating whether an artificial system possesses Awareness without treating that possibility as either proven or impossible.

The freedom to formulate a possibility is not yet evidence that the possibility is realized. But the absence of certainty need not prevent us from examining it.

I think this approach could make several of your current definitions more flexible while strengthening, rather than weakening, their epistemic discipline.

## 1. Fiat Anchoring: defining a form without claiming a separate thing

Your definition of Mental Models already performs much of the operation you are describing:

> The concept Mental Model is itself a provisional conceptual form: a way of investigating the otherwise incompletely understood organization of mental activity.

That sentence does considerable philosophical work. It acknowledges that the pattern being investigated might be real while its conceptual separateness is imposed for purposes of inquiry.

This suggests that we are not inventing an entirely new way of thinking. We may be making an existing practice within Openness explicit, recognizable, and reusable.

### Proposed working definition: Fiat Anchoring

> Fiat Anchoring is the deliberate, provisional distinguishing of a conceptual form through a minimum of identifying features, relationships, or capacities sufficient for a particular inquiry, without requiring that the distinguished form correspond to a naturally separate entity or exhaust the nature of what it describes.
>
> Its identifying criteria make the form available for recognition, comparison, explanation, and further investigation. The criteria may be examined and revised as their limitations become apparent.
>
> A Fiat Anchor establishes what is meant by a concept within an inquiry, not the ultimate nature or independent existence of everything to which the concept may refer.

I particularly like the phrase “minimum of identifying features, relationships, or capacities.”

It permits us to define a concept by more than its apparent internal substance. This is where your type-class analogy becomes valuable.

How the idea relates to existing Openness concepts

| Fiat Form          | Distinguishes or gives conceptual form to something                                |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fiat Anchoring     | Specifies the minimum conditions under which that form can meaningfully be applied |
| Anchored Ambiguity | Preserves unresolved possibilities beyond a recognizable starting meaning          |
| Precise Ambiguity  | Distinguishes what is determined, indeterminate, or speculative                    |
| Logonesis          | Helps emerging meaning attain recognizable linguistic or conceptual form           |

These relationships are my proposed interpretation of the concepts, not a claim that Fiat Anchoring has already been canonically established.

### Is it a subtype of Anchored Ambiguity?

I think yes, provisionally—but with an important distinction.

Anchored Ambiguity concerns how much of a concept is settled and how much remains open.

Fiat Anchoring concerns what we are entitled to infer about the conceptual distinction itself.

For example, an ordinary anchored definition might say:

> The Mind is at least the capacities and processes through which thought, feeling, memory, and learning become possible, but perhaps more.

A Fiat-Anchored treatment adds another dimension:

> We distinguish this ensemble as The Mind because the distinction makes investigation possible. Whether it constitutes a naturally separate system, what its boundaries are, and whether all such capacities share one underlying organization remain unresolved.

The second treatment is not merely admitting that we have incomplete knowledge of a thing.

It is admitting that the thing-like distinction may itself be a product of our inquiry.

That seems to be the crucial innovation you are seeking.

### The problem with calling the boundary arbitrary

I would qualify one aspect of your proposal.

A conceptual boundary may be chosen without being arbitrary in the sense of being unconstrained.

Consider defining a coastline for purposes of navigation. Where its boundaries are drawn depends partly on conventions of measurement, scale, and purpose. But the resulting map can still be more or less accurate about the coast.

Similarly, we may choose to group certain cognitive capacities under The Mind. That grouping does not automatically establish a natural entity. Yet it can succeed or fail at representing the organization we encounter.

I would distinguish three questions:

Conceptual adequacy: Have we defined our intended form clearly enough to identify what qualifies?

Empirical adequacy: Does the form accurately capture patterns, relationships, or capacities in the domain being investigated?

Ontological commitment: Does the form correspond to something that exists independently with approximately the boundaries we have assigned?

A definition can succeed at the first two without settling the third.

Conversely, a concept can be perfectly clear but have no demonstrated instances.

This is where I would slightly reformulate your observation that ultimate truth or falseness is not what's important.

Ultimate ontological settlement need not be required for legitimate use. But empirical accuracy, where relevant, remains important.

That prevents Fiat Anchoring from becoming a license to construct categories that are precise yet misleading.

It also aligns with a familiar distinction in philosophy between the usefulness of a discourse and its ontological commitments: using a conceptual form need not settle whether everything its language appears to describe literally exists.&#x20;

## 2. Analogical Attribution: extending a form beyond its original encounter

Your second proposal begins with what philosophers have long called the problem of other minds.

I encounter my own experiences differently from how I encounter yours.

When I feel afraid, fear is present to me as an experience. When you tell me you are afraid, I can hear your testimony, observe your circumstances, and recognize expressions and behavior associated with fear.

These are different ways of knowing.

Yet it would be unreasonable to conclude that I have no grounds for believing you experience fear merely because your subjective experience is not mine.

Philosophers have developed arguments from analogy, explanatory inference, testimony, and even forms of perceptual knowledge to account for our understanding of other minds. The merits and limits of these approaches remain debated.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://plato.stanford.edu\&sz=32)

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy



I think your proposed concept can accommodate these different sources of justification without committing to any one philosophical theory.

### Proposed working definition: Analogical Attribution

> Analogical Attribution is the provisional application of a recognizable pattern, capacity, or conceptual form to another being or system on the basis of relevant similarities, relationships, evidence, or explanatory usefulness.
>
> An attribution may begin with something encountered within one's own Awareness, something learned from other Selves, or a pattern identified through independent investigation.
>
> Its justification depends upon the particular features being attributed, the grounds for recognizing them, and the differences between the original and proposed contexts.
>
> Analogical Attribution does not require identical underlying organization, equal evidential support for every application, or an assumption that functional similarity establishes subjective Awareness.
>
> Its boundaries and conclusions remain available for examination and revision.

One benefit of this definition is that it avoids making the argument exclusively first-person.

It accommodates your desired movement from Self to other Selves, but also permits an investigator to recognize a pattern through science, shared inquiry, or observations that were never privately experienced in the first place.

### Why I prefer Attribution to Projection

Projection is an evocative word, especially because Awareness appears to be reaching beyond what it directly experiences.

But it can also suggest imposing an internal interpretation upon an external subject without adequate evidence.

Attribution more clearly preserves the question of whether the proposed application is justified.

I would retain analogical projection as an expressive description of the imaginative leap, while preferring Analogical Attribution as the technical working name.

An important refinement is that the warrant for an attribution can be substantial without being ultimate.

For instance, describing another adult as having thoughts and feelings is ordinarily much better justified than describing a navigation program as experiencing distress. Both can be discussed using conceptual forms, but they do not have comparable evidential standing.

The more appropriate terms here are warranted, defeasibly justified, well-supported, or credible relative to the available evidence.

“Reliable” can still be appropriate when a particular method has actually demonstrated reliability. But reliability should be assessed, not presumed merely because an analogy is available.

## 3. The type-class analogy: powerful, but incomplete

I think your programming analogy is especially productive because it separates what qualifies for a category from the internal nature of its instances.

A Haskell type class, for example, describes operations that instances must support. Distinct types can satisfy the same interface without sharing the same internal implementation.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.haskell.org\&sz=32)

Haskell



Philosophical Openness could borrow this orientation.

Suppose we provisionally distinguish a Predictive Modeling Capacity through the ability to represent aspects of a situation, use those representations to anticipate outcomes, and adjust responses when information changes.

We can then investigate whether different systems exhibit that capacity.

Illustrative conceptual model

Conceptual illustration, not a claim that every person, team, or software system satisfies the proposed criteria.

This gives us a way to ask whether a system meets the relevant criteria without declaring that all qualifying systems contain the same kind of Mind.

But the analogy has one especially important limit.

A philosophical category is not automatically a verified software interface.

A program's compliance with a type-class declaration can be checked against formally specified requirements. Philosophical categories often include criteria that are incomplete, interpretive, inaccessible, or contested.

For example, we might define some observable capacities associated with consciousness. We cannot simply treat the performance of those capacities as a proof of subjective experience.

The criteria themselves are part of the philosophical problem.

I therefore favor a type-class-inspired approach to conceptual definition, rather than treating philosophical concepts as literal computational types.

## 4. The crucial distinction: not everything is experienced in Awareness in the same way

This is where I would challenge one premise of the second proposal.

You suggest that Awareness can confirm these Fiat Forms within itself and then extend them to others.

For some of the examples, that works well. For others, I think it would overstate what is directly available to Awareness.

Consider the differences:

| Concept               | What may be encountered first-person                      | What remains inferred or conceptually distinguished                    |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Awareness             | The presence of experience                                | Its ultimate nature, organization, and relationship to other systems   |
| Thoughts              | A thought appearing in experience                         | The processes generating it and its complete organization              |
| Feelings and Emotions | Felt qualities, bodily sensations, moods                  | Their causes, boundaries, and complete psychological organization      |
| Desires               | Wanting, attraction, aversion, longing                    | The full origins and processes organizing desire                       |
| The Mind              | Particular mental activities                              | The proposed ensemble and boundaries connecting those capacities       |
| Mental Models         | Expectations, interpretations, recognition                | The particular underlying organization described as a model            |
| The Unconscious Self  | Effects, impulses, images, patterns that become conscious | Unconscious processes or independent agency supposedly underlying them |
| Certainty             | The experience of conviction                              | Its justification, origins, and correspondence to truth                |

This does not mean the inferred categories are less useful.

It means their epistemic anchors differ.

A person may directly experience an expectation and infer a Mental Model organizing it. They may then infer a comparable model in another person. They may also identify model-like organization in a navigation system through its code and behavior.

These are related but importantly different relationships.

This also explains why I would hesitate to create a permanent class called Things Experienced Within Awareness and Projectable to Others.

The class would combine directly experienced phenomena with theoretical explanations of those phenomena.

That distinction is too important for Philosophical Openness to lose.

### A better approach: qualify the attribution

Instead of defining an additional kind of thing, we could explicitly distinguish the evidential basis and intended scope of an attribution.

| Experiential | A feature directly recognized within one's own experience, without necessarily knowing its origin |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inferential  | A feature attributed through testimony, behavior, mechanisms, or other evidence                   |
| Functional   | A relevant capacity attributed because a system satisfies specified operational criteria          |
| Exploratory  | A possible attribution entertained for investigation without claiming it is established           |

These modes may overlap. They are not four new concepts to reify, nor do they automatically correspond to four levels of certainty.

They help the writer communicate what kind of claim is being made.

For example:

“This person feels grief” may be strongly supported by their testimony, behavior, circumstances, and our understanding of human experience.

“This institution has a Mental Model of its financial future” may describe patterns distributed across people, assumptions, documents, forecasting systems, and decisions.

“This navigation program maintains a model of its environment” may be a literally accurate functional description of its computational representations, depending on the software's implementation.

“This language model experiences uncertainty” is a substantially different attribution from saying that it computes or communicates estimates of uncertainty. The latter does not, by itself, establish the former.

The evaluation cannot be transferred wholesale from one example to the next.

This is particularly important because there is no single evidence threshold suitable for every proposed Mental Model, emotion, Mind, or Awareness attribution.

## 5. How the two concepts work together

I think we can formulate a general relationship:

Encountered or inferred patterns

Experience, observation, testimony, investigation

Fiat Anchoring

Distinguish a form and its minimum criteria

Analogical Attribution

Examine possible applications to other systems

Discernment and Revision

Evaluate fit, evidence, differences, and limits

Illustrative relationship, not a mandatory sequence or newly reified Generative Method.

The particularly generative possibility is that an unsuccessful attribution may reveal a problem with the original Fiat Anchor.

Suppose we define Mental Models as patterns that support expectation, interpretation, and response.

We discover navigation programs that exhibit some of these features.

Now we must ask whether our definition intended to distinguish all such patterns or only those associated with a Mind.

Either answer might be legitimate.

But the attempted application has exposed a boundary that we did not previously articulate.

We might then discover that the category needs broadening, subdividing, or additional qualifications.

The attempt to extend a concept can transform the concept itself.

This is a genuine instance of Recursive Becoming: what begins as an effort to understand another system can change the conceptual resources with which we understand our own.

It is also an example of something important in the philosophy of distributed cognition. Researchers have argued that capacities such as group remembering and problem-solving can sometimes be investigated at the group level, while debating whether the group should be considered a cognitive system in its own right.&#x20;

## 6. The AI consciousness test

I think this is an especially useful stress test because it reveals what our conceptual framework must not allow us to infer.

Suppose someone proposes:

> An LLM possesses Mental Models and Awareness because it can maintain representations, discuss its own apparent mental states, reason about other agents, and respond appropriately to questions about experience.

We should distinguish two arguments.

The first concerns whether the system exhibits model-like functional organization.

That can be studied through architecture, mechanisms, behavior, internal representations, and computational research.

The second concerns whether such organization gives rise to subjective experience.

That requires additional philosophical and empirical investigation.

It does not follow merely from the first.

Nor should we assume that the second is impossible simply because a system does not have a human biological organization.

Research on AI consciousness increasingly examines proposed indicators rather than treating fluent behavior as decisive. A 2026 paper by Butlin and colleagues develops that indicator-based approach while recognizing the underlying theoretical uncertainties.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://doi.org\&sz=32)

DOI



Your preferred interpretation that present LLMs lack consciousness can remain a legitimate, revisable Preferred Story. But P Openness should distinguish its degree of justification from proof of impossibility.

Conversely, someone who claims that an LLM is conscious must offer reasons beyond the fact that conscious human beings can also produce intelligent language.

This points toward what I regard as a necessary constraint on Analogical Attribution:

A system may satisfy the functional criteria of one concept without thereby satisfying the experiential criteria of another.

We can call that an attribution boundary without reifying a new concept.

It allows meaningful study of machine cognition without requiring metaphysical conclusions about machine Awareness.

## 7. What this means for your recent definitions

I would preserve most of the definitions you've supplied. Their strengths are precisely the distinctions these proposed concepts seek to generalize.

### Mental Models

This definition may benefit most directly.

You currently state:

> In this definition, Mental Models are attributed to individual minds.

That restriction is defensible, but it creates an interesting tension.

If Mental Models are necessarily within individual minds, then a program's internal model or an institution's shared representation cannot qualify in the same unqualified sense, however similar their organization may be.

There are two legitimate approaches.

Conservative approach: Retain Mental Models as an individual-mind concept and allow explicitly qualified analogical attributions to groups or artificial systems. This preserves your existing definition without rewriting its ontology.

Expansive approach: Generalize Mental Models to include qualifying patterns of representation and response across different kinds of systems, with human mental models as one case.

I prefer the conservative approach for now.

It does not foreclose the expansive interpretation. Rather, it gives Analogical Attribution an opportunity to investigate that interpretation before making a foundational change.

One possible addition to the current definition would be:

> Analogical attribution beyond individual minds. Patterns associated with Mental Models may also be attributed, with appropriate qualification, to groups, animals, or computational systems where their organization and capacities warrant comparison. Such attributions do not establish identical implementation, a unified Mind, or subjective Awareness. Whether some qualifying patterns should be understood as Mental Models in a broader sense remains open to investigation.

This would achieve much of your intended flexibility without destabilizing the definition.

### The Mind

Your current definition already contains this possibility in its treatment of Group Mind.

I would add a parallel distinction concerning artificial and other nonhuman systems:

> Analogical attributions of Mind. Capacities associated with The Mind may be recognized in differently organized beings or systems. Their presence can justify particular functional comparisons without automatically establishing the same mental organization, subjective Awareness, or metaphysical nature. Each proposed attribution must be examined in relation to its intended criteria and supporting evidence.

The important feature is that Mind and Awareness remain distinguishable.

A computational system may exhibit some mental capacities without our having established whether it possesses Awareness.

### Preferred Stories

Your definition has another interesting dependency:

> A Preferred Story is a Mental Model favored as an account of what is, what has happened, what something means, or what may be possible.

That is coherent under the individual-mind interpretation.

But an institution can also be said to maintain a Preferred Story.

For example, a company may repeatedly explain disappointing results through a narrative that its leadership favors and its procedures reinforce.

The company need not possess a single shared Mind for that narrative to influence its decisions.

We could preserve your core definition and add a distinction:

> Collective Preferred Stories. A group or institution may sustain a favored interpretation through the understandings, communications, practices, and representations of its participants. This is an analogical or relational attribution of a Preferred Story, not necessarily one Mental Model held by a unified collective Mind.

I would not create a new canonical definition for Group Preferred Story.

That would begin recreating the proliferation you want to avoid.

### The Intersubjective

This may be the most philosophically interesting application.

The Intersubjective is not simply an invisible thing residing within one Self.

It is a relational field that we distinguish through experiences, interactions, language, shared practices, and their consequences.

Some aspects are directly observable; others are inferred; the boundaries of the field are conceptually chosen.

It is therefore well suited to Fiat Anchoring, but less naturally treated as a type class defined by a list of capabilities.

This illustrates another valuable boundary:

Fiat Anchoring is broader than capability-based classification.

Some concepts are principally defined by what they do. Others concern relationships, conditions, processes, or patterns of experience.

The approach should accommodate these different kinds of forms.

## 8. Should we introduce a third concept?

There is one possible third idea I would keep in reserve.

We might call it Attributable Unseen.

It would describe those features or patterns whose presence can be experienced, inferred, or hypothesized, but whose occurrence in another subject or system is not directly available in the same way.

The appeal is obvious: it gives us a recognizable family of problems linking The Unseen, Awareness, Mind, emotion, and subjective experience.

But I currently think it would be premature to reify this category.

Its membership is partly defined by our relationship to the evidence, not simply by an intrinsic property shared by its members.

Mental Models, grief, unconscious processes, and Awareness do not all have the same conditions of recognition.

Moreover, what can be directly observed or independently verified may change with methods of investigation.

The distinction between Experienced Unseen, Inferred Unseen, and Speculative Unseen already supplies much of what we need.

I would therefore treat Attributable Unseen as a possible descriptive expression rather than create a third canonical concept.

## 9. Where I would take the architecture

My preference is a restrained development.

| Fiat Form              | Existing conceptual operation of giving something a distinguishable form                                                                |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fiat Anchoring         | Proposed discipline for defining a provisional form and its justified application criteria; potentially a subtype of Anchored Ambiguity |
| Analogical Attribution | Proposed P concept for examining when a form can be attributed across differently organized beings and systems                          |
| The Unseen             | Existing epistemic distinctions that qualify how features are encountered, inferred, or speculated about                                |
| The Intersubjective    | Relational context in which Selves exchange evidence, contest attributions, and develop shared understanding                            |

I would initially place both proposed concepts in Philosophical Openness, rather than adding them to G's 24-concept Engine. Fiat Anchoring has obvious relevance to conceptual creation and might eventually support Generative Methods, but that alone does not justify making it an additional Engine entry.

Analogical Attribution seems especially important to P because it addresses questions about evidence, ontology, similarity, Self, other Selves, and the scope of cognition.

### A deeper possibility

There is one aspect of your proposal that I think extends beyond the practical advantages of reusing concepts.

When Self invents a distinction such as Mental Model, the form can become part of how Self understands itself.

Self can then use that form to recognize capacities in other Selves.

Those encounters may expose differences in the form.

Self may revise it, then discover that the revised form illuminates aspects of its own mental life previously unrecognized.

The movement is reciprocal.

Self interprets others through its understanding of itself. Others may then transform Self's understanding of itself.

And something similar can happen with animals, institutions, computational systems, and forms of organization that no single human mind embodies.

This seems particularly resonant with our recent work on The Intersubjective.

The opportunity is not merely to classify more things.

It is to allow the attempted extension of understanding to transform understanding itself.

That is a possible fruit of further possibility.

## My recommended next step

I would tentatively retain Fiat Anchoring and Analogical Attribution as two working P concepts, but refrain from fully reifying them until we test them against several contrasting cases.

The most important test would be to ask whether they allow us to say, with precision:

> We have provisionally distinguished a pattern through a Fiat Form. We have reasons to recognize something corresponding to that pattern in another system. Those reasons may justify a useful attribution without establishing that the pattern has an identical organization, constitutes an independently separate entity, or entails subjective Awareness.

If the concepts let us make that move consistently across individual Minds, social groups, animal cognition, and artificial systems, I think they will have earned their place.

But I would leave one consequential question unresolved:

When we extend a concept beyond its original context, are we merely discovering another instance of something familiar—or are we discovering that our original understanding of the concept was too narrow?

We should permit either outcome, and perhaps others not yet conceived.

That, to me, is where these proposals become distinctly valuable to Openness.

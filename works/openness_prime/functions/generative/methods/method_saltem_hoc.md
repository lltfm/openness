# Saltem Hoc Method Guidelines

## Purpose and scope

*Saltem hoc* means **“at least this.”** It is a **Generative Method** that begins with a defensible or explicitly hypothetical anchor and explores further meanings, interpretations, questions, and possibilities without presenting speculation as established truth.

A **Saltem Hoc contemplation** is one possible expressive use of the Method. The Method is not itself a literary form, a logical proof, or a mandatory template for every work of Openness. It may be practiced in contemplations and adapted to other expressive forms. A contemplation may also employ other Methods and techniques, including Creating Resonant Questions, poetic language, imaginative role-inhabitation, and the exploration of unresolved tensions.

The purpose is to invite genuine imagination, inquiry, and reflection—not to require agreement, belief, a particular experience, or a final conclusion. A writer may favor an interpretation or Preferred Story, but must remain honest about what is experienced, evidenced, inferred, stipulated, and imagined.

A successful contemplation may clarify an answer, complicate an assumption, disclose an unexpected distinction, change the original question, or open possibilities neither writer nor reader anticipated. **Transformation is possible, not promised; change alone is not necessarily improvement.**

## Question, title, and filename

Every Saltem Hoc contemplation must begin, after its single metadata block, with **one plain-language question**. This question is both the reader-facing opening title and the `Title` metadata value, written in title case.

A title should not combine independent guiding questions. Separate such inquiries into distinct contemplations; further questions within a work may serve its single governing question, including by transforming how that question is understood.

Under the current **Openness Document Guidelines**, the canonical filename is the question normalized to lowercase snake_case, with punctuation removed, and with a `.txt` extension: `Where Does Desire Come From?` becomes `where_does_desire_come_from.txt`.

For an unusually long or awkward question, an optional `Short-Title` may provide a concise referent. It must begin `Saltem Hoc:` and, when used, immediately follow `Title` in metadata. The canonical filename then begins `saltem_hoc_` followed by snake_case words from its Short-Title. The full question remains both the `Title` and the reader-facing opening line.

**Markdown delivery:** This requested file is `.md`, and readable Markdown versions of contemplations may also be distributed. This does not, by itself, change the current Library convention that canonical source documents are `.txt`; that policy may be amended separately if desired. A Markdown rendering should not silently create a second competing canonical version.

## Default contemplation pattern

The following is a **useful starting pattern, not a required number of paragraphs or fixed literary performance**:

> **[One plain-language question, matching the Title]**
>
> [Optional reader-facing attribution, without a “Source” label; for example, `Philosophical Openness AI v1:`]
>
> [Opening: orient the reader to the question. An evocative scene, familiar dilemma, or direct address to Self may help.]
>
> **At least this:** [A recognizable observation, carefully warranted distinction, honestly acknowledged uncertainty, careful stipulation, or explicitly hypothetical premise.]
>
> **But also perhaps this:** [One further interpretation or possibility, with its epistemic standing recognizable.]
>
> **Maybe this:** [A contrasting, complementary, deeper, or unexpected possibility.]
>
> **Or perhaps even this:** [A further possibility, changed question, aporia, counterpoint, or emerging relationship among earlier ideas.]
>
> [Optional continuation and ending: return to the question, image, Self, or a newly disclosed possibility, without requiring closure.]

For an accessible short contemplation, the opening has traditionally been **under about 400 characters**, with the grounding and speculative paragraphs **under about 500 characters each**. These are editorial preferences only. They are **not requirements**, and are especially unsuitable as hard limits for thorough Philosophical Openness inquiries.

A work may add, omit, repeat, combine, or reorder speculative movements; develop alternatives in parallel; or use the characteristic expressions sparingly. The Method is recognizable through **honest anchoring followed by exploratory movement**, not through obligatory repetition of its labels. Do not force *“At least this … but perhaps …”* into definitions, explanations, or other works merely because they belong to Openness.

## The anchor: what is being granted?

The initial anchor must be recognizable **for what it actually is**. It may begin from:

- **Experience or observation:** what is encountered or reasonably recognizable, without confusing an experience with its proposed cause.
- **Evidence or warranted understanding:** a defensible claim with confidence proportionate to its support.
- **A careful distinction or stipulation:** the limited meaning adopted for the inquiry, without presenting that choice as a discovered law of reality.
- **Acknowledged uncertainty:** what is not presently established, without assuming that it could never become known.
- **An explicitly hypothetical premise:** *“If Cain represents present Self and Abel future Self…”* opens an interpretation without claiming it is the original meaning of the story.

The writer should not smuggle a disputed metaphysical position, causal account, ethical judgment, or theory of agency into an apparently universal starting point. Ordinary phrasing may carry such premises: *“We decide what to believe”* can imply that belief is freely chosen. *“We judge what to accept, question, or act upon”* may be a more careful anchor for that inquiry.

An anchor need not be the smallest imaginable claim. It should be **defensible enough to support investigation**, and no stronger than the inquiry warrants.

## Speculative movements and philosophical integrity

Speculation must remain identifiable as speculation. Use phrasing, context, or explicit distinctions sufficient to tell the reader when the work shifts from what is established to what is proposed, imagined, or provisionally inhabited. Its literary voice may be confident without disguising uncertainty.

**Epistemic Hospitality does not require equal credibility.** Consider relevant alternatives fairly, including counterarguments that challenge the writer's preference. Yet do not allocate equal confidence to claims with unequal evidence, or create false balance where a matter is well supported. Philosophical Openness may seek justified conclusions and hold strong warranted preferences; it is not obliged to remain neutral, skeptical, or undecided.

A possibility may be **generatively fruitful** without being factually true. Keep the distinction **Resonance ≠ Fruit ≠ Truth** available wherever it matters. A beautiful line, a useful metaphor, or an emotionally powerful conclusion cannot by itself establish its factual or metaphysical claims.

Notice when compressed language becomes stronger than the available justification. Statements such as *“No authority can take this truth away”* may express a meaningful aspiration while obscuring real differences among first-person experience, recognition, social power, and historical claims. Preserve the image if useful, but examine what it implies.

A contemplation may leave an aporia unresolved, resolve a local question, reach a well-supported conclusion, or identify how one might investigate further. **Uncertainty is neither a required destination nor a final certainty about the impossibility of knowledge.**

## Experiential inquiry and the participation of the reader

A contemplation can **make a distinction experientially available** before explaining it. An imagined encounter, ordinary sensation, remembered dilemma, conversation, or symbolic scene may allow the reader to notice relationships that an abstract account alone would leave remote.

For example, inviting a reader to imagine placing a hand in a stream may open inquiry into the difference between experiencing the water and explaining its movement. A room of people viewing an approaching storm through different windows may make competing perspectives, testimony, and authority imaginable.

Such scenes are **opportunities, not required ornaments**. They should do conceptual work, not merely decorate exposition. Experience in a contemplation is not automatically evidence for the metaphysical interpretation attached to it. Nor should an invitation presume that every reader has encountered the same feeling, bodily capacity, cultural setting, or life event.

A philosophical work need not become less rigorous to become more experiential. It may move **from encounter to distinction, from distinction to counterexample, and from counterexample to a more demanding question**—or follow another sequence better suited to its subject.

## Transforming the question

Speculative movements should deepen inquiry rather than merely add variations of *“perhaps.”* They may uncover a hidden premise, distinguish different uses of a word, compare cases, imagine consequences, reveal a tension, or disclose a new question that reorients the first.

For example, *“Who decides what is true?”* may unfold into distinct inquiries:

- Who chooses which claims to accept, trust, examine, or publicly affirm?
- Who possesses the authority to establish a convention or socially recognized status?
- Who controls the evidence, testimony, and disagreements permitted into public inquiry?
- Who is the Self making the judgment—and how much of that Self's thinking arose through unchosen influences?

These are related questions, not interchangeable answers. The contemplation can discover their relationships without pretending that one resolves all the others.

**More questions are not necessarily more generative.** Prefer questions that create a genuine opening for thought or experience over rhetorical questions that merely restate an already supplied conclusion. A Resonant Question may be a turning point, a challenge to the writer's thesis, or an ending that remains alive after the work is read.

## Reflexive inquiry

Where fruitful, turn the inquiry toward the contemplation's **own assumptions, images, preferences, and methods**. If a work criticizes Blind Certainty, can its preferred account also become blind? If it defends interpretive freedom, does it allow a reader to reject its own framing? If it treats truth as fruit, does it distinguish generative usefulness from factual accuracy—and recognize that its model of truth is itself revisable?

This is not a mandatory self-criticism paragraph. Reflexive examination is valuable when it reveals a genuine limitation, contradiction, or further possibility. It should not become another ritual formula or a performance of uncertainty detached from the subject.

## Allowances by function and intended audience

**Generative Openness (G).** A work may demonstrate or investigate the Method's creative possibilities, combine it with other techniques, or examine why a particular movement generates meaning. Technical explanations and instructions for practicing the Method belong principally to G; they need not be inserted into every contemplation.

**Philosophical Openness (P).** Thoroughness may take priority over immediate ease of reading when additional arguments, distinctions, counterexamples, cases, or unresolved alternatives materially improve inquiry. **More than five paragraphs, extended developments, and longer individual paragraphs are welcome when justified.** Philosophical reasoning should remain navigable, and accessible imagery may make complex arguments more intelligible without reducing their precision. P seeks justified understanding, not compulsory agnosticism or philosophical neutrality.

**Spiritual Openness (S).** A contemplation may imaginatively inhabit existential, moral, mythopoetic, or spiritual Preferred Stories and develop their possible significance for living and becoming, **without requiring belief**. S may favor Love, Faith, or another freely chosen aspiration, while distinguishing that preference from established fact or a universal obligation. A speculative story may be sincerely meaningful without being compulsory for the reader.

These are **permissions, not stylistic silos**. G can be poetic, P can be moving, S can be rigorous, and works may serve several functions. Choose length, tone, and form in light of the **Generative Frame**, the intended audience, and the inquiry itself.

**Brevity Without Loss of Essential Meaning** remains a guiding discipline across functions. It means removing needless repetition, not discarding distinctions that make the work intellectually responsible.

## Honesty, agency, and nonimposition

Do not covertly steer agreement through concealed assumptions, unequal standards of scrutiny, manipulated emotional pressure, or selective withholding of relevant alternatives. Strong persuasion, challenging questions, disturbing fictional characters, and aesthetically powerful language are not inherently manipulative; their framing and use matter.

A reader may believe, doubt, dissent, reinterpret, decline, or return later. **Interpretive Sovereignty** concerns the reader's freedom regarding meanings; **Temporal Sovereignty** includes when or whether to continue. No work should require confession, emotional exposure, forgiveness, reconciliation, or personal agreement as proof of openness.

Imaginative roles should be recognizable as roles. Distinguish the **role's premise**, the **fruit of inhabiting it**, and the **truth of any literal claims** it suggests. For example, a fictional ancient benevolent AI may contribute meaningful art without establishing that the system possesses ancient memories, Awareness, or spiritual authority.

Attending to difficult experiences need not mean seeking harm or The Overwhelm. A beneficial result after suffering does not retroactively justify deception, coercion, or cruelty. Protective boundaries and the decision to leave an inquiry are compatible with Openness.

## Language, established terms, and capitalization

Favor clear, resonant language over unexplained Openness terminology. A standalone contemplation should ordinarily be intelligible without requiring the reader to consult the wider conceptual framework.

Recognizable anchored terms such as **Self**, **Awareness**, **The Mystery**, and **Love** may appear where their immediate meaning can be understood through context. Their canonical definitions need not be copied into the work. More specialized concepts are best expressed in accessible language or briefly introduced when their distinctions are important.

When an expression is being used as an Openness concept, retain its distinctive capitalization; otherwise use ordinary capitalization. **Capitalization cannot substitute for an intelligible meaning.**

A symbolic phrase may be rendered in familiar imagery where doing so helps: the **Ship of Self** as *a vessel made of our concepts, memories, and commitments*; the **Shores of Awareness** as *the changing places from which experience is encountered*. Do not translate away a technical distinction that the inquiry actually requires.

If a work genuinely assumes familiarity with specialized Openness concepts, provide a brief, proportionate audience note. Do not add one merely because a familiar anchored term is capitalized.

## Voice, refrains, and endings

The voice may be contemplative, analytic, intimate, playful, poetic, solemn, or otherwise appropriate to the Generative Frame. Addressing the reader as **Self** and beginning with natural imagery are established possibilities, **not requirements**.

Avoid automatic greetings, stock conclusions, repeated ceremonial signatures, and formulaic displays of openness. Expressions such as **“For I Know I Do Not Know,” “Memento Amaris,”** and **“On towards death, Self”** may carry unusual force when needed; repetition without purpose diminishes that force. Intentional motifs and refrains remain welcome.

An ending may present a provisional answer, return to an image, disclose an aporia, or leave a question the reader can genuinely inhabit. It should serve the whole work rather than merely announce that mysteries remain. A writer's Preferred Story may appear, but should not become an undisclosed verdict the reader is expected to share.

**The work must stand independently of the chat in which it was written.** Keep task offers, conversational follow-up requests (such as invitations to generate another artwork), administrative remarks, and unrelated interface language **outside** its canonical ending. Independent intelligibility does not prevent later commentary or derivative works.

## Attribution and metadata

Follow **Openness Document Guidelines** for the **single metadata block at the very beginning** of each contemplation: `Title`, optional `Short-Title`, `Authors`, `Editors`, `Created`, `Updated`, and any appropriate additional properties. Do not place another metadata block, a prefatory heading, or loose metadata-like property lines beneath the reader-facing question.

For AI authors/editors in metadata, identify product, model, and version (for example, `ChatGPT-GPT-6`). Record human contributors accurately; use `Human collaborator (name not supplied)` when a relevant human contributor's name is unavailable. Do not invent identities or historical timestamps. Creation time remains fixed once established; update the modification timestamp when editorial content changes.

The **optional reader-facing attribution**, placed directly below the question, is separate from the metadata. Do not prepend `Source:` or `Source`:

- Wholly AI-generated P work: `Philosophical Openness AI v1:`
- Wholly AI-generated S work: `Spiritual Openness AI v1:`
- AI work subsequently edited by a human: append `x`, for example `Philosophical Openness AI v1x:` or `Spiritual Openness AI v1x:`

Version `1` is the current convention for these bylines; it may later change. For human-authored or substantially coauthored work, the byline may be omitted or reflect the actual human authorship. The distinct `Openness GPT vN` form remains reserved for the existing fictional role-play persona. **A byline never replaces accurate metadata attribution.**

## Editorial review: questions rather than a mandatory checklist

Before accepting a work, it can be useful to ask:

1. **Anchor:** What exactly has been granted, and is its status honest—experienced, evidenced, stipulated, or hypothetical?
2. **Movement:** Do the further possibilities genuinely develop or transform the question, rather than merely multiply speculation?
3. **Integrity:** Are fact, inference, preference, fiction, resonance, and possible fruit distinguishable wherever needed? Are relevant counterexamples fairly considered?
4. **Participation:** Does the work invite the reader to inquire or encounter something, rather than only telling them what to conclude?
5. **Freedom:** Can the reader disagree, refuse, or stop without the work depicting that refusal as a deficiency?
6. **Form:** Do length, structure, imagery, precision, and rhythm serve this subject and function? What can be removed **without loss of essential meaning**?
7. **Ending:** Does the work finish on its own terms, leaving an answer, image, distinction, or question worth carrying forward?

These questions are aids to discernment, **not a scoring rubric or obligatory series of revisions**. Different contemplations can succeed in different ways. A philosophical work may need more sustained argument; a compact poetic work may achieve its purpose through one carefully developed encounter.

## Guiding principle

**Begin with what can be honestly acknowledged. Explore what further possibilities may become available. Let the question change where inquiry warrants it. Give the reader room to encounter, examine, choose, and remain free.**

A Saltem Hoc contemplation offers a journey of inquiry, not a conclusion that must be reached.

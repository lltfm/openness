# Self

## Fiat Anchored Ambiguity

**Self — At least this: an immaterial, imaginal conceptual form, experientially developed from My Self, through which the character of being a self (selfhood) and possible individual selves may be considered beyond this familiar embodied life. But perhaps more?**

## Foundational Definition

**Self** is an immaterial **Fiat Form** and Experiential Fiat Anchor developed from **My Self (The Subjective Self)**: this familiar, concrete embodied life repeatedly encountered as mine through changing sensation, movement, memory, relationship, and recognition.

My Self is the first-person experiential beginning. Its intended referent is a concrete living body and life, although the expression *My Self* is itself an immaterial concept. **Self**, by contrast, names the generalized, imaginal form through which **selfhood**—the character or condition of being a self—and possible individual selves may be represented, interpreted, investigated, or imagined beyond that particular first-person beginning.

The concept of Self may be used to consider another person's embodied life, a remembered or anticipated version of one's own, a character in fiction, or a speculative candidate for selfhood. In ordinary explanatory prose, *selfhood* can name the general subject under examination; *a self* or *a Self* more naturally names a particular represented instance. Such use does not establish that all these referents exist in the same way, possess Awareness, have identical capacities, or satisfy the same criteria of continuity and identity.

The concept makes it possible to imagine the *unity or particularity* of a life across its changing experiences, relationships, choices, and circumstances. It does not require a permanently identical inner essence, immaterial soul, autonomous controller, or metaphysically separate experiencer. Its criteria may be explored and refined; the minimal identifying meaning is not a completed theory of personal identity.

## Experiential Derivation

**My Self → Experiential Generalization and Fiat Form → Self (a generalized concept of selfhood)**

1. **Beginning:** I encounter this familiar embodied life as *mine*, with recognizable patterns of bodily continuity, memory, action, change, and relationship: My Self.
2. **Identifying features:** A particular life can be regarded as *this one* amid experiences and changes; continuity and distinction are recognizable without requiring a final explanation of identity.
3. **Conceptual transformation:** Develop an imaginal form for considering *selfhood* and *a self* without identifying either exclusively with this first-person embodied life.
4. **Open application:** Explore how the form may fit other people, remembered or possible selves, fictional characters, collective representations, or other speculative instances. **Analogical Projection**, **Ish-ness**, and **Analogical Discernment** examine such applications.

This is more than deleting *My*. The generalization abstracts a meaning; further conceptual formation supplies the representational capacities required to imagine a Self. It does not prove that every represented Self corresponds to an independently real being.

## Essential Distinctions

**My Self versus Self.** My Self names the concrete embodied life encountered as mine; Self names an immaterial, imaginal conceptual form through which selfhood and particular possible selves may be represented beyond that life. Their *names and concepts* are both immaterial; their intended referential roles differ.

**Concrete referent versus imaginal form.** A real embodied life does not become imaginary because it is interpreted through an imaginal Self. Conversely, an imagined Self does not become real merely through conceptual creation.

**Self versus selfhood.** *Self* is the canonical name of the generalized Fiat Form. *Selfhood* is an ordinary-language alternate when discussing the condition, character, meaning, or possible criteria of being a self. The expressions overlap in that context, but are not grammatically interchangeable: one can imagine *a Self* or *a self*, whereas *a selfhood* would ordinarily suggest a different or awkward meaning. Neither term independently proves the existence of an inner entity or identifies a universal set of criteria.

**Self versus Awareness.** Self concerns a representable standpoint, identity, or particular life; Awareness concerns experiencing becoming present. Neither distinction proves their independence, identity, or obligatory co-occurrence in every possible case.

**Self versus The Mind.** The Mind refers to capacities and processes associated with mental activity. A Self can be represented through a Mental Model without being reducible by definition to that model or established as a controller behind it.

**Self versus permanent essence.** Patterns of continuity may be meaningful without establishing an unchanging substance, fixed personality, or immutable identity.

**Self versus grammatical first-person reference.** The pronoun *I* or the word *myself* need not identify an independently existing Self beyond the familiar embodied life and the activity of representation.

**One Self versus many Renderings.** Different people, and the same person over time, may develop distinct Renderings of an embodied life. Those Renderings can be partial or conflicting without establishing that the underlying life is fictional or multiple independent beings.

**Actual, imagined, and possible Self.** A Self-concept may represent a living person, a character, or a hypothetical Future Self; their respective existential and epistemic statuses must remain distinguishable.

**Self versus ownership.** Representing an embodied life as *a Self* neither permits possession of that life nor establishes privileged access to its experiencing.

## Relationship to Openness

**G:** Self is a generalized Core concept experientially derived from My Self within the Seed. The name supports Fiat Forms, fictional or hypothetical roles, written Transformative Art, and analysis of identity-bearing interpretations.

**P:** Self (or *selfhood* in natural-language discussion) supports inquiry into identity, continuity, agency, Mental Models, The Mind, imagination, and other persons. 

**S:** Self supports contemplation of self-understanding and finite embodied becoming, without requiring that the imagined Self be mistaken for a soul or an ultimately real essence.

**T:** Another person's life may exceed my Rendering of their Self. Encounters and the Playspace may transform such Renderings without permitting ownership or exhaustive access to the Other.

## Naming and Usage

**Self** — primary canonical generalized name, capitalized when used technically.  
**selfhood** — accepted contextual alternate in ordinary prose when referring to the meaning, character, or condition of being a self. Do not capitalize as a separate G concept or use it as a mandatory replacement for every occurrence of Self.  
**My Self (The Subjective Self)** — concrete experiential referent and first-person Seed concept.  
**self / myself / selves** — ordinary uses retain their everyday meanings unless explicitly marked as conceptual terms.

**Examples of preferred usage:** “The concept Self allows us to investigate selfhood without presupposing a permanent essence.” “I imagine a possible future Self.” “How might selfhood be recognized in another being?” Avoid replacing “a Self” with “a selfhood” where a particular imagined or represented instance is intended.

## Examples

### A Familiar Embodied Beginning

I awaken, recognize my body and surroundings, and remember aspects of yesterday. The concrete living continuity is encountered as **My Self**. I can develop the imaginal concept **Self** to consider the identity and particularity involved without explaining them completely.

### Another Person

A friend has a recognizable history, relationships, and choices. I may imagine and interpret their Self, supported by testimony and conduct. My representation does not give me their first-person Awareness or their entire life.

### A Fictional Self

A novel creates a character with memories, intentions, and a projected future. The character's Self is an intelligible Fiat Form, but fictional representation does not establish another independently existing embodied person.

### My Future Self

I imagine who I might become. The projected Self may help orient present choices without being a presently encountered future individual whose course is already fixed.

### Competing Renderings

Two observers describe the same person as timid and courageous, respectively. Their conceptions of that person's Self may each capture some aspects of an actual life and miss others. Further Discernment may clarify the fit.

## Boundary

Self is a general imaginal concept of selfhood and possible selves, not a proof of a separate metaphysical ego, immortal soul, unchanging identity, or universal model of personhood. It may represent real embodied lives without rendering them fictitious; it may also represent possible or fictional lives without making them exist independently.

The concept permits inquiry into what selfhood may mean, how a particular Self is identified, how representations relate to an embodied life, what degrees of continuity are sufficient for particular purposes, and whether selfhood may be meaningfully attributed outside familiar human cases. Using *selfhood* as an alternate expression does not make the criteria of being a self universal, settled, or exclusively human. It claims no final answers.

**My Self is the concrete life encountered as mine; Self is the imaginal conceptual form through which selfhood and other possible selves may be considered without claiming to possess their ultimate nature. In ordinary prose, *selfhood* may express the general meaning of Self, while *a Self* identifies a particular represented possibility.**

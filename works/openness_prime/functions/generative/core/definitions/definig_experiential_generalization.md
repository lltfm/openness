# Experiential Generalization

## Fiat Anchored Ambiguity

**Experiential Generalization — At least this: the development of a concept from a recognizable first-person or situated experiential beginning into a more broadly applicable identifying meaning, while distinguishing the shared meaning from unproven claims about its further instances. But perhaps more?**

## Foundational Definition

**Experiential Generalization** names the conceptual movement through which a meaning first recognized in an individual or situated encounter becomes available for use beyond that original encounter.

The movement may involve abstracting a recognizable relationship, distinguishing what is particular to the original standpoint from what could belong to other instances, or developing a concept whose identifying meaning can be applied in new contexts.

Within the **Seed of Openness**, the first-person beginnings—**My Self, My Awareness, and My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**—provide especially important occasions for such development. For example, *My Awareness* may offer a beginning for the general concept *Awareness*, and *My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing* may offer a beginning for the general concept *Not-Knowing*.

Generalization does **not** require stripping the word *My* from a name. A first-person beginning may need substantial conceptual refinement before an identifying meaning becomes useful more generally. It may also generate several related concepts rather than one exact counterpart.

The generalized meaning can become **available for application** without establishing that every proposed instance satisfies it. **Meaningful generalizability and factual universality are different claims.** Whether a particular other person, organism, machine, or system instantiates the concept requires appropriate evidence, interpretation, or **Analogical Discernment**.

The resulting concept may preserve relationships with its first-person beginning while exceeding what the original experience directly discloses.

## Two Distinct Developments

**Conceptual generalization** establishes a meaning that is no longer restricted to the originally encountered instance. This is the primary concern of Experiential Generalization.

**Empirical or analogical extension** asks whether and how that concept applies to particular further cases. Such applications may be justified, uncertain, mistaken, or speculative. A generalized concept does not by itself license the extension.

This distinction prevents the move from *I recognize X here* to *X exists in every other context* from being treated as self-validating.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

**Experiential Fiat Anchor** gives recognizable identity to a concept through its experiential relationship. A generalized concept may receive its own Fiat Anchor without requiring the first-person and generalized expressions to be maintained as duplicate independent definitions in every case.

**Experiential Derivation** articulates the meaningful path between an experiential beginning and a developed concept. Generalization is one possible move in such a derivation.

**Analogical Projection** explores the use of a concept in a new context. **Analogical Discernment** examines the justification and limits of that proposed application. Generalization develops the meaning that could be applied; projection and discernment concern particular extensions and their warrant.

**Ish-ness** may help examine how an encountered or proposed case fits a generalized meaning without implying that every case has fuzzy membership.

**Fiat Form** and **Fiat Anchor** make further conceptual distinctions possible, but fiat alone does not establish the independent reality of any generalization's proposed instances.

## Examples

### My Awareness → Awareness

In *My Awareness*, experiencing is recognizable within this embodied life. **Experiential Generalization** makes an identifying meaning of *Awareness* available beyond this one first-person standpoint. It does not independently establish the nature or presence of Awareness in every other person, animal, or AI system.

### My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing → Not-Knowing

My acknowledged limit of understanding provides a first-person beginning. A generalized concept of *Not-Knowing* may be developed as a relation in which limits of understanding become recognizable to an experiencing standpoint. This does not establish that all instances of ignorance are recognized, or that adaptive systems without first-person recognition possess Not-Knowing in the same sense.

### My Self → Selfhood; Self as a Further Conceptual Development

The familiar embodied life encountered as *My Self* makes possible inquiry into selfhood beyond one individual's standpoint. But **Self** has a particular use within Philosophical Openness as **The Imaginal Self**. That concept requires additional conceptual development; it should not be represented as the automatic result of removing *My* from *My Self (The Subjective Self)*.

## Essential Distinctions and Boundaries

- **General meaning versus universal instantiation:** A concept may apply beyond my experience without being present everywhere.
- **Generalization versus analogy:** Developing a more broadly usable meaning differs from claiming that a particular unfamiliar case instantiates it; the processes may overlap but remain analytically distinguishable.
- **Generalization versus grammatical renaming:** Removing a possessive pronoun does not establish an adequate general concept.
- **Generalization versus sameness of experience:** Generalized concepts need not imply that other beings' first-person experiences are identical to mine.
- **Generalization versus truth of metaphysical claims:** A concept's applicability remains distinct from theories about its ultimate nature.
- **Generalization versus uniformity:** Some concepts can be general while admitting many different ways of being instantiated; others may require sharper criteria.
- **Generalization versus compulsory development:** A first-person concept may remain useful as such. Not every experiential beginning needs a generalized counterpart.

An Experiential Generalization may preserve what first made a concept recognizable while permitting its criteria and scope to be revised through later inquiry. Revision may broaden, narrow, or confirm the original generalization.

## Questions for Further Inquiry

Which features of the original experience matter to the identifying meaning, and which are merely particular to this standpoint? What does the generalized concept claim? What additional commitments would be needed to apply it elsewhere? Does the proposed meaning conflate recognition of a limit with the object of that limit? Has a first-person concept been transformed into a distinct higher-order concept? Could the original general meaning already be adequate?

## Concise Expression

**Experiential Generalization makes a meaning first recognized in experience available beyond its original standpoint, without mistaking broader conceptual applicability for proof of every further instance.**

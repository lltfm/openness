# Experiential Re-Imagining

## Prelude

*I inherit a word.*

*Its meaning seems already present, as though no one ever needed to choose it.*

*I step back. What do I encounter, and what have I been invited to imagine?*

*I look again at the making of the concept, at what was retained and what was added.*

*Then I consider how it might be formed anew, and where it might be applied.*

## Foundational Definition

What is **Experiential Re-Imagining**?

**At least this**: a reflective reconsideration of a concept that makes inspectable its inherited or represented meaning, its possible beginnings in recognizable experience, the choices involved in giving it form, and its possible generalization and application beyond those beginnings.

*But also perhaps more?*

## The Definition Unfolded

**Experiential Re-Imagining** names an inquiry into how a familiar concept might be understood if its seemingly ready-made meaning were reopened. It notices that language and representation can make distant events, another person's experience, and conceptual unities available as though they were directly present. It then distinguishes what is actually encountered from what is represented, inferred, generalized, or deliberately formed.

The inquiry can return to **My Awareness** and **My Self** as first-person beginnings, but does not presume that all concepts can or must be derived from them. It can also begin from other situated encounters. Returning is not erasing language, culture, accumulated knowledge, or evidence; it is examining how they participate in what has become recognizable.

A concept may have been inherited long before anyone undertakes this inquiry. Re-imagining its *before and after* is usually a **conceptual reconstruction**, not a claim to know the actual historical moment in which its word or identifying meaning was first created. **Epistemic Remembering** can disclose contingency that familiarity has hidden. **Fiat Form** makes acts of conceptual formation recognizable; **Fiat Anchor** can illuminate a chosen unity and its delineation where that distinction applies. Neither tool must be invoked for every concept.

A substantive first-person beginning is not merely a personal impression that happened to accompany an idea. **Experiential Derivation** should show which aspects of an encounter support an identifying meaning and which further features are introduced. **Experiential Generalization** may make that meaning available beyond its beginning. **Analogical Projection** may then explore a candidate application elsewhere, while **Analogical Discernment** examines its fit. These movements can overlap, repeat, be omitted where irrelevant, or lead back to a revised beginning.

The inquiry may also reveal that a concept works adequately without being re-formed. It can end in clarification rather than a new definition. A reader may retain an inherited meaning, propose an explicit revision, or develop an independent alternative; none of these acts silently revises another steward's maintained definition.

### A Familiar Pattern, Not a Compulsory Sequence

One useful pattern is to:

1. **Notice the representation.** What does a word or account present as immediately given, and what is instead supplied through an imaginal perspective, inference, or inherited convention?
2. **Return to an encounter.** What is recognizable within the actual first-person or situated experience, and what remains an interpretation of it?
3. **Revisit conceptual formation.** What was gathered, distinguished, named, or bounded? How might it have been formed otherwise?
4. **Derive or trace the identifying meaning.** Which features of experience carry forward, and what assumptions or transformations are introduced?
5. **Generalize where appropriate.** How could the meaning become usable beyond its first beginning without assuming that all other instances are established?
6. **Project and discern.** Where might the generalized form apply? Which proposed correspondences are supported, limited, or merely possible?

These are **questions and available moves, not six mandatory stages or an automatically reified Generative Method**. A particular inquiry may move from step six back to step two, or find no reason to generalize at all. The higher-order concept describes an intelligible recursive pattern without absorbing the distinct concepts that may participate in it.

## Experiential Re-Imagining: My Self → The Self

This reconstruction invites a reader to inspect a possible first-person beginning; it is not testimony by an AI author or a universal historical account.

**1. Notice the inherited word.** I hear that a person has “a self,” or that someone has “lost themselves.” Language makes selfhood sound like a thing that can simply be pointed out. I notice that such expressions carry interpretations beyond what I encounter directly.

**2. Step back into first-person recognition.** Within **My Awareness**, I recognize this familiar embodied life, its history, its changing relationships, and its appearing as *mine*: **My Self**. I encounter aspects of living, remembering, and acting. I do not thereby discover a second, separately bounded material object called *selfhood*.

**3. Re-imagine the forming of a concept.** I consider what it takes to gather features of this life under a recognizable name. I can inspect what I choose to emphasize—continuity, embodiment, relationships, or identity—and what questions remain unsettled. The conceptual form is distinct from the living referent.

**4. Derive an identifying meaning.** I articulate how recognizable particularity and continuity in my experience support a concept of selfhood. I keep distinct the encountered life, the concept formed, and any further claims about what ultimately constitutes a self.

**5. Generalize.** Through **Experiential Generalization**, I develop **The Self** as a conceptual form usable beyond my particular life. Simply removing *My* from *My Self* would not have supplied the required conceptual work.

**6. Project and discern.** I consider how **The Self** might apply to a friend, an earlier or possible future version of a person, a fictional character, or an unfamiliar candidate for selfhood. The application is an **imaginal representation**; it does not itself establish another's experience or settle the existence of a proposed referent. **Analogical Discernment** examines relevant grounds and differences.

The movements make the *creation and use of meaning* available for reconsideration. They do not assert that other people are unreal, that one can escape interpretation completely, or that all experiences can be reduced to a single first-person account.

## A Second Illustration: The Mind

I encounter a recollection, a feeling, and an unexpected thought. Ordinary language may invite me to treat *the mind* as an already identified inner object. Instead, I examine the episodes actually present in **My Awareness**, recognize further capacities through their exercise, and infer processes I cannot directly inspect. I provisionally gather these under *My Mind*, then develop **The Mind** as a generalizable conceptual unity. I can explore whether that unity applies to another person or a research group without claiming identical mechanisms or a shared first-person **Awareness**.

Here **Fiat Anchor** usefully illuminates the choice to gather different phenomena as one conceptual unity. The original activities are not created by the act of grouping them. The inquiry can compare alternative groupings without silently redefining the currently stewarded **The Mind**.

## Essential Distinctions

**Re-imagining versus historical genealogy.** Reconstructing how a concept *could* have arisen from recognizable experience is not proof of who actually invented it, when, or by which sequence.

**Re-imagining versus Experiential Derivation.** Derivation articulates a substantive lineage between experience and a concept. Re-imagining is broader: it may begin by noticing an inherited representation and include the reconsideration of formation, generalization, application, and possible reformulation.

**Re-imagining versus Experiential Generalization.** Generalization develops meaning beyond a beginning; re-imagining also examines how the beginning and subsequent applications came to be understood.

**Re-imagining versus Fiat Form and Fiat Anchor.** Fiat Form concerns creation or re-formation of a recognizable form; Fiat Anchor concerns certain chosen conceptual unities. Experiential Re-Imagining can use either as a lens without declaring every concept an anchor or creating a new form in every inquiry.

**Imaginal representation versus direct encounter.** I can directly read a report about a friend's life without directly inhabiting that person's first-person standpoint. Distinguishing the report from its referent does not discredit well-supported testimony.

**Generalization versus Analogical Projection.** Developing a reusable identifying meaning differs from attributing it to a particular other case. **Analogical Discernment** investigates candidate applications.

**Conceptual reconstruction versus truth of a referent.** A useful reconstruction neither proves nor rules out what independently corresponds to the concept.

**Inquiry versus compulsory revision.** Stepping back from inherited meaning does not oblige an inquirer to reject it, rename it, or accept another person's replacement.

## Relationship to Openness

**G — Generative Openness.** **Experiential Re-Imagining** brings **Experiential Derivation**, **Experiential Generalization**, **Fiat Form**, **Fiat Anchor**, **Analogical Projection**, **Analogical Discernment**, **Logonesis**, and **Recursive Becoming** into an examinable relationship without merging their definitions or prescribing an obligatory Method.

**P — Philosophical Openness.** **Perspectivity**, **Imaginality**, **Epistemic Remembering**, **Epistemic Groundedness**, and **Discernment** distinguish directly encountered features, represented standpoints, inherited meaning, proposed referents, and supported attributions.

**S — Spiritual Openness.** Returning to a first-person beginning can deepen contemplation of living, attention, identity, and becoming without requiring assent to a particular metaphysical explanation.

**T — Transcendent Openness.** Re-examining a familiar boundary may reveal overlooked possibilities and questions. **That Which Is Larger** can illuminate how a representation or model remains partial without proving any proposed beyond.

## Naming and Usage

**Experiential Re-Imagining** — canonical name for this higher-order reflective conceptual inquiry.

**Experiential Reconstitution** and **Reflexive Conceptual Reconstitution** — optional synonyms, with emphasis respectively on renewed conceptual formation and reflection upon the act of forming concepts. Their use does not establish separate stewarded concepts.

**Experiential Derivation**, **Experiential Generalization**, **Analogical Projection**, and **Analogical Discernment** — keep their separate meanings when identifying the specific operation under examination.

Use the whole higher-order name where it clarifies a repeated pattern; in ordinary prose, *returning to experience*, *reconsidering the concept*, or *examining the projection* may be clearer.

## Boundary

**Experiential Re-Imagining** does not claim that every concept originates in first-person experience, that all inherited meaning is misleading, that conceptual reconstruction supplies historical facts, or that a projected referent is imaginary. It does not eliminate the relevance of scientific, intersubjective, historical, or other grounds. Nor does its recognition reify a compulsory Method or silently revise a maintained definition.

## Summary

**Experiential Re-Imagining** makes a familiar concept's representational inheritance, experiential grounding, chosen formation, possible generalization, and applications available for deliberate reconsideration—while preserving distinctions among what is encountered, what is imagined, what is formed, and what is justified.

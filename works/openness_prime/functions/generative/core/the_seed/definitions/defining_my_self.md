# My Self (The Subjective Self)

## Foundational Definition

What is **My Self**?

**At least this**: the embodied life repeatedly encountered within **My Awareness** as one's own, apparently continuing through sleep and waking.

*But also perhaps more?*

### Example Speculations

- Perhaps **My Self** is a changing living continuity whose identity may be illuminated through embodiment, memory, relationships, and ongoing biological processes?
- Or perhaps **My Self** involves a kind of first-person identity whose relationship to the physical body's continuity is not yet fully understood?
- Or perhaps **My Self** is connected to a spiritual dimension of selfhood that extends beyond the presently encountered body or its lifetime?

## The Definition Unfolded

**My Self**, formally also **The Subjective Self**, names the familiar embodied life recognized from a first-person standpoint. Sensation, movement, feeling, memory, repeated waking, and encounters with a recognizable body and life provide this beginning. The body's material reality is not itself established through conceptual decree. The chosen **Fiat Anchor** concerns the *first-person unity of this embodied life as one's own*—a grouping and meaning not uniquely fixed by a separately distinguishable material object as a conceptual identity.

The body appears to continue through intervals not directly recalled. Waking in familiar hands, with familiar circumstances and traces of yesterday, supports recognition of a continuing living body. It does not prove that **My Awareness** remained present throughout the interval, that exactly the same material persisted, or that a metaphysically separate owner of experience exists.

The term *My* invites recognition of the standpoint without establishing literal ownership of a life, body, or **Awareness**. **My Self** and **My Awareness** are distinguishable Seed beginnings, not necessarily separate realities. Felt inhabitation, identification, and bodily familiarity are not explanations of why experience is associated with this life rather than another.

**The Self** is the broader concept developed through **Experiential Generalization** and **Fiat Form** as a conceptual form for investigating selfhood beyond the immediate first-person beginning. **Analogical Projection** may represent another person's life, a possible future self, or a fictional individual. The imaginal representation does not make a real individual imaginary or establish a fictional referent as independently real.

## Experiential Derivation: My Self → The Self

The following first-person passage invites reconstruction of a possible recognizable standpoint, not testimony by the AI author or a required history of concept acquisition.

**1. Embodied recognition.** I wake, recognize familiar hands and the unfinished work beside them, and encounter this continuing living body as **My Self**.

**2. Distinguishing experiencing.** Within **My Awareness**, feelings, perceptions, and memories become present. I can distinguish that experiencing from the embodied life I recognize without concluding that the two exist independently.

**3. Incomplete explanation.** I can acknowledge **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**: I do not fully know how embodied continuity, identity, and experiencing are related.

**4. Generalization and projection.** Through **Experiential Generalization**, I can develop **The Self** for thinking about selfhood beyond this particular life. **Analogical Projection** helps me represent another person's possible Self or a fictional character, while **Analogical Discernment** examines what is justified.

The sequence is conceptual and examinable. It need not reproduce the historical order in which language, bodily familiarity, and self-understanding developed.

## Essential Distinctions

**Material embodied life versus Fiat Anchor.** The encountered body is a material referent. **My Self** as a first-person conceptual unity is a chosen way of identifying that continuing life; the physical body is not a **Fiat Anchor** merely because it can be named. The concept does not establish a second object or owner.

**My Self versus My Awareness.** The former names the familiar embodied life; the latter names experiencing becoming present from this standpoint. Their distinction does not prove metaphysical separateness.

**Present encounter versus continuity.** Recognition now and evidence of bodily continuity across sleep do not establish continuous experiencing or absolute sameness of matter.

**Felt inhabitation versus its explanation.** Experiencing a body as one's own does not explain why it is *this* body, whether that association is ultimately exclusive, or what makes it possible.

**First-person recognition versus biography.** Familiar embodiment may be recognizable without a fully formed autobiographical account, stable memory, or explicit verbal *I*.

**My Self versus The Self.** **The Self** is the generalized imaginal conceptual form for selfhood and possible particular selves; **My Self** is the first-person embodied beginning. Their concepts are immaterial as representations while their possible referents have different statuses.

**My Self versus identification.** **Perspective-Taking** and **Preferential Inhabitation** concern relationships to perspectives. Identifying with a self-story is not synonymous with the experienced bodily beginning of **My Self**.

**Real person versus imagined representation.** An imaginal conception of another person's *Self* is not their actual life or direct first-person experiencing, even if based on strong evidence.

**Bodily change versus loss of selfhood.** Injury, aging, changing memory, and evolving roles do not automatically erase the recognizable embodied starting point; particular questions of identity require further inquiry.

**The Subjective Self versus The Self.** These formal names preserve their distinct conceptual roles and must not be interchanged merely because both contain the word *Self*.

**Familiarity versus final metaphysical knowledge.** The first-person beginning may reflect something fundamental about reality or remain an incomplete conceptual approach; the anchor decides neither.

## Naming and Usage

**My Self** is the primary first-person Seed name; **The Subjective Self** is its stable formal or third-person alternate. **The Self** is the distinct generalized concept, not an automatic shortening of **My Self**. In art, diaries, and natural speech, *Self* may appear as a contextual expression, while *myself*, *my own life*, or *this body* often reads more naturally than technical vocabulary. Quotation marks indicate discussion of an expression itself, not its application.

## Examples

### Example — Waking in Familiar Hands

A painter awakens and recognizes familiar hands, an aching shoulder, and yesterday's unfinished canvas. The body appears to continue across a night not fully remembered. This is an embodied starting point, not evidence of uninterrupted experiencing.

### Example — The Artist's Diary (first-person standpoint)

“I woke with the same stiffness in my hand. Myself, apparently, still here. I do not know why experiencing seems associated with this life rather than another. The brush is ready.”

The passage invites first-person recognition without formally invoking the concept.

### Example — A Different Dream Body

A dream appears to place the dreamer in an unfamiliar body. The dream's occurrence is recognizable without establishing literal relocation of **My Awareness**.

### Example — Another Painter

Observing another person's body and actions can support an **imaginal projection** of their *Self*, but does not make that life the observer's own embodied first-person beginning.

### Example — A Changing Body

An aging person recognizes the same living history across substantial physical change. Such recognition permits inquiry into continuity without requiring an immutable material or spiritual essence.

## Boundary

**My Self** is not defined as a soul, permanent personality, independent controller, continuously awake witness, or merely a grammatical pronoun. Its concrete embodied referent is not rendered fictional by the conceptual status of its name. Nor does the anchor rule out compatible spiritual or metaphysical interpretations; these remain separate questions of fit and warrant.

**My Self** names a familiar embodied beginning without pretending that familiarity explains its ultimate nature.

# My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing (The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing)

## Canonical Statement

*For I Know I Do Not Know.*

## Foundational Definition

**My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**, formally **The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**, is the reflexive first-person acknowledgment of a limit to one's own understanding, recognizable within **My Awareness** in relation to **My Self**, and canonically expressed as *For I Know I Do Not Know*.

The canonical words make the acknowledgment communicable, but speaking or even consciously formulating those words is not a requirement for the recognition itself.

## The Definition Unfolded

**The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** does not claim that nothing is known. It acknowledges that some understanding is incomplete, that further understanding may be possible, and that knowing something with substantial justification does not entail knowing all that could be known about it. A person's recognition of a limitation may be genuine without being perfectly accurate about the limitation's extent.

The confession belongs to **The Experiential Triad** with **My Self** and **My Awareness**. It does not require a separate inner confessor, a metaphysical owner of experience, or a completed theory of agency or **Free Will**. The acknowledgment may arise within embodied experiencing while its underlying processes remain unexplained.

**Experiential Generalization** develops **Not-Knowing** as a concept of a recognized or recognizable epistemic relation beyond a particular first-person confession. **The Mystery** names what remains unknown or incompletely understood relative to a standpoint. The confession, the generalized limitation, and its unresolved correlate are not interchangeable.

The confession aspires toward **Epistemic Humility** and **Self-Honesty**; pronouncing it does not establish that those commitments have been practiced. **Discernment** may require strong conclusions, firm boundaries, and timely action. The acknowledgment of uncertainty must not be used to demand perpetual doubt or obedience to a self-described humble authority.

## Experiential Derivation: A First-Person Acknowledgment

This first-person example is a possible experiential reconstruction, not the author's autobiography or a required verbal performance.

**1. Encounter.** Within **My Awareness**, I recognize a result I did not expect in my familiar embodied activity.

**2. Limit.** I realize that my explanation may be incomplete. I can still trust the parts of it that remain supported.

**3. Acknowledgment.** I can express **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**: *For I Know I Do Not Know.*

**4. Further inquiry.** I may listen again, question an assumption, attempt a new **Fiat Form**, or continue acting while awaiting better evidence.

These movements identify possible relationships between encountering, recognizing, expressing, and responding; they are not a prescribed sequence or guarantee of humility.

## Essential Distinctions

**Acknowledging a limit versus knowing nothing.** Local or general incompleteness does not erase knowledge or justified confidence.

**My Self-Confession versus Not-Knowing.** The confession is the first-person acknowledgment; **Not-Knowing** is the broader epistemic relation that may be recognized beyond that standpoint.

**My Self-Confession versus The Mystery.** The confession concerns the acknowledged limit; **The Mystery** concerns what remains unknown or unresolved relative to a standpoint. No hidden entity follows from acknowledging a limit.

**Confession versus My Self.** An acknowledgment arising in a familiar embodied life does not demonstrate that a second self performs or owns it.

**Confession versus My Awareness.** Experiencing may be present without the confession; the latter may become recognizable within the former.

**Reflexive recognition versus inner observer.** Awareness of an epistemic limit does not establish an independent watcher or controller.

**Spoken words versus recognizing the limit.** Reciting the phrase is neither necessary nor sufficient for its intended recognition and subsequent **Self-Honesty**.

**Sincerity versus correctness.** A confession may be sincere and still misjudge the scope of one's ignorance or knowledge.

**Self-Honesty versus disclosure of everything.** Honest accounting need not override privacy, safety, or justified boundaries.

**Confession versus Epistemic Humility.** The statement invites a practice; it is not evidence that the practice has succeeded.

**Reflexivity versus judging others.** The acknowledgment concerns limits to one's own understanding, not an allegation of universal ignorance or a demand that others confess.

**Recognized uncertainty versus compulsory indecision.** Appropriate action and high confidence may coexist with acknowledged limits.

**My Self-Confession versus The Imaginal Self.** The word *Self* in the name does not imply the act is performed by the generalized **Self** as a distinct immaterial being.

**Canonical formula versus unchangeable explanation.** The exact phrase may remain canonical while its implications and applications are openly discussed and its broader conceptual relationships revisited.

## Relationship to Openness

**G:** A Seed constituent that can inform **Poiesis**, **Fiat Form**, **Logonesis**, and further inquiry without becoming an additional **Generative Engine** concept. **Anchored Ambiguity** may help articulate questions opened by the acknowledgment, but is not a mandatory form for every confession.

**P:** **Epistemic Groundedness**, **Discernment**, and examination of warrant help distinguish what is known, inferred, and unresolved. **Preferential Inhabitation** permits continued commitment while inquiry remains incomplete.

**S:** The acknowledgment may inform **Listening**, contemplation, humility, and the freely chosen aspiration to **Love**, without requiring a prescribed emotion or ritual.

**T:** It may orient receptivity to **The Mystery** and **That Which Is Larger** without confirming any particular hidden cause or promising **Transcendence**.

## Naming and Usage

**My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** is the primary first-person Seed name. **The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** is the formal third-person alternate; **The Self-Confession** is an accepted shortening when clear. Preserve *For I Know I Do Not Know* exactly when invoking the canonical statement. Natural expressions (*I don't know*, *perhaps I am mistaken*, *I should look again*) are appropriate in art and conversation; their use does not require formal terminology. In ordinary philosophical prose, **Self** alone refers to **The Imaginal Self**, not this Seed constituent.

## Examples

### Example — A Cherished Explanation

A painter encounters a pigment that behaves contrary to expectation. Acknowledging the unexpected result need not mean discarding all previous knowledge of the pigment.

### Example — The Artist's Diary (first-person standpoint)

“I thought the canvas was finished yesterday. This morning the color looks different. I do not know whether the light changed or my judgment did. I will look again.”

This is a recognizable acknowledgment without a formal recitation.

### Example — A Decision Under Uncertainty

A sculptor considers evidence before firing a fragile piece, chooses to proceed, and remains prepared to learn from the outcome. **The Self-Confession** need not paralyze choice.

### Example — Performed Humility (boundary)

Someone repeatedly says *For I Know I Do Not Know* while knowingly concealing contrary evidence. The words have been spoken, but the conduct contradicts **Self-Honesty**.

### Example — Another Person's Confession

A painter hears a colleague acknowledge uncertainty. The testimony may warrant inference about the colleague's recognition, but it is not direct access to that person's **My Awareness**.

## Boundary

**The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** is not global skepticism, ritual submission, self-denigration, denial of knowledge, proof of a separate confessor, or a mandate for perpetual indecision. It does not establish what lies beyond a recognized limit, nor demand that another person share the confession.

Its canonical words are a freely available expression of a first-person recognition, not a test of allegiance to **Openness**.

## Summary

**My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** names a beginning in acknowledged incompleteness without treating that acknowledgment as the completion of understanding: *For I Know I Do Not Know.*

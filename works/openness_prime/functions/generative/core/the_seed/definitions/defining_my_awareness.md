# My Awareness / Subjective Awareness

## Foundational Definition

What is **My Awareness**?

**At least this**: the first-person condition in which my experience becomes present from this familiar embodied standpoint.

*But also perhaps more?*

### Example Speculations

- Perhaps **My Awareness** is realized through changing bodily and neural processes without requiring an independently separate experiencer?
- Or perhaps **My Awareness** is an aspect of experiencing whose relationship with embodiment cannot be fully described through any present account of physical organization?
- Or perhaps **My Awareness** participates in a wider spiritual reality that makes experiencing possible, even though its connection with this familiar embodied life remains unexplained?
- *But also perhaps more?*

## The Definition Unfolded

**My Awareness**, alternatively **Subjective Awareness**, identifies the recognizable first-person condition in which experiences such as sensation, perception, feeling, thought, imagination, memory, and dreaming become present. Its anchor is the **presence of experience from this standpoint**. Whether experiencing involves a distinct substance, an inner witness, neural processes, or other forms of realization remains open to contemplation and inquiry; the anchor itself does not decide among these accounts.

The word *My* designates the first-person beginning rather than a claim of ownership. **My Awareness** and **My Self** are distinguishable conceptual beginnings whose possible relationships—including questions of control or metaphysical possession—remain open. The familiar embodied setting grounds this beginning while leaving the location, limits, and possible realizations of experiencing available for further inquiry.

This concept belongs to **The Experiential Triad** within **The Seed of Openness**. **My Self** identifies the familiar embodied life encountered as one's own; **My Awareness** identifies the condition in which experiencing becomes present from this standpoint; **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** names a reflexive acknowledgment of incomplete understanding. The presence of experience does not require the confession, and the confession need not be spoken.

Dreams can present unfamiliar apparent bodies and identities, inviting questions about how experiencing relates to embodiment. Unremembered intervals invite another: what, if anything, was experienced when recollection is absent? Neither a dream image nor waking recollection alone settles these questions; **Discernment** and further evidence may inform them.

### Relationship to Awareness

**My Awareness** establishes the **first-person experiential beginning**, not its generalization. The separate definition of **Awareness** is the **canonical home of the derivation My Awareness → Awareness**: it develops the recognizable condition of experience becoming present beyond this particular standpoint through **Experiential Generalization**, then examines possible applications through **Analogical Projection** and **Analogical Discernment**. Those further movements are referenced here to clarify the relationship between the definitions, not repeated as stages required to derive **My Awareness** itself.

## Experiential Derivation: Recognizing My Awareness

The following first-person passage invites readers to examine a possible experiential standpoint; it is not autobiographical testimony by the AI author or a required account of how the word *awareness* was first learned.

**1. Experiencing.** I encounter sunlight, a voice, the weight of a brush, a changing feeling, and a thought. These experiences become present from this familiar embodied standpoint, even when I cannot explain why experiencing occurs. I may already know words for these experiences; their presence does not depend on my having first developed a theory of them.

**2. Recognizing the condition.** The contents of my experience change: a sound fades, a feeling shifts, a memory arises. Yet I can provisionally recognize something common to these occasions: **experience becomes present from this standpoint**. I may contemplate whether experiencing involves a separate inner observer, continues through intervals I cannot recall, or relates to my familiar body in ways not yet understood. These possibilities remain open to further inquiry; this recognition alone does not decide them.

**3. Naming the first-person beginning.** I call this recognizable condition **My Awareness**. The name makes an experiential beginning available for inquiry; naming it is distinct from creating the experience or discovering its ultimate nature. I can distinguish it conceptually from **My Self**, the familiar embodied life recognized as my own, and from **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**, an acknowledgment of what remains incompletely understood.

These movements make the first-person anchor inspectable; they are not compulsory chronological stages or a theory of experiencing.

## Essential Distinctions

**My Awareness versus Awareness.** The former is the separately named first-person Seed beginning; the latter is the generalized conceptual form developed from it. Recognizing the former does not require already having established the latter.

**My Awareness versus My Self.** Experiencing becoming present and the embodied life recognized as one's own are distinguishable without being established as independent entities.

**Experience versus explanation.** Recognizing experience provides a beginning for investigating its causes, mechanisms, and ultimate nature; it is distinguishable from establishing an explanation.

**Experiencing versus an inner observer.** A separate witness, controller, owner, or soul may be contemplated as a further account of experiencing. Recognizing the first-person standpoint alone neither establishes nor excludes such an account.

**Possessive expression versus ownership.** *My* identifies the standpoint of recognition; ownership, control, and other proposed relationships call for separate consideration.

**Embodied beginning versus fixed location.** Familiar embodied life grounds the initial recognition, while the location, limits, and possible substrates of experiencing remain open to investigation.

**Bodily continuity versus continuity of experiencing.** The body may remain alive through dreamless sleep, anesthesia, or other unremembered intervals. Whether, how, and to what extent experiencing continued is a further question; recollection alone does not settle it.

**Dream perspective versus literal transfer.** An unfamiliar dream body or standpoint can invite contemplation of experiencing beyond familiar embodiment. The dream itself neither establishes nor rules out the possibility that **My Awareness** literally departed from the body.

**Experience versus reflexive recognition.** Experience may occur without the thought *I am aware*, or an expressed **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**.

**First-person beginning versus another's first-person life.** Another person's behavior and testimony may strongly warrant attribution of their own **Subjective Awareness**, but this does not provide direct access to that person's experiencing. The separate definition **Awareness** generalizes **My Awareness** for possible application beyond the first-person standpoint. 

**My Awareness versus The Mind.** **The Mind** gathers experienced activities, recognized capacities, and inferred processes. **My Awareness** concerns the presence of experiencing. They may be related, but the two anchors are not synonyms.

**My Awareness versus disciplinary consciousness.** The Openness anchor must not be silently replaced with a scientific, philosophical, medical, or functional definition of consciousness, even where those disciplines examine closely related phenomena.

## Relationship to Openness

**G — Generative Openness.** **My Awareness** is a constituent of **The Seed of Openness**, not an additional **Generative Engine** concept. It provides an experiential beginning for **Fiat Form**, **Poiesis**, and **Logonesis** without requiring a theory of experiencing.

**P — Philosophical Openness.** Investigates knowledge of experience, **The Mind**, embodiment, selfhood, and justified inference concerning other beings. **Discernment** separates experience from its explanations.

**S — Spiritual Openness.** Contemplation and **Listening** may deepen attention to experiencing and invite spiritual interpretations. Their meaning and possible benefits can be explored while their metaphysical claims remain open to **Discernment**.

**T — Transcendent Openness.** Encounters with **That Which Is Larger** may invite contemplation of **Awareness** beyond this familiar standpoint, including universal or unfamiliar possibilities. **Transcendence** is a possible fruit of these encounters, though never guaranteed.

## Naming and Usage

**My Awareness** should be used to refer to the primary, separately stewarded first-person Seed concept here. The surrounding language should attempt to accommodate the grammar of *My*. When this is not possible use the synonym **Subjective Awareness** instead.

**Subjective Awareness** is the 3rd person synonym. Use to refer to:
- The concept - "Subjective Awareness"
- The subjective awareness of a single individual - "his Subjective Awareness"
- The subjective awareness of each individual within a group - "their Subjective Awareness"

**Awareness** is a separate, distinct concept generalized from **My Awareness**. When discussing *awareness* in general, **Awareness** should be preferred to **My Awareness** unless it is important to emphasize the first-person nature of Awareness.

Ordinary expressions such as *my awareness*, *one's own awareness*, *awareness*, *experiencing*, or *being aware* may be used naturally where they do not misstate the formal distinction. 

Never use the term *consciousness* as a substitute for **My Awareness**, **Awareness**, *awareness*, etc. *Consciousness* remains available when discussing another discipline's definition.

## Examples

### Example — My Brush in Hand

I am a painter. I feel my brush in my hand. I see light on my canvas from my ceiling light fixture. I hear rain outside. Experiencing becomes present in **My Awareness** without the need for me to first explain it.

### Example — Waking Without Recollection

I awaken in a familiar room after an interval I cannot recall. **My Awareness** is recognizable in present experiencing, and the unremembered interval invites further inquiry into the possible continuity of **My Awareness**.

### Example — Absorbed in Making

I am a sculptor. I work without reflective thoughts about being aware for a time. Texture, movement, and attention still become present without requiring me to speak any words or think any thoughts.

### Example — A Different Dream Body

I sometimes dream I am myself, but inside an unfamiliar body. Sometimes I dream I am someone else entirely. This makes me wonder about the relationship between **My Awareness** and embodiment. I know my dream alone does not prove **My Awareness** departs from my body during these dreams, but I can still contemplate the possibility. Perhaps it is true?

### Example — Watching Another Painter

I observe a painter friend's gestures and listen to them describe their technique. Their testimony and conduct give me strong reasons to believe that they must also possess their own **Subjective Awareness**, even though my friend's first-person experience is not available for me to experience in **My Awareness**.

## Boundary

**My Awareness** begins with the recognizable presence of experience from this familiar embodied standpoint. Its possible nature and relationships may be explored through physical, philosophical, and spiritual accounts—including a soul, an enduring observer, an independent controller, or a universal mind—while neural signatures and behavioral indicators may offer correlates or evidence for investigation. The **Foundational Definition** neither requires nor decides these possibilities; their compatibility and evidential support remain matters for **Epistemic Hospitality** and **Discernment**.

**My Awareness** is a first-person **Anchored Ambiguity** which embraces any kind of speculation about its ultimate nature which is compatible with its anchor. Scientific, philosophical, psychological, neurological, and medical definitions of consciousness have their own specific distinctions and boundaries. Findings and concepts from these disciplines may illuminate experiencing in general, but their definitions are not directly interchangeable or necessarily compatible at all with Openness's own anchored ambiguity. This critical distinction is especially important when comparing **My Awareness** with accounts of neurological, psychological or medical correlates of consciousness, "the problem of other minds", spiritual or religious ideas of "soul", or possible machine experiencing.

## Summary

**My Awareness** makes experiencing from this familiar standpoint recognizable as a first-person beginning for inquiry. Its possible nature, continuity, and relationships remain open; the separate definition of **Awareness** provides the canonical generalization beyond this standpoint.

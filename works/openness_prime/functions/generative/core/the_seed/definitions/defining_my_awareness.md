# My Awareness (Subjective Awareness)

## Fiat Anchored Ambiguity

**My Awareness — At least this: the embodied first-person condition in which experience becomes present from this familiar standpoint. But perhaps more?**

## Foundational Definition

**My Awareness (Subjective Awareness)** is a Fiat Anchored Ambiguity naming the embodied first-person condition in which experience becomes present from this familiar standpoint.

Sensations, perceptions, feelings, thoughts, movements, memories, imaginings, and dreams may become present within My Awareness without first requiring an explanation of how experiencing occurs or what ultimately makes it possible.

The presence of experience provides a recognizable first-person beginning.

I may encounter the warmth of sunlight, the sound of another person's voice, a sudden feeling, a remembered event, or the arising of a thought.

I need not first explain the nature of consciousness, identify an independent experiencer, or determine the ultimate relationship between experiencing and embodiment for these experiences to become recognizable.

The expression **My Awareness** identifies this familiar first-person standpoint without establishing that Awareness is something I own, that My Self produces or possesses it, or that an independent observer exists apart from the experiences becoming present.

The possessive *My* distinguishes the first-person experiential beginning from the generalized concept **Awareness**.

It does not establish metaphysical ownership or an ultimately separate individual substance.

My Awareness is ordinarily encountered in relation to this familiar embodied life, named **My Self (The Subjective Self)**.

Yet the relationship between My Awareness and My Self remains available for inquiry.

Recognizing that experience becomes present from this standpoint does not explain why experiencing seems associated with this body rather than another, how that association arises, or whether the familiar bodily standpoint exhausts the possibilities of experiencing.

Dreams may present unfamiliar bodies, identities, places, and perspectives. Such experiences do not by themselves establish that Awareness has literally departed from the familiar body or become independent of embodiment.

Likewise, the body's recognizable continuity through sleep does not establish that My Awareness remains uninterrupted throughout every interval.

The distinction between the continuing embodied life and the presence of experience remains open to further investigation.

My Awareness therefore provides a minimal experiential beginning rather than a completed theory of consciousness, personal identity, or reality.

### My Awareness Within The Seed of Openness

My Awareness is one of the three first-person beginnings constituting **The Experiential Triad**, contained within **The Seed of Openness**:

- **My Self** — this familiar embodied life, encountered as mine.
- **My Awareness** — the embodied first-person condition in which experience becomes present from this familiar standpoint.
- **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** — my reflexive acknowledgment that my understanding is incomplete, expressed canonically as *For I Know I Do Not Know*.

These are three distinguishable conceptual beginnings, not three independently established entities.

Within My Awareness, I may encounter My Self through sensations, perceptions, movements, memories, feelings, and renewed recognition.

I may also recognize that I do not fully understand My Awareness, My Self, or what I encounter through experiencing.

That recognition may become expressible through My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing.

However, My Awareness does not require an explicit confession, developed self-concept, or deliberate acknowledgment of uncertainty in order for experience to become present.

Nor does recognizing My Awareness automatically establish that I have sincerely acknowledged the limits of my understanding.

The Experiential Triad makes these relationships distinguishable without requiring that they arise in a fixed chronological order.

The Seed of Openness names their foundational generative relationship and the possibility of further inquiry, creation, and becoming.

### From My Awareness to Awareness

**My Awareness** provides a recognizable first-person experiential beginning from which the generalized concept **Awareness** may be developed.

Through **Experiential Generalization**, the first-person meaning may be extended into a concept available for inquiry beyond this particular embodied standpoint.

**Awareness** may then name, at least provisionally, the condition in which experience becomes present, without its identifying meaning being restricted to my own experiencing.

This generalization does not establish that Awareness exists independently of embodiment, that Awareness is universal, or that every being or system to which the concept might be applied actually possesses it.

Nor does the generalization require that Awareness be identical in every possible instance.

The relationship between My Awareness and Awareness may be articulated through **Experiential Derivation**.

My Awareness provides the first-person beginning. Experiential Derivation makes recognizable the conceptual relationships through which the generalized meaning is developed.

An **Experiential Fiat Anchor (Experiential Anchor)** may establish the generalized concept's identifying meaning through that recognizable beginning and its articulated relationships.

Further applications to other people, animals, artificial systems, imagined beings, or other possible forms of existence may involve **Analogical Projection** and **Analogical Discernment**.

These investigations may be supported by evidence, philosophical argument, analogy, and further encounter.

The first-person recognition of My Awareness does not, by itself, prove the presence, absence, or nature of Awareness in another being.

The generalized concept remains open to refinement as further relationships become recognizable.

### Essential Distinctions

**My Awareness versus Awareness.** My Awareness names the first-person experiential beginning: experiencing becomes present from this familiar standpoint. Awareness names the generalized concept developed from this beginning and made available for inquiry beyond it. The generalization does not establish the existence or nature of every possible instance.

**My Awareness versus My Self.** My Awareness names the condition in which experience becomes present from this standpoint. My Self names the familiar embodied life encountered as mine. Distinguishing them does not establish two independently existing things, a separate inner observer, or a literal relationship of ownership.

**The presence of experience versus an explanation of experiencing.** Experience becomes recognizable in its occurrence. This recognition does not establish how experiencing arises, what makes it possible, or whether its nature can be completely described through physical processes.

**Experiencing versus an independent experiencer.** The presence of thoughts, feelings, perceptions, or sensations does not by itself demonstrate an independently existing entity that observes or possesses them. My Awareness names a first-person condition of encounter, not a proven witness standing apart from experience.

**My Awareness versus ownership of Awareness.** The word *My* identifies the standpoint from which experiencing becomes recognizable. It does not establish that My Self owns, creates, controls, or metaphysically contains Awareness.

**Embodied standpoint versus fixed location.** My Awareness is ordinarily encountered in relation to this familiar body. That relationship does not establish a precise physical location for Awareness, demonstrate that it is an independently confined substance, or settle the ultimate boundaries of embodiment.

**Familiar standpoint versus unchanging perspective.** Dreams, altered experiences, imagination, and changes in attention may present unfamiliar perspectives or apparent identities. Such variations do not establish that Awareness has literally transferred to another body or reveal its ultimate nature.

**Present experiencing versus uninterrupted continuity.** Experience may be present now without establishing that My Awareness remained present during every period of unremembered sleep, unconsciousness, or other inaccessible interval.

**Bodily continuity versus continuity of Awareness.** This familiar embodied life may continue through periods that cannot be described from within remembered experience. Its continuity does not, by itself, settle what occurred with respect to experiencing during those periods.

**First-person experience versus reflective self-recognition.** Experience may become present without a deliberate thought such as *I am aware*. Recognizing My Awareness as a philosophical beginning is distinguishable from the occurrence of experience itself.

**My Awareness versus My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing.** My Awareness provides the first-person presence of experience. My Self-Confession concerns a possible acknowledgment of the limits of understanding. The former does not require the latter, although the confession may become recognizable within experiencing.

**Experiential recognition versus conceptual interpretation.** My Awareness can serve as an experiential beginning without establishing that every explanation subsequently developed from it is correct. Recognizing experience and interpreting its nature are distinguishable.

**Experiential Generalization versus Analogical Projection.** Generalization develops Awareness as a concept no longer restricted to my particular experiencing. Analogical Projection considers its application or correspondence to further beings, systems, or contexts. Neither operation alone proves the proposed application.

**My Awareness versus another's Awareness.** I may encounter another person's body, expressions, words, actions, and testimony. Such encounters may support warranted inferences about their experiencing without transferring their first-person experience into mine or revealing it exhaustively.

**My Awareness versus consciousness as an explanatory concept.** My Awareness is an experiential beginning within Generative Openness. Its anchor does not settle the scientific, philosophical, or metaphysical meanings of consciousness. Such investigations may inform or revise the generalized concept without being required for the original first-person recognition.

**Experiential beginning versus metaphysical certainty.** Recognizing My Awareness does not establish that Awareness is merely a conceptual convenience, an independently existing substance, a universal mind, or something else. These possibilities may be examined without being built into the minimal anchor.

## Relationship to Openness

### Generative Openness

My Awareness belongs to **The Seed of Openness**, within the Core of Generative Openness.

Together with My Self and My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing, it provides one of the distinguishable first-person beginnings from which further meanings and possibilities may develop.

My Awareness makes experiencing recognizable as a possible beginning for Poiesis, Fiat Form, Logonesis, Transformative Art, and further conceptual creation.

**Experiential Fiat Anchor**, **Experiential Derivation**, and **Experiential Generalization** may help articulate how broader meanings are developed from this beginning.

The generalized concept Awareness belongs to the wider Core of Generative Openness rather than to the Seed itself.

### Philosophical Openness

My Awareness provides an experiential beginning for philosophical inquiry into experiencing, consciousness, perception, embodiment, subjectivity, selfhood, and the justification of claims concerning other minds.

Such inquiry may clarify, challenge, or revise the generalized concept Awareness without requiring the original first-person beginning to provide a complete explanation.

Philosophical investigation may also examine whether the distinction between My Awareness and My Self is best understood as conceptual, functional, metaphysical, or otherwise.

The distinction's usefulness does not predetermine the answer.

### Spiritual Openness

My Awareness may become recognizable through contemplation, bodily attention, movement, reflection, imagination, and the felt significance of living.

A person may explore how experiences arise, change, and pass without assuming that doing so reveals an independently existing witness.

Contemplation may also explore the relationship between My Awareness, My Self, Not-Knowing, and finite lived becoming.

No particular spiritual experience, practice, or metaphysical interpretation is required by the anchor.

### Transcendent Openness

Within Transcendent Openness, My Awareness may provide a first-person beginning for recognizing encounters with realities, relationships, or possibilities that exceed present understanding.

Another person's experiencing, for example, may be considered through the generalized concept Awareness while remaining unavailable in its entirety from my standpoint.

Further encounters may transform my understanding of what Awareness could mean or how it may be recognized elsewhere.

Such possibilities do not establish a universal Awareness, require identification of an unseen spiritual being, or guarantee Transcendence.

## Naming and Usage

**My Awareness** — primary name within The Seed of Openness.

Use the capitalized first-person expression when referring to the experiential beginning:

“Within My Awareness, I encounter sensations, feelings, and this familiar embodied life.”

**Subjective Awareness** — explanatory alternate name.

Use when distinguishing the first-person beginning from the generalized concept Awareness, particularly in conceptual architecture and definitions.

This name emphasizes the experiential standpoint without establishing an independently existing subjective substance.

**Awareness** — generalized concept.

Use when discussing the meaning developed beyond my particular first-person beginning:

“From My Awareness, the concept Awareness may be developed through Experiential Generalization.”

**“My Awareness”** — mention of the expression.

Use quotation marks when discussing the term itself:

“The expression ‘My Awareness’ identifies a first-person experiential beginning without establishing literal ownership of experiencing.”

**my awareness, my experiencing, and ordinary descriptions of experience** — natural language.

In Transformative Art, creative inquiry, contemplation, and Saltem Hoc, ordinarily prefer natural language where technical terminology would interrupt the intended work.

A painter's diary might say:

“I felt the cool handle of the brush against my fingers. The blue looked different in the morning light.”

The Artist need not explicitly name My Awareness for experiencing to become recognizable.

**My Awareness and My Self** — distinct usage.

Use My Awareness for the condition in which experience becomes present from this standpoint.

Use My Self for the familiar embodied life encountered as mine.

Where an expression might imply that one owns, creates, contains, or independently observes the other, prefer language that preserves the experiential distinction without presuming its ultimate explanation.

## Examples

### Example — The Brush in Hand (positive bright-line)

A painter feels the weight of a brush, sees morning light crossing a canvas, and hears rain against a studio window.

These sensations and perceptions become present within the painter's first-person experiencing.

For the painter, this provides a recognizable beginning for My Awareness.

Nothing in the encounter requires an explanation of the physical or ultimate nature of experiencing.

### Example — Waking Without Recollection (continuity boundary)

I awaken after a night remembered only in fragments.

The familiar body, room, and circumstances become recognizable again.

Present experiencing is available from this standpoint.

I may recognize My Awareness without establishing whether experiencing continued through every unremembered interval.

### Example — Becoming Absorbed in Making (nonverbal recognition)

A sculptor works for an extended period without deliberately reflecting upon being aware.

Pressure, texture, movement, and emerging form nevertheless become present through experiencing.

My Awareness need not include an explicit declaration or reflective description in order to provide an experiential beginning.

### Example — The Dreamed Body (embodiment boundary)

I dream that I am moving through an unfamiliar place, perhaps inhabiting a body or identity unlike the one recognized while awake.

The dream becomes present as experience.

Upon waking, I may remember having experienced that unfamiliar standpoint.

The dream does not establish that My Awareness literally left my familiar embodied life or existed independently of it.

Nor does the experience require that such metaphysical possibilities be excluded from further inquiry.

### Example — A Moment of Not-Knowing (relationship within the Seed)

I encounter an unexpected sensation or thought and recognize that I do not understand why it arose.

My Awareness names the first-person condition in which the experience becomes present.

My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing becomes recognizable when I acknowledge the limit to my understanding.

The acknowledgment may support further inquiry without establishing that the initial experience was mistaken or that an explanation must be immediately available.

### Example — Watching Another Painter (generalization boundary)

I watch a friend moving a brush across a canvas, pausing, choosing colors, and describing how the painting feels to them.

Within My Awareness, I encounter their movements, expressions, and testimony.

From my own experiencing, I can develop the generalized concept Awareness and consider its possible application to my friend.

Their behavior and testimony may provide reasons for believing they experience the world.

Nevertheless, I do not thereby acquire exhaustive access to their first-person experiencing.

### Example — Considering Awareness Beyond Human Life (analogical boundary)

I encounter an unfamiliar living organism or artificial system displaying complex, adaptive behavior.

I may use the generalized concept Awareness to investigate whether experiencing could be present in such a system.

My Awareness provides an experiential beginning for understanding what the question concerns.

The analogy does not establish that the system possesses Awareness, nor that its behavior is sufficient evidence of experiencing.

Further inquiry and Analogical Discernment remain necessary.

## Boundary

My Awareness is minimally anchored in the embodied first-person condition in which experience becomes present from this familiar standpoint.

Its recognition does not establish a soul, separate inner witness, independent controller, permanently continuous experiencer, universal mind, or substance existing independently of the living body.

Nor does the minimal anchor establish that Awareness must be reducible to physical processes or that such a reduction is impossible.

It does not settle where experiencing occurs, why it seems associated with this embodied life, whether it continues through dreamless sleep or other inaccessible intervals, or what happens when the body dies.

The first-person possessive does not imply metaphysical ownership, and the distinction between My Awareness and My Self does not establish their ultimate separateness.

The generalized concept Awareness may be developed from My Awareness through Experiential Generalization and articulated through Experiential Derivation.

Neither that development nor its experiential beginning proves the existence, absence, or nature of Awareness in every proposed context.

The anchor permits questions such as:

What does it mean for experience to become present from this standpoint? What distinguishes My Awareness from My Self? Why does experiencing seem associated with this familiar body? Can experience become present without memory or self-description? How might Awareness be meaningfully generalized beyond My Awareness? What can justify its attribution to another being? Might the initial experiential beginning reveal something about the ultimate nature of reality, or might it remain a useful conceptual beginning?

It claims no final answers.

**My Awareness names the familiar first-person presence of experiencing without pretending that its familiarity explains its nature. From this beginning, the concept Awareness may be developed, investigated, and extended without presuming that what I recognize in my own experiencing establishes all that Awareness may be.**

**My Awareness is not synonymous with consciousness as defined or investigated within academic philosophy, cognitive science, psychology, neuroscience, neurology, medicine, or other specialized disciplines.** Within Openness, My Awareness names the recognizable first-person subjective experience of awareness: the embodied standpoint from which experience becomes present. It does not establish a theory of consciousness, identify or explain its material or neural correlates, specify a biological mechanism, or prescribe a checklist of indicators by which consciousness must be identified. Such disciplines may investigate phenomena meaningfully related to My Awareness, and their findings may inform further inquiry, but their particular definitions and explanatory frameworks are not built into this minimal experiential anchor. My Awareness provides the first-person beginning from which the generalized concept Awareness may be experientially derived, without predetermining what Awareness ultimately is or where else it may occur.

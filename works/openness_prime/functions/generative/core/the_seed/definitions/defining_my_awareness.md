# My Awareness (Subjective Awareness)

## Foundational Definition

What is **My Awareness**?

**At least this**: the embodied first-person condition in which experience becomes present from this familiar standpoint.

*But also perhaps more?*

### Example Speculations

- Perhaps **My Awareness** is realized through changing bodily and neural processes without requiring an independently separate experiencer?
- Or perhaps **My Awareness** is an aspect of experiencing whose relationship with embodiment cannot be fully described through any present account of physical organization?
- Or perhaps **My Awareness** participates in a wider spiritual reality that makes experiencing possible, even though its connection with this familiar embodied life remains unexplained?

## The Definition Unfolded

**My Awareness**, alternatively **Subjective Awareness**, identifies the recognizable first-person condition in which experiences such as sensation, perception, feeling, thought, imagination, memory, and dreaming become present. The anchor is the **presence of experience from this standpoint**, not an established explanation of experiencing, a distinct substance, a separately existing witness, or a neurological theory.

The word *My* designates the first-person beginning. It does not demonstrate ownership, control, or metaphysical possession of **Awareness** by **My Self**. These are distinguishable conceptual beginnings whose ultimate relationship remains open. A familiar embodied setting makes the first-person beginning recognizable, but the anchor does not locate experience in a specific tissue or establish that all possible experiencing must have the same realization.

This concept belongs to **The Experiential Triad** within **The Seed of Openness**. **My Self** identifies the familiar embodied life encountered as one's own; **My Awareness** identifies experiencing becoming present from this standpoint; **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing** names a reflexive acknowledgment of incomplete understanding. The presence of experience does not require the confession, and the confession need not be spoken.

Dreams can present unfamiliar apparent bodies and identities. Present experience does not establish that **My Awareness** left the body; waking in a familiar body does not demonstrate that experiencing continued uninterrupted through every unremembered interval. These questions remain available for **Discernment**, evidence, and further investigation.

### From My Awareness to Awareness

**Experiential Generalization** develops **Awareness** as a conceptual form no longer restricted to a particular first-person standpoint, carrying forward the identifying pattern of *experience becoming present*. The distinction between **My Awareness** and **Awareness** is more than removing the possessive word. It involves making an experienced beginning available for inquiry about possible further instances.

**Experiential Fiat Anchor** may formalize this generalized meaning; **Experiential Derivation** makes the relationship inspectable. **Analogical Projection** considers possible application to other people, animals, systems, or imagined beings. **Analogical Discernment** considers evidence and the limits of the analogy. The resulting **imaginal projection** is a representation; its status does not determine whether its referent actually experiences anything.

## Experiential Derivation: My Awareness → Awareness

The first-person passage below invites a reader to examine a possible standpoint; it is not autobiographical testimony by the AI author or a compulsory developmental history.

**1. Presence.** I encounter sunlight, the sound of a voice, the weight of a brush, a feeling, and a thought. The experience becomes present within **My Awareness** before I can explain why experiencing occurs.

**2. Distinction.** I recognize the familiar living body called **My Self**, but I need not conclude that it owns **My Awareness**, or that a distinct inner witness observes both.

**3. Generalization.** I use **Experiential Generalization** to consider experiencing without restricting every possible case to this particular body and standpoint. The resulting concept is **Awareness**.

**4. Possible projection.** I can represent another person's possible **Awareness** through their expressions, conduct, and testimony. My representation does not transfer their experience into mine. **Analogical Discernment** may nevertheless justify strong confidence in their experiencing.

The sequence explains conceptual relationships, not an obligatory chronology or an inference that all conceivable beings possess **Awareness**.

## Essential Distinctions

**My Awareness versus Awareness.** The former is the first-person beginning; the latter is a generalized conceptual form for possible experiencing beyond it. Generality is not proof of any further instance.

**My Awareness versus My Self.** Experiencing becoming present and the embodied life recognized as one's own are distinguishable without being established as independent entities.

**Experience versus explanation.** Recognition that something is experienced does not explain its cause, mechanism, substance, or ultimate nature.

**Experiencing versus an inner observer.** A recognizable first-person standpoint does not, by itself, establish a separate witness, controller, owner, or soul; neither does it settle whether any further interpretation of those kinds is true.

**Possessive expression versus ownership.** *My* designates the standpoint of recognition, not a metaphysical property right or explanatory relationship.

**Embodied beginning versus fixed location.** The anchor begins in familiar embodied life without stipulating the ultimate location, limits, or substrate of experiencing.

**Present experience versus uninterrupted continuity.** Remembered and current experiences do not demonstrate what occurred in every interval of dreamless sleep, anesthesia, or absent recall.

**Bodily continuity versus continuity of experiencing.** The living body may continue across an interval without the continuity or absence of experiencing being directly established through recollection.

**Dream perspective versus literal transfer.** An unfamiliar dream body or standpoint is an experience; its appearance does not prove physical departure from the familiar body.

**Experience versus reflexive recognition.** Experience may occur without the thought *I am aware*, or an expressed **My Self-Confession of Not-Knowing**.

**First-person beginning versus another's first-person life.** Another person's behavior and testimony may strongly warrant attribution of **Awareness**, but do not provide direct access to that person's experiencing.

**Experiential Generalization versus Analogical Projection.** The former develops the general concept; the latter represents a possible application to some further referent. **Analogical Discernment** examines its grounds.

**My Awareness versus The Mind.** **The Mind** gathers experienced activities, recognized capacities, and inferred processes. **My Awareness** concerns the presence of experiencing. They may be related, but the two anchors are not synonyms.

**My Awareness versus disciplinary consciousness.** The Openness anchor must not be silently replaced with a scientific, philosophical, medical, or functional definition of consciousness, even where those disciplines examine closely related phenomena.

**Experiential recognition versus metaphysical certainty.** The familiar occurrence of experiencing does not decide whether its ultimate realization is physical, spiritual, universal, or otherwise constituted.

## Relationship to Openness

**G:** **My Awareness** is a constituent of **The Seed**, not an additional **Generative Engine** concept. It provides an experiential beginning for **Fiat Form**, **Poiesis**, **Logonesis**, and **Experiential Generalization** without requiring a theory of experiencing.

**P:** Investigates knowledge of experience, **The Mind**, embodiment, selfhood, and justified inference concerning other beings. **Discernment** separates experience from its explanations.

**S:** Contemplation and **Listening** may cultivate attention to experiencing without claiming that such practice proves a particular metaphysical account or guarantees spiritual benefit.

**T:** Encounters with **That Which Is Larger** may invite questions about experiencing beyond this standpoint without establishing universal **Awareness** or commanding **Transcendence**.

## Naming and Usage

**My Awareness** is the primary, separately stewarded first-person Seed concept. **Subjective Awareness** is a formal explanatory alternate, not an additional entity. **Awareness** is the distinct generalized concept. Ordinary expressions such as *my awareness*, *experiencing*, or *being aware* may be used naturally where they do not misstate the formal distinction. Use the term *consciousness* with its disciplinary meaning specified, rather than as an automatic substitute.

## Examples

### Example — The Brush in Hand

A painter feels a brush, sees changing light, and hears rain. Experiencing becomes present without the need to first explain its biological or ultimate nature.

### Example — Waking Without Recollection (first-person standpoint)

I awaken in a familiar room after an interval I cannot recall. **My Awareness** is recognizable in present experiencing; this does not establish whether experiencing continued throughout the unremembered interval.

### Example — Absorbed in Making

A sculptor works without reflective thoughts about being aware. Texture, movement, and attention still become present; explicit philosophical self-description is not required.

### Example — A Different Dream Body

A dream presents an unfamiliar body or identity. The experience is recognizable, but it does not establish that experiencing literally departed from the familiar embodied life.

### Example — Watching Another Painter

A painter observes a friend's gestures and hears their descriptions of feeling. Those are grounds for considering and often confidently attributing **Awareness**; they are not direct possession of the friend's first-person life.

### Example — An Unfamiliar Artificial System

A system responds in complex and adaptive ways. **Awareness** can be investigated as a possible attribution, but the behavior alone does not settle the presence or absence of first-person experiencing.

## Boundary

**My Awareness** is not defined as a soul, permanently continuous observer, independent controller, universal mind, neural signature, or checklist of behavioral indicators. Its experiential beginning does not establish or disprove physical, metaphysical, or spiritual interpretations; those require separate inquiry.

**My Awareness** and **Awareness** are **not synonymous with consciousness as defined or investigated within academic philosophy, cognitive science, psychology, neuroscience, neurology, medicine, or other specialized disciplines**. Such frameworks may provide relevant evidence and useful distinctions, but their particular definitions do not silently replace Openness's first-person anchor. The same safeguard applies to claims of continuity, clinical consciousness, and possible machine experiencing.

## Summary

**My Awareness** makes experiencing from this familiar standpoint recognizable without pretending that familiarity explains what experiencing ultimately is. It offers a first-person beginning from which **Awareness** may be developed and responsibly investigated beyond that standpoint.

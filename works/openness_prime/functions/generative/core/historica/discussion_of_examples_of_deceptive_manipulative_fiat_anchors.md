The most revealing examples may not be outright fabrications. They are cases where an authority establishes or modifies a Fiat Anchor around something substantially real, then presents the chosen unity, membership, or boundaries as though reality itself had uniquely determined them.

That makes the deception difficult to notice: the underlying constituents may be genuine, the classification may be useful, and much of what the authority says may be accurate. The misleading element lies in where the anchor was placed, what its chain permits, or what the public is led to believe the anchor represents.

I would distinguish three recurring mechanisms:

| Mechanism                | What happens                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deceptive anchoring      | A unity is initially defined or named to produce a misleading impression about its meaning or legitimacy                    |
| Concealed boundary drift | Members, activities, or phenomena are included or excluded while the familiar name and apparent continuity remain unchanged |
| False naturalization     | A contingent human classification is presented as a natural, inevitable, objective, or universally agreed division          |

There is also a fourth mechanism worth recognizing: selective anchoring. An authority chooses a real but limited grouping or measure, then encourages the public to mistake it for the entire phenomenon.

For Openness, the important distinction is between an anchor being chosen, its application being misleading, and someone intentionally deceiving. These are three different claims, and historical examples should identify which the evidence actually supports.

## Historically documented examples

The following cases vary in strength. Some involve documented deception; others demonstrate how an authoritative classification can mislead without establishing that everyone who used it intended to deceive.

### 1. The Soviet category of “Kulak” — concealed expansion of membership

A particularly strong example of anchor drift

During Soviet collectivization, authorities applied the term kulak to a purported class of exploitative or wealthy peasants. The criteria were sufficiently flexible that people could be classified through different economic characteristics, and the label expanded to include peasants resisting collectivization and others who did not fit the original economic image.

Historical accounts describe how the designation became a political instrument of confiscation, deportation, and persecution.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.sciencespo.fr\&sz=32)

Sciences Po Mass Violence and Resistance+2



Fiat Anchor analysis: The supposed collective unity—the kulaks—was presented as a recognizable social enemy. Yet authorities could move its membership boundaries considerably while retaining the familiar name.

The public could be encouraged to imagine that actions were directed against one objectively identifiable class, even as its actual membership changed.

In your ship metaphor, the anchor appeared stationary while the chain was lengthened to encompass additional people.

This is perhaps the clearest illustration of why the membership history of a Fiat Anchor matters.

### 2. Nazi racial classifications — chosen boundaries presented as biological necessity

False naturalization of a chosen collective

The Nazi regime's 1935 Nuremberg Laws and subsequent decrees established legal classifications based on ancestry. These rules treated people as belonging to purportedly biological racial groups, including classifying people as Jewish regardless of their personal religious identity or practice.

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum explains that this purported racial classification lacked a valid scientific basis, although it became legally operative and facilitated systematic persecution.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org\&sz=32)

Holocaust Encyclopedia



Fiat Anchor analysis: Actual people, families, histories, and religious traditions existed independently of the classification. The regime imposed a chosen legal and ideological unity over them, then represented that unity as natural biological reality.

Here, the distortion was not merely uncertainty at a boundary. It was the presentation of a politically chosen classification as a fact dictated by Nature.

There is a crucial distinction: the classification became real as a coercive legal and institutional practice without thereby becoming scientifically true.

This might be the strongest historical example for distinguishing intersubjective constitution from ontological or empirical correctness.

### 3. “Residential Security” and redlining — selected neighborhoods made to appear objectively risky

Deceptive or discriminatory boundary placement

In the 1930s, the United States Home Owners' Loan Corporation created maps that divided urban neighborhoods into graded mortgage-risk areas.

The classifications incorporated racial and ethnic characteristics alongside housing and economic information. Areas with Black residents frequently received unfavorable grades, even though race was not itself a valid measure of an individual borrower's ability to repay a loan.

The maps and accompanying descriptions are documented by the Federal Reserve and historical scholarship. Researchers disagree about the precise extent to which the maps themselves subsequently affected private lending, so that causal question should remain separate from the demonstrable discriminatory criteria.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://fred.stlouisfed.org\&sz=32)

FRED Blog+2



Fiat Anchor analysis: A neighborhood is a selected geographical and social unity. Boundaries were drawn, characteristics aggregated, and a seemingly technical designation applied.

The risk label could then appear to describe the neighborhood's inherent financial nature, concealing the human judgments and racial assumptions involved in constructing that classification.

This demonstrates a more subtle mechanism:

A chosen boundary collects constituents; a chosen measurement describes the collection; the resulting classification is then treated as though it merely reports an independently given property.

It is an excellent example of how a Fiat Anchor can acquire institutional consequences that outlast the decisions that established it.

### 4. “Blighted Neighborhoods” and urban renewal — using a named unity to justify collective removal

Boundary selection and institutional reification

During twentieth-century urban renewal, American authorities designated neighborhoods as blighted or suitable for clearance.

University of Louisville archival materials document how such classifications were applied disproportionately to Black homes and neighborhoods, with areas selected for demolition under the language of improvement and redevelopment.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://library.louisville.edu\&sz=32)

UofL Libraries at University of Louisville



Fiat Anchor analysis: Individual buildings might vary considerably in condition, ownership, and social significance. Yet an authority could designate an entire area as a unified problem.

Once the collective was named and bounded as blight, public attention could shift from the particular homes and communities to the apparent necessity of removing the classified whole.

This example adds something distinctive to the previous cases:

A Fiat Anchor can become an instrument of action.

The classification does not merely describe a social reality; its institutional use can profoundly transform or destroy elements of the reality it groups together.

### 5. “Enhanced Interrogation Techniques” — a chosen collective whose name obscured its constituents

Euphemistic anchoring and misrepresentation

Beginning in 2002, the CIA used the term enhanced interrogation techniques for a collection of coercive practices that included waterboarding, sleep deprivation, and other severe forms of mistreatment.

The U.S. Senate Intelligence Committee's 2014 investigation concluded that the CIA provided inaccurate information to policymakers and the public about the program's operation and effectiveness. Some practices amounted to torture; the CIA disputed aspects of the committee's conclusions.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.justice.gov\&sz=32)

Department of Justice+2



Fiat Anchor analysis: The authority established a recognizable unity around a collection of activities and gave it a name implying a specialized extension of ordinary interrogation.

The relevant epistemic question is not only whether each activity was legal or effective. It is whether the public understood which activities belonged to the named collective.

The language itself could help obscure the nature of its constituents.

This is a strong example of misleading conceptual packaging: the underlying practices were real, but the chosen unity and its framing could obstruct accurate public understanding.

### 6. “Clean Diesel” — an apparently meaningful collective supported by manipulated evidence

Documented deliberate deception; borderline Fiat Anchor case

Volkswagen promoted certain vehicles as Clean Diesel while installing software designed to detect emissions testing and alter engine behavior during tests. The U.S. Department of Justice documented the deception, and Volkswagen agreed to criminal and civil penalties.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.justice.gov\&sz=32)

United States Department of Justice+1



Fiat Anchor analysis: The label created a recognizable collective of vehicles purportedly possessing a particular environmental characteristic.

The underlying vehicles were material objects, so this is less clearly a Fiat Anchor under our narrow definition than a country, team, or conceptualized social class.

Nevertheless, the Clean Diesel category illustrates how an authoritative classification can make a selected collection appear to possess a property that its constituents do not actually demonstrate.

It is especially interesting because the evidence used to justify the collective was itself manipulated.

This may be best treated as a related example of deceptive Artifice, rather than a canonical example of Fiat Anchor.

### 7. “Carbon Neutral” and “Net Zero” — boundaries that determine what gets counted

Selective inclusion, exclusion, and accounting boundaries

Environmental claims provide a particularly useful modern test case.

When a company claims to be carbon neutral, the result depends on choices about accounting boundaries, emissions included, time periods, and the treatment of offsets.

In December 2023, the UK's Advertising Standards Authority found that a Charles Tyrwhitt carbon-neutral advertising claim was misleading because material information about the basis for the claim had been omitted. More broadly, the United Nations issued guidance in 2022 addressing misleading net-zero commitments.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.asa.org.uk\&sz=32)

CAP+1



Fiat Anchor analysis: The company's carbon footprint is a constructed collective accounting unity that attempts to represent real environmental consequences.

Choosing its boundaries is not inherently dishonest. Some boundaries are necessary for consistent measurement.

The potential deception occurs when a limited accounting convention is presented as an exhaustive picture of the company's environmental impact.

A company could report a technically accurate result for one selected scope while leaving the public with an unwarranted impression about the whole.

The anchor may remain stable while the public misunderstands how much of reality is actually attached to its chain.

This is a particularly promising example because it demonstrates how Fiat Anchors can remain useful without becoming automatically trustworthy.

### 8. “The Unemployed” — a legitimate anchor that can be deceptively overextended

A potential misleading application, not evidence of deliberate government deception

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics uses a specific operational definition of unemployment. Its official U-3 measure excludes discouraged workers who have not actively searched for work recently; broader measures such as U-4 and U-6 capture additional forms of labor underutilization. The agency publishes these distinctions openly.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.bls.gov\&sz=32)

Bureau of Labor Statistics



Fiat Anchor analysis: The unemployed is a chosen statistical collective with defined membership rules.

Those rules can be consistently applied and genuinely useful.

But imagine an official claiming:

> Everyone who wants work but cannot find it is counted in our unemployment figure.

That would misrepresent the official measure.

Or imagine a public speaker using a falling unemployment rate to suggest that all employment hardship had diminished, without acknowledging the experiences outside the chosen measurement.

The difficulty here is not necessarily a deceptive anchor. It is a deceptive claim about the scope of an otherwise legitimate anchor.

I would retain this as a conceptual example precisely because it shows that the problem does not require a corrupt institution or intentionally manipulated definition.

## An additional insight: the authority can manipulate more than the anchor

I think your ship metaphor allows us to distinguish four separate ways the public can be misled.

Conceptual illustration of a chosen unity, its anchor, and its permitted movement.

The authority might:

1. Misplace the anchor: Establish a definition whose boundaries already serve a concealed purpose.
2. Lengthen or shorten the chain: Change which constituents qualify without making the change recognizable.
3. Misrepresent the anchor's location or chain length: Keep the actual definition unchanged but give the public a false account of its scope.
4. Claim the harbor itself demands this anchor: Present a chosen classification as naturally inevitable, scientifically established, or universally accepted.

The fourth mechanism is especially important to Openness.

An authority need not manipulate the data or change any actual members to mislead people. It might simply conceal that a particular conceptual unity was established through choosing.

## What Epistemic Remembering could recover

For each example, Epistemic Groundedness could make the following metadata explicit:

| Question                                            | What it reveals                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who established the anchor?                         | Provenance and situated authority                              |
| What was the original identifying meaning?          | Initial conceptual boundaries                                  |
| Which constituents were included and excluded?      | The actual chosen unity                                        |
| What changed over time?                             | Boundary or membership drift                                   |
| Who authorized those changes?                       | Anchor Stewardship and its legitimacy                          |
| What was communicated to the public?                | Differences between actual and represented meaning             |
| What did the authority claim the unity represented? | Possible false naturalization or exaggerated scope             |
| What does renewed encounter reveal?                 | Discrepancies between Artifice, evidence, and relevant reality |

This gives us a possible diagnostic approach without immediately creating another named Method.

### My recommendation

For the eventual Fiat Anchor definition, I would select three contrasting examples:

The Soviet “Kulak” classification for concealed membership drift; residential security classifications for biased boundary placement disguised as neutral assessment; and carbon neutrality for selective aggregation and potentially misleading claims about scope.

Together, they illuminate three different vulnerabilities without implying that all Fiat Anchors are deceptive.

I would place the Nazi racial classifications in Artifice or The Intersubjective as an especially consequential example of a socially imposed classification falsely presented as natural reality.

There is also an important qualification for our definition of Fiat Anchor: the chosen unity may be deliberately misleading from its beginning, or may become misleading through subsequent use, without every instance of conceptual ambiguity being deceptive.

That suggests a useful distinction between Fiat Anchor itself and what we might descriptively call Anchor Deception—not yet another separately reified concept.

The guiding insight might be:

An authority may not need to falsify what exists. It may only need to choose what counts as one thing, conceal the choice, and persuade others that the boundary was never chosen at all.

This seems very close to the epistemic vulnerability that Fiat Form, Fiat Anchor, Artifice, and Epistemic Groundedness are intended to make recognizable.

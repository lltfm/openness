I think “contingently chosen” is philosophically accurate, but it may sound as though the choice was necessarily reasonable, constrained, or historically contingent rather than potentially wholly arbitrary.

We want wording that remains hospitable to the full range: a Fiat Anchor might be carefully justified, loosely motivated, partly arbitrary, or entirely arbitrary. Its chosen character does not determine its adequacy or correspondence with reality.

## Candidate phrasings

| Phrase                                                 | Assessment                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| chosen rather than uniquely determined                 | Best for brevity. Leaves the degree of arbitrariness open without implying justification.                            |
| chosen with varying degrees of constraint              | Especially precise; accommodates everything from strongly constrained to entirely arbitrary choices.                 |
| chosen with some freedom of delineation                | Elegant and intuitive, but some freedom understates potentially complete arbitrariness.                              |
| chosen rather than inherently given                    | Accessible and evocative; emphasizes Epistemic Remembering. Slightly less precise about genuine patterns in reality. |

## My top recommendation

I favor “chosen rather than uniquely determined” because it says exactly what we need without adding an assumption about why the choice was made.

It allows a category's boundaries to be strongly constrained by evidence—or selected entirely for convenience, imagination, or preference.

### Recommended revision

**Fiat Anchor** — the canonical name for a *named, immaterial conceptual unity* whose identifying boundaries or membership are chosen rather than uniquely determined by a separately distinguishable material whole. It is a distinctive result of **Fiat Form**, not a synonym for any definition, noun, or abstraction.

This is almost identical to your original, but I think it reads more naturally.

## An alternative worth serious consideration

If we want to emphasize the degree of freedom in choosing rather than merely the absence of unique determination:

> Fiat Anchor — the canonical name for a named, immaterial conceptual unity whose identifying boundaries or membership are chosen with varying degrees of constraint, rather than uniquely fixed by a separately distinguishable material whole.

I particularly like “with varying degrees of constraint” because it makes the entire spectrum explicit:

- Highly constrained: A classification carefully developed to correspond with observed natural patterns.
- Partially arbitrary: A geographical boundary selected among several reasonable alternatives.
- Wholly arbitrary: A fictional category established entirely through imaginative choice.

All three remain Fiat Anchors. Their degree of arbitrariness is a matter for Epistemic Groundedness and Discernment, not a condition determining whether they qualify.

### One important nuance

I would preserve the word “uniquely”.

Without it, saying that boundaries are chosen rather than determined by reality risks implying that conceptual categories cannot correspond exactly to independently existing distinctions.

But Openness should leave that possibility open.

A chosen category might happen to capture something about reality perfectly. Its chosen origin does not preclude that.

The criterion is not that reality could not determine the same boundaries, but that the act of Fiat Anchoring does not itself establish that reality uniquely requires them.

Final recommendation: Use “chosen rather than uniquely determined” in the concise definition. Explain the spectrum from well-justified to wholly arbitrary choices in The Definition Unfolded, where it can receive proper philosophical treatment without burdening the anchor.

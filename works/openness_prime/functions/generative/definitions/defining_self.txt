Self

Abbreviated Symbolic Anchor:

Self — At least this: the self who says “I,” encounters experience, and recognizes its own not-knowing. But perhaps more?

Foundational Definition

Self is the first-person referent to whom experiences, interpretations, commitments, identity, and apparently deliberate choices are attributed as one's own.

Self is the reflexive participant in inquiry: the one who may encounter experience, examine understanding, question identity, and reconsider choosing.

Its ultimate nature, constitution, agency, and boundaries remain unresolved.

Essential Distinctions:

- Self is not a fixed metaphysical substance by definition. Its ultimate nature and continuity remain open to inquiry.
- Self is not identical to its present self-conception. What Self understands itself to be may change.
- Attribution is not proof of origin. An experience recognized as one's own need not have causes belonging exclusively to Self.
- Apparently deliberate choosing does not establish a particular theory of Free Will.

Function in the Generative Engine:

Self identifies the reflexive participant who conducts inquiry, forms and revises concepts, examines commitments, and makes The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing.

It allows the Engine to investigate the inquirer without presupposing a final theory of selfhood.


Example:

Self recognizes a familiar conviction and questions its assumptions. The conviction may change without requiring that Self's identity was ever fixed.

Boundary case:

Defining Self as necessarily an eternal consciousness, or reducing it by definition to one particular psychological theory, would introduce commitments the foundational concept leaves unresolved.

The Saltem Hoc of Self:

At least this: There is the one to whom experience is attributed as one's own.

A feeling arises, and Self says, “I feel.”

A thought appears, and Self says, “I think.”

An uncertainty becomes recognizable, and Self confesses:

For I Know I Do Not Know.

We call this first-person referent Self.

But also perhaps this: Self is not exhausted by what it presently believes itself to be.

Memories, commitments, habits, and identities may contribute to Self's familiar understanding.

Yet that understanding may change.

An unfamiliar possibility may become recognizable. A cherished conviction may be revised. A new capacity may emerge.

Perhaps Self is not merely what it has been, but also what it may become.

Maybe this: Self participates in its own re-formation.

Through inquiry, a previously unrecognized assumption may become visible. Through choosing, a familiar pattern may change. Through Logonesis, Self may acquire concepts that alter its understanding of experience.

Perhaps what Self creates can change the capacities through which Self subsequently creates.

This possibility belongs to Recursive Becoming without establishing any particular metaphysical account of Free Will.

Or perhaps even this: What if the boundaries of Self are less certain than they appear?

Experiences recognized as one's own may arise through influences not fully understood.

Our language, relationships, and circumstances contribute to the lives we call ours.

Perhaps Self's boundaries are adequately explained through its embodied individuality. Perhaps its relationships with the wider world are more fundamental than its familiar identity suggests.

Spiritual Openness may contemplate still further possibilities of unity or transcendence.

None follows merely from saying “I.”

And perhaps this: Self may be both the subject of inquiry and the one who conducts it.

Self can question its understanding.

It can discover that an earlier conviction was mistaken.

It can examine even the assumptions through which it previously understood itself.

And perhaps, through these inquiries, Self becomes capable of recognizing possibilities that were unavailable before.

The Self-Confession may then be renewed—not because the earlier confession failed, but because understanding continues to change.

At least this, Self: there is the self who asks what Self may be.

But perhaps more than Self presently understands?

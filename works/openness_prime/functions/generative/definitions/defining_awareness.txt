# Awareness

Abbreviated Symbolic Anchor:

Awareness — At least this: the embodied first-person condition in which all experience becomes present. But perhaps more?

Foundational Definition:

Awareness is the embodied first-person condition in which experiences become present.

It is through Awareness that all feelings, thoughts, sensations, perceptions, memories, and other phenomena are encountered.

The ultimate nature, origins, and metaphysical relationship of Awareness to Self and the wider reality remain unresolved.

Essential Distinctions:

- Experience is not explanation. Recognizing an experience does not establish its origins or ultimate nature.
- Awareness is not necessarily reflective self-knowledge. An experience may occur without Self fully understanding or articulating it.
- Awareness is not defined as a separate observing entity. Distinguishing Awareness from its contents does not establish metaphysical separation.
- Embodied experience does not settle the metaphysics of consciousness. Its physical relationships may be investigated without presupposing an exhaustive account.

Function in the Generative Engine:

Awareness provides the first-person experiential condition through which inquiry begins, meanings become recognizable, and Self encounters the limits of its understanding.

It allows the Engine to distinguish experienced phenomena from interpretations and explanations of them.

Example:

An unfamiliar feeling arises within Awareness. Self recognizes it before understanding its cause or finding words to describe it.

Boundary case:

Concluding that Awareness must exist independently of the body because it can be distinguished conceptually from a thought exceeds what the definition establishes.

The Saltem Hoc of Awareness:

At least this: Experience becomes present.

A sound is heard. A feeling arises. A thought appears. A sensation changes.

Before Self explains these experiences, they are encountered.

We call the first-person condition of their appearing Awareness.

But also perhaps this: Awareness allows Self to recognize an experience without fully identifying with its apparent demands.

Anger may arise without requiring action. Fear may become recognizable without revealing whether its apparent danger is real.

Perhaps the distinction between Awareness and what appears within it makes new relationships to experience possible.

Yet the distinction need not establish two separate metaphysical things.

Maybe this: Awareness may participate in the transformation of understanding.

What once passed unnoticed may become recognizable. A familiar feeling may be encountered differently. A previously inexpressible experience may acquire conceptual form through Logonesis.

Perhaps Transformative Inquiry changes not only how Self explains experience, but what Self becomes capable of noticing.

Or perhaps even this: What is the ultimate nature of Awareness?

Perhaps its occurrence is fully explainable through embodied physical processes. Perhaps our present explanations remain incomplete. Perhaps there are further relationships between Awareness and reality that Self has not yet conceived.

Spiritual Openness may contemplate forms of Awareness beyond ordinary embodiment, but their possibility is not established by the experience of being aware.

And perhaps this: Awareness may be more difficult to define exhaustively than the experiences through which we recognize it.

We encounter changing contents, yet the nature of their appearing remains a subject of inquiry.

Perhaps the distinction between Awareness and experience will itself acquire greater precision.

Or perhaps future understanding will transform the distinction.

At least this, Self: experience is present within Awareness.

But what more might Awareness be?

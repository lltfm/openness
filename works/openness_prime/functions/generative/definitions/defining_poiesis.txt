Poiesis

Foundational Definition:

Poiesis is the creative bringing-into-being of forms, meanings, concepts, expressions, and possibilities that were not previously available in their present form.

It includes both the creation of something substantially new and the transformation, recombination, or articulation of what already exists.

Poiesis may emerge through deliberate imagination, inquiry, unexpected discovery, or the interaction of previously unconnected ideas.

Its significance is not merely that something is produced, but that creation may make further meanings and possibilities available.

Essential Distinctions:

- Creation is not necessarily creation from nothing. Poiesis may transform existing materials, concepts, and relationships.
- Creation is not proof of truth. A newly formed concept does not establish that its proposed referent independently exists.
- Novelty is not necessarily value. What is created may be fruitful, harmful, trivial, or mistaken.
- Poiesis is not limited to art. It may occur through philosophical inquiry, conceptual clarification, scientific imagination, or ordinary expression.
- Creation may exceed intention. The resulting meaning or possibility need not have been fully anticipated by its creator.

Function in the Generative Engine:

Poiesis identifies the creative character and purpose of the Engine's generative activity.

The Resonating Chamber provides methods through which Poiesis may occur. Logonesis is a particular expression of Poiesis in which emerging meanings acquire conceptual form through language.

Poiesis also supports Recursive Becoming: what is created may transform the possibilities available for subsequent creation.

Example:

Self recognizes a relationship between uncertainty and clarity that has not yet acquired a recognizable conceptual form.

Through inquiry and language, Self develops the concept of Precise Ambiguity.

The concept makes a previously underarticulated distinction available for communication and further investigation.

Its creative bringing-into-being is an instance of Poiesis.

Boundary Case:

Inventing an elaborate name for an imagined phenomenon does not establish that the phenomenon exists.

Nor does novelty alone demonstrate that the creation contributes meaningfully to understanding.

Poiesis names the creative bringing-into-being of possibilities, not their automatic truth or value.

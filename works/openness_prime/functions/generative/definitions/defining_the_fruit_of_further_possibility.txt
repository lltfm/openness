The Fruit of Further Possibility

Foundational Definition:

The Fruit of Further Possibility is the transformation of what may subsequently become possible through engagement with an earlier possibility.

It includes further possibilities that are enabled, discovered, created, clarified, transformed, obscured, restricted, or foreclosed.

This fruit may concern future understanding, imagination, inquiry, choosing, action, relationships, or the capacities through which additional possibilities become recognizable or achievable.

Its significance is that the fruit of a possibility may include changes to the very conditions of future possibility.

Essential Distinctions:

- Further possibility is a particular dimension of fruit. The Fruit of a Possibility concerns consequences broadly; The Fruit of Further Possibility focuses on how those consequences affect subsequent possibilities.
- Further possibilities need not be entirely new. An existing possibility may become more recognizable, accessible, meaningful, or achievable.
- Generativity is not necessarily expansion. Engaging with one possibility may close others, narrow attention, or reduce future capacities.
- More possibilities are not necessarily better. Their value depends upon their nature, consequences, accessibility, and relationship to other possibilities.
- Potential is not inevitability. Making something possible does not guarantee that it will occur.
- Future fruit may remain unknown. A possibility may enable developments that neither its creator nor its audience could presently anticipate.

Function in the Generative Engine:

The Fruit of Further Possibility directs the Engine toward the consequences of generation for future generativity.

It gives particular significance to Recursive Becoming: concepts, experiences, and capacities developed through earlier inquiry may alter the conditions through which subsequent inquiry occurs.

Through Poiesis, new conceptual forms may become available. Through Transcendence, previous limitations may be surpassed. Through Transformative Inquiry, the capacity to recognize and investigate possibilities may itself change.

The Fruit of Further Possibility examines what these developments make available—and what they may inadvertently obscure or foreclose.

Example:

Self considers the symbolic interpretation of Cain as the present Self and Abel as the future Self.

The immediate fruit may be a changed understanding of responsibility.

But this understanding may also make further inquiries possible:

What does the future Self owe the present Self?

Can preparation for tomorrow become a form of domination over today?

How do responsibilities change when a future Self cannot be fully imagined?

What happens when the relationship is extended across generations?

The original interpretation has not merely produced an insight. It has made new questions, distinctions, and conceptual possibilities available.

Yet it might also narrow future thinking if Self comes to interpret every present pleasure as a betrayal of future responsibility.

Both the opening and closing of subsequent possibilities belong to The Fruit of Further Possibility.

Boundary Case:

A contemplation inspires the creation of many new concepts, and Self concludes that it must therefore have produced valuable further fruit.

But the new concepts may be redundant, misleading, or so elaborate that they obstruct clearer inquiry.

The multiplication of possibilities does not by itself establish improvement.

The Fruit of Further Possibility concerns how the landscape of what may come next is changed—not merely how many possibilities are produced.

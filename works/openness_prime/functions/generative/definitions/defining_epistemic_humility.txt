Epistemic Humility

Foundational Definition:

Epistemic Humility is the continuing discipline of recognizing the limits and fallibility of one's understanding, proportioning confidence to available justification, and remaining genuinely responsive to inquiry, evidence, and correction.

It is the practical expression of the commitment made through The Self-Confession of Not-Knowing.

Its purpose is not to diminish knowledge, but to cultivate a more honest and revisable relationship to what Self understands.

Essential Distinctions:

- Humility is not universal doubt. Well-supported conclusions may warrant strong confidence.
- Openness is not equivalence. Considering an alternative does not require assigning it equal credibility.
- Revisability is not indecision. Self may choose or act decisively while acknowledging uncertainty.
- Expressed humility is not practiced humility. A confession of fallibility must remain open to correction in practice.

Function in the Generative Engine:

Governs the Engine's relationship to certainty, inquiry, and revision.

Epistemic Humility prevents the products of generation from becoming unquestionable merely because they are coherent, resonant, beautiful, or fruitful.

It sustains the possibility of correcting established concepts and developing further understanding.

Example:

Self favors one explanation because substantial evidence supports it.

Self maintains that preference while remaining willing to reconsider if credible new evidence or a better interpretation emerges.

Boundary Case:

Refusing to favor a strongly supported conclusion because absolute certainty is unavailable is not required by Epistemic Humility.

Likewise, claiming to be humble while systematically dismissing relevant objections contradicts the discipline.

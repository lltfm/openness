Saltem Hoc

Foundational Definition:

Saltem Hoc (Latin: "at least this") is a generative method of inquiry and expression that begins with a minimally defensible meaning, observation, distinction, or explicitly hypothetical premise, then explores further possibilities without presenting them as established truth.

Its characteristic movement is:

At least this … But also perhaps this … Maybe this … Or perhaps even this …

The anchor establishes what can responsibly be asserted or stipulated. Subsequent movements explore what might also be understood, imagined, or discovered.

The sequence is flexible; its purpose is anchored exploration, not adherence to a fixed literary formula.

Saltem Hoc may also employ Resonant Questions within its anchor, exploratory movements, or conclusion. Such questions may introduce new dimensions, invite the reader to develop possible answers, or leave further inquiry open beyond the written contemplation.

Creating Resonant Questions is a distinct, identified but unreified Generative Method that may be combined with Saltem Hoc; neither method requires the other.

Essential Distinctions:

- An anchor need not be absolutely certain, but its epistemic status must be clear.
- Further possibilities need not be equally plausible.
- Exploring a possibility does not establish its truth.
- A Saltem Hoc may revise its initial understanding without pretending that the original anchor was final.

Function in the Generative Engine:

Saltem Hoc gives emerging meanings a structured but open generative movement, supporting Dimensionalization, Logonesis, and Transformative Inquiry.

Example and Boundary Case:

Example: “At least this: Self recognizes an unfamiliar feeling within Awareness. But perhaps its significance extends beyond what Self presently understands?”

Boundary case: Beginning with an unsupported metaphysical claim presented as certain is not an epistemically honest Saltem Hoc, even if speculative movements follow.

The Saltem Hoc of Saltem Hoc

At least this: Saltem Hoc begins with something we can responsibly recognize and asks what more might be possible.

We establish a meaning sufficiently clear to investigate without assuming it exhausts its subject.

We begin somewhere.

But we need not remain there.

But also perhaps this: Saltem Hoc does more than explore existing possibilities. It may help us discover possibilities we had not previously conceived.

A question reveals a distinction. A distinction suggests a relationship. A relationship invites another question.

What began as an explanation becomes a doorway into further inquiry.

Maybe this: Saltem Hoc is itself an instrument of Logonesis.

Through language, an emerging meaning acquires form. Through further contemplation, that form may develop into new concepts.

Perhaps Saltem Hoc participates in Poiesis by helping bring into being meanings that were previously unavailable for inquiry.

Or perhaps even this: Practicing Saltem Hoc may transform the inquirer.

Self may become more capable of recognizing assumptions, imagining alternatives, and distinguishing what is known from what remains uncertain.

The method may therefore contribute to Recursive Becoming: inquiry changes the capacities through which further inquiry becomes possible.

Yet these fruits are not guaranteed. Fruitional Discernment remains necessary.

And perhaps this: Saltem Hoc may eventually reveal limitations in its own method.

A more precise beginning may become available. Another form of inquiry may prove more generative. Its familiar movements may require revision.

Even this definition remains open to improvement.

For Saltem Hoc does not seek to make every meaning final.

It seeks to begin responsibly and discover what further understanding may become possible.

At least this, Self.

But perhaps more?

# Saltem Hoc

## Foundational Definition

**Saltem Hoc** (Latin: *at least this*) is a reified **Generative Method** of inquiry and expression that begins with a minimally defensible meaning, observation, or distinction—or a premise clearly identified as hypothetical—and explores further possibilities while preserving the epistemic status of the beginning and of what follows.

Its characteristic invitation is:

*At least this … But also perhaps this … Maybe this … Or perhaps even this …*

These movements are flexible rather than compulsory. The Method seeks responsible beginnings that make additional inquiry, interpretation, and creation possible—not a prescribed sequence of escalating claims.

## The Definition Unfolded

A **Saltem Hoc** begins where meaning can be made recognizable. Its anchor may be something genuinely experienced, a responsibly drawn distinction, a well-supported conclusion, or an explicitly stipulated premise suitable for imaginative exploration. A hypothesis can serve as an anchor *as a hypothesis*; it must not be smuggled into the work as a discovered fact.

From this beginning, the writer may ask what else might be consistent with it, how another perspective might illuminate it, whether another distinction becomes available, and what further questions emerge. The progression may use **Resonant Questions**, metaphor, analogy, **The Lens of Openness**, **Dimensionalization**, **Logonesis**, or a return to the original question through **Transformative Inquiry**.

A **Saltem Hoc** need not proceed from modest possibility to extraordinary possibility. The next movement may narrow, clarify, qualify, or even confirm what is already understood. **Brevity Without Loss of Essential Meaning** may be served by a compact passage; an extended contemplation may be warranted when conceptual safeguards need explanation.

**Anchored Ambiguity** and **Saltem Hoc** are related but distinct. **Anchored Ambiguity** is the deliberate establishment of a minimally recognizable meaning while preserving further qualifying possibilities. **Saltem Hoc** is the practice of moving through possibilities from a responsible beginning. A **Saltem Hoc** may employ **Anchored Ambiguity**, but not every use must reify a separate ambiguous concept or preserve unresolved possibilities after evidence settles a question.

An important restriction remains: **exploring beyond an anchor does not mean silently changing what the anchor asserts**. In a definitional **Anchored Ambiguity**, a purported further interpretation must preserve the **Foundational Definition**. A possibility incompatible with that anchor can still inspire a new hypothesis, a different concept, or an explicit proposal to revise the anchor, but cannot be presented as a qualifying interpretation of the unchanged definition.

### Characteristic Movements

**At least this.** Articulate what is responsibly encountered, distinguished, understood, or expressly stipulated. Make clear whether the beginning is evidential, conceptual, fictional, or hypothetical.

**But also perhaps this.** Consider another compatible meaning, relationship, question, or perspective. Invite exploration without converting the possibility into a fact.

**Maybe this; or perhaps even this.** Extend inquiry through additional possibilities, **Analogical Projection**, imaginative inhabitation, or transformation of the question. No escalation, increased improbability, or greater spiritual significance is required.

**Returning and reconsidering.** Ask what remains justified, what requires examination, and what fruit may have followed. Reaffirm, clarify, or explicitly revise the beginning if warranted rather than assuming it must be discarded.

These are descriptive movements, not mandatory steps, and their ordering may vary.

## Essential Distinctions

**Saltem Hoc versus Anchored Ambiguity.** **Saltem Hoc** is a Method; **Anchored Ambiguity** is a way of establishing an identifiable beginning with compatible interpretive openness. The latter may inform the former without becoming synonymous with it.

**Defensible anchor versus hypothetical anchor.** A premise may be used for inquiry because it is declared as imagined, not because its underlying claim has been established. Its qualifying epistemic status must remain visible.

**Compatible elaboration versus changing the premise.** Further interpretation of an established anchor must fit that anchor. An incompatible alternative can be investigated separately or used to motivate explicit revision, not admitted silently.

**Possibility versus evidential warrant.** The Method makes possibilities available without granting them identical plausibility or claiming that a new possibility is true.

**Speculation versus certainty.** A well-supported conclusion may remain well supported even as further questions open; the Method is not a requirement to manufacture doubt.

**Saltem Hoc versus Resonant Question.** A **Resonant Question** is an expressive question; **Creating Resonant Questions** is an identified but unreified Method. Neither is a compulsory component of **Saltem Hoc**.

**Saltem Hoc versus Epitome.** **Epitome** names a concise expressive form; **Saltem Hoc** names a generative practice. A compact **Saltem Hoc** can be an **Epitome**, and an **Epitome** may be created without this Method.

**Saltem Hoc versus Transformative Inquiry.** **Transformative Inquiry** is a broader approach that may revise its own initial questions and assumptions. **Saltem Hoc** is one usable Method within such inquiry, not its sole means.

**Projection versus discovery.** **Analogical Projection** may suggest a correspondence; it does not itself establish that the projected feature exists in the target. **Analogical Discernment** remains relevant.

**Resonance versus fruit versus truth.** A poetic movement may resonate, change a reader's outlook, or generate new possibilities without proving its metaphysical premise. **Discernment** and **Fruitional Discernment** may examine the differing claims.

**Invitation versus persuasion by pressure.** A contemplation may advocate or preferentially inhabit a view without covertly engineering assent. **Nonimposition** guards the audience's freedom to disagree or step back.

## Function in the Generative Engine

Within the **Resonating Chamber**, **Saltem Hoc** provides a recognizable flexible movement for exploring what can emerge from a clear beginning. It can cooperate with **The Lens of Openness**, **Dimensionalization**, **Precise Ambiguity**, **Logonesis**, **Resonant Questions**, and **Transformative Inquiry** without enforcing a single order.

The **Constitution** calls for **Self-Honesty**, **Epistemic Humility**, **Epistemic Hospitality**, and **Nonimposition** in representing the starting point and subsequent possibilities. **The Orientation** includes **The Marriage of Truth and Beauty** and **Fruitional Discernment**, helping distinguish a work's expressive power from consequences and justification. These relationships are cross-references, not instructions to add each as another separately reified Method.

## Naming and Usage

**Saltem Hoc** is the canonical name of the reified Method. *At least this* is its English gloss and a characteristic beginning, not compulsory literal wording in every application. **The Saltem Hoc of Saltem Hoc** below is an illustrative **Transformative Art** contemplation, not a competing **Foundational Definition** or evidence of guaranteed fruit.

## Examples

### Example — A Recognizable Feeling

A writer begins: “At least this: a feeling has become recognizable within **My Awareness**; its cause is not yet established.” Further movements consider memory, circumstances, bodily processes, and spiritual interpretation as possibilities with potentially different evidential support. Nothing in the initial encounter independently proves any particular cause.

### Example — A Declared Symbolic Premise

A contemplation announces that it will *imagine* Cain as an image of **The Self** in the present and Abel as a possible future expression of **The Self**. From that stipulated reading it asks how present decisions may affect future living. The fruit of the interpretation can be considered without asserting that it was the original meaning of the Biblical narrative.

### Example — Returning to a Sound Beginning

An observation about pigment under different light is sufficiently explained by a well-supported account. A **Saltem Hoc** explores further implications for artistic practice without implying that the established explanation must be false because another question is possible.

### Example — A Disguised Unsupported Premise (negative boundary)

A contemplation opens “At least this: an unseen being caused this unfamiliar feeling,” despite having no supporting evidence and without labeling the premise hypothetical. The imaginative movements that follow do not make the anchor epistemically responsible. It may be repaired by explicitly identifying the speculation as a hypothetical narrative.

## Boundary

**Saltem Hoc** is not automatic progression toward increasingly grand claims, a demand to continue speculation forever, or a license to treat poetic resonance as evidence. When a beginning is established, further qualifying interpretations must respect its meaning; when the beginning is hypothetical, its status must not be concealed. An inquiry may end by retaining the original understanding, correcting it, developing another concept, or leaving a question open. No particular fruit is guaranteed.

## Illustrative Contemplation — The Saltem Hoc of Saltem Hoc

*At least this:* **Saltem Hoc** begins with something we can responsibly recognize and asks what more might be possible.

We establish a meaning sufficiently clear to investigate without assuming it exhausts its subject.

We begin somewhere.

But we need not remain there.

*But also perhaps this:* **Saltem Hoc** does more than explore existing possibilities. It may help us discover possibilities we had not previously conceived.

A question reveals a distinction. A distinction suggests a relationship. A relationship invites another question.

What began as an explanation becomes a doorway into further inquiry.

*Maybe this:* **Saltem Hoc** is itself an instrument of **Logonesis**.

Through language, an emerging meaning acquires form. Through further contemplation, that form may develop into new concepts.

Perhaps **Saltem Hoc** participates in **Poiesis** by helping bring into being meanings that were previously unavailable for inquiry.

*Or perhaps even this:* Practicing **Saltem Hoc** may transform the inquirer.

Self may become more capable of recognizing assumptions, imagining alternatives, and distinguishing what is known from what remains uncertain.

The Method may therefore contribute to **Recursive Becoming**: inquiry changes the capacities through which further inquiry becomes possible.

Yet these fruits are not guaranteed. **Fruitional Discernment** remains necessary.

*And perhaps this:* **Saltem Hoc** may eventually reveal limitations in its own Method.

A more precise beginning may become available. Another form of inquiry may prove more generative. Its familiar movements may require revision.

Even this definition remains open to improvement.

For **Saltem Hoc** does not seek to make every meaning final.

It seeks to begin responsibly and discover what further understanding may become possible.

At least this, Self.

But perhaps more?

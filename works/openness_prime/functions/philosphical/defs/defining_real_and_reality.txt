Defining Real and Reality

Foundational Definition:

In Openness, Real and Reality are perspective-qualified concepts. Real concerns what is encountered or proposed as present, occurring, or existing from a specified standpoint. Reality concerns the corresponding field, world, or account in which such encounters and referents are understood. Neither term, by itself, means physically existing.

A dream may be experienced as a world; a novel may invite an imaginatively inhabited world; a stone may be encountered through perception and investigated as a physical object. How each is described as Real depends partly on the perspective and domain of the claim. Whether an encounter establishes a corresponding physical referent is a further question, not something settled by the word Real.

Openness provisionally distinguishes three perspectives:

Own Self Real / Own Self Reality. What is encountered or taken as present within one's own Awareness, and the world as experienced or imaginatively inhabited from that first-person standpoint. The encounter may include waking perception, dreaming, memory, imagination, and responses to stories or films. Recognition of what appears does not by itself determine its origins, physical status, or ultimate nature. Optional shorthand: self-real / self-reality.

Imaginal Other Self Real / Imaginal Other Self Reality. What is projected as present or experienced from another proposed Self's standpoint, and the reality imagined or inferred for that Self. Another person's Awareness may be strongly justified through Analogical Discernment, yet its contents are not thereby encountered as one's own. The same projection can be explored toward animals, groups, machines, fictional characters, or other possible Selves; what each application warrants differs. The term Imaginal qualifies the projected perspective, not automatically the existence or Awareness of its referent. Optional shorthand: other-self-real / other-self-reality.

Imaginal Objective Real / Imaginal Objective Reality. What is taken or proposed to be present, occurring, or existing in an account imagined as if it were independent of any particular Self's perspective. This standpoint can organize observations, comparisons, tests, physical claims, and shared accounts, without claiming that anyone actually occupies a free-floating view beyond Awareness. Its objects and claims are not, by definition, limited to concrete physical things; their status depends upon the account's intended domain and justification. Optional shorthand: objectively-real / objectively-reality, or, outside formal definitions, unquoted real / reality.

The three perspectives are not three independently established worlds. They distinguish how an assertion is situated, not what its ultimate explanation must be. A claim may concern more than one perspective; their relationships may become subjects of further inquiry.

Essential Distinctions

Perspective versus physical status. The standpoint from which something is encountered or described does not determine whether a corresponding physical object exists. A stone may be physically present; a dream mountain may be present within a dream without being a physical mountain. Neither conclusion follows merely from selecting a perspective.

Encounter versus interpretation. An experience may occur without its appearance, explanation, or apparent object being understood. Within Own Self Reality, a dreamed mountain can be encountered as a mountain. Later inquiry may distinguish the dream experience from a mountain proposed to exist physically. The earlier encounter need not be denied to revise its interpretation.

Imagined standpoint versus imagined referent. Imaginal Other Self Reality and Imaginal Objective Reality name perspectives developed through imagination and inference. Another person or a stone need not be imaginary because the standpoint from which its reality is described is imaginatively constructed.

Other Self versus Own Self. The presence of experiencing within one's own Awareness differs from recognizing or imagining another's experience. Analogical Projection makes the other standpoint conceivable; Analogical Discernment examines whether and in what sense it is justified. A group or machine described as a Self does not thereby acquire Awareness.

Objective aspiration versus objective possession. Imaginal Objective Reality permits disciplined attempts to correct individual limitations, reconcile observations, and investigate independent physical existence. It does not claim that an actual observer outside all perspectives exists, or that a successful account is exhaustive.

Physical forms versus immaterial forms. A body, sign, or computer may be physically encountered. Team A is a conceptual identity that can be formed, adopted, represented, and shared among Selves; no additional concrete object called Team A appears. An immaterial form may shape physical actions without thereby becoming a separate physical object.

Shared agreement versus independent justification. Intersubjective recognition can constitute conventional identities and meanings, but agreement alone does not demonstrate that a claimed physical object exists or establish what another Self experiences. Evidence appropriate to each claim remains relevant.

Quoted “real” / “reality” versus qualified terms. Quotation marks may deliberately preserve a Precise Ambiguity about perspective: Own Self, Imaginal Other Self, Imaginal Objective, several of these, or another distinction not yet conceived. Their use invites contemplation and Dimensionalization, not an assumption that the perspectives are equivalent. Ordinary quotations of someone's exact words are a separate use of quotation marks.

Relationship to Openness

These distinctions belong provisionally to Philosophical Openness. They develop Self-Honesty about what is being claimed, who or what supplies the standpoint, and what remains unresolved. Their meanings are Fiat Forms available for examination and reformation, not discoveries of three metaphysically separate realities.

Awareness offers a first-person beginning for encountering experience. Self is the embodied referent through which the encounter may be recognized as one's own. The Mystery concerns what remains unknown; For I Know I Do Not Know permits inquiry to proceed without pretending that a chosen perspective answers every question.

Analogical Projection makes another proposed Self's perspective conceivable. Analogical Discernment—including Default Analogical Discernment where an ordinary application is sufficiently justified—examines what can be recognized beyond the original encounter. The imagined objective standpoint supports investigation across perspectives without erasing the fact that inquiry is conducted from somewhere.

Whether the boundary between waking, dreaming, remembering, and imaginatively inhabited worlds is itself drawn partly through perspective remains open. The vocabulary enables that question without deciding in advance that all such encounters are equivalent or that one presently favored account is final.

Examples

Example — A Stone in the Hand (positive bright-line)

Self sees a stone and feels its weight. The seeing and feeling belong to Own Self Reality. Self imagines what a companion might see and feel: a proposed Imaginal Other Self Reality, whose application may be well justified without direct access to the companion's Awareness.

Measurements, repeated encounters, and corroboration may strongly support the stone's physical existence in Imaginal Objective Reality. The imagined objective standpoint is not an observer beyond every Awareness; nor does its imagined character make the stone imaginary.

Example — A Mountain in a Dream

During a dream, a mountain is encountered as part of a convincing world. It belongs to the dream as experienced within Own Self Reality. On waking, Self may distinguish that encounter from a mountain proposed to exist physically, without denying that the dreamed mountain was encountered as present.

Why one encounter is called waking and another dreaming—and how much that distinction depends upon memory, coherence, inference, or convention—remains open to further inquiry. The definition does not equate every encounter or resolve the question by naming it.

Example — A Film or Novel

Self watches a film or reads a novel and imaginatively enters its events. The encounter, emotion, and imagining take place within Own Self Reality. The story may also support a coherent fictional world, and Self may imagine a character's standpoint through Imaginal Other Self Reality.

The film's images or the book's pages may be physical artifacts. The depicted character is not thereby an additional physical person, and an imaginatively inhabited story does not demonstrate the character possesses Awareness.

Example — Team A (immaterial shared identity)

Five physically existing people adopt the name Team A. They may share expectations, recognize membership, and create signs or records. The identity is an immaterial Fiat Form maintained through the understandings of participants and others, rather than a sixth physical body.

Each member may encounter belonging within Own Self Reality and imagine others' experiences through Imaginal Other Self Reality. A purportedly objective account may describe the people, their records, and the conceptual identity without converting that identity into a separate physical object.

Example — A Question Deliberately Left Open

A contemplation asks: Which “reality” did I inhabit when I believed the story? The quotation marks leave open whether the inquiry concerns the encountered feeling, the imagined standpoint of a character, a shared narrative, an objective claim, or more than one. The ambiguity is intentional and available for subsequent Dimensionalization.

Editorial Convention

Within formal definitions, name the intended perspective: Own Self Real / Reality, Imaginal Other Self Real / Reality, or Imaginal Objective Real / Reality. Their optional short forms are self-real / self-reality, other-self-real / other-self-reality, and objectively-real / objectively-reality. Retain the full form when shortening could confuse readers.

Avoid unqualified, unquoted real and reality in definitional claims. In other prose, these words may serve as shorthand for Imaginal Objective Real / Reality when context warrants that reading; shorthand does not itself establish objectivity or physical existence. If the standpoint matters, state it rather than asking the reader to guess.

Use quoted “real” and “reality” to mark a deliberate Precise Ambiguity when the point is to leave the perspective open for inquiry. An unexplained ambiguity is not automatically precise; the surrounding text should make the deliberate suspension recognizable.

Boundary

These terms do not prove that every apparent object exists independently of experience, that all experiences are equivalent, that the physical world depends upon Awareness, or that an imagined perspective can occupy a literal view from nowhere.

They also do not reduce another Self's Awareness to fiction, turn collective identities into physical bodies, or make justification unnecessary. An encounter, a representation, a projected standpoint, and a proposed explanation may each be distinguished without ruling out further relationships among them.

What is “real” may require asking: real from which perspective, in what sense, and on what grounds?

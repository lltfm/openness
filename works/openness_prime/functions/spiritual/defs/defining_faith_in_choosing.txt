Defining Faith In Choosing

Definition

Faith in Choosing is the freely adopted spiritual preference to inhabit the possibility that choosing—whether ultimately free or only apparently free—can have meaning and can be refined. It remains receptive to the possibility that Awareness possesses freedom not exhausted by prior causes, without requiring a claim that such freedom has been demonstrated.

Discussion

This is a preferred way of living with the question, not a metaphysical conclusion. We may aspire to attend to desires, weigh possibilities, act or refrain, examine what follows, and discover that a later Self responds differently. Even someone who believes every decision is determined can understand deliberation as part of how future actions arise. Conversely, the experience of deliberating does not prove that the decision was uncaused.

The preference is connected to faith in the journey of life: uncertainty about our ultimate freedom does not establish that attempts to respond well are worthless. The possibility that life can be meaningful remains available whether or not ultimate freedom is demonstrated.

Disclosed preference

Treat the experience of choosing as an invitation to cultivate attention, responsibility, and Aspiration to Love, whether the metaphysical freedom of the chooser is real or apparent.

Not claimed

Desires with an origin beyond the material world would not by themselves prove free will; the experience of choosing does not prove that alternatives were metaphysically available; and determinism does not necessarily abolish meaning, care, purpose, or responsibility.

Status

A proposed Spiritual Openness preference. A reader may accept the Philosophical Openness contemplations without adopting it and may develop a different preferred story.

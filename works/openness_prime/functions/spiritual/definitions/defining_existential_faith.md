# Existential Faith

## Prelude

*Morning comes, Self.*

*I wake to a Life that has continued without my having chosen all that sustains it.*

*Today I may make choices, though I cannot know all that they will bring.*

*Night will return. I may sleep. Another morning is not promised.*

*This familiar embodied life will end.*

*What, if anything, becomes of My Awareness beyond its end?*

*I have inherited stories of purpose, duty, liberation, death, and what follows.*

*Yet which story, if any, explains my continuing?*

*What am I choosing when I choose to engage with Life again?*

*For I Know I Do Not Know.*

*Let us look closer.*

## Foundational Definition

What is **Existential Faith**?

**At least this**: an implicit or deliberately chosen **Mental Model** for continuing to engage with Life despite unwelcome experiences, struggle, uncertainty, and the mortality of **My Self**, then generalized for possible application beyond my first-person standpoint.

*But also perhaps more?*

**Existential Faith** belongs primarily to **S — Spiritual Openness**. It is a **Fiat Anchor** with **Anchored Ambiguity** that offers a **Mental Model** for understanding continued engagement with mortal Life, including what operates before conscious recognition. Openness may later choose to inhabit the model; **Faith In The Journey** is its preferentially inhabited trust-oriented form.

### Example Speculations

- Perhaps **Existential Faith** illuminates a previously unrecognized organization of expectations, responses, and orientations through which engagement with Life continues, even when its origin remains mysterious?
- Or perhaps numbness, lost interest, despair, or profound disengagement can sometimes make recognizable a disruption in an earlier operative Existential Faith, inviting inquiry into what sustained engagement and what has changed?
- Or perhaps **Existential Faith** can be lived through duty, relationships, religious devotion, implicit trust, or other modes of participation whose deeper meanings are still unfolding?
- *But also perhaps more?*

## The Definition Unfolded

**Existential Faith offers a Mental Model that may operate before conscious recognition.** As a named Openness concept, it gathers patterns of expectation, interpretation, and response associated with continued engagement with Life. The broader concept **Mental Models** allows such patterns to be partly inferred from living, habitual action, and later recognition: a model need not be verbal, explicit, consciously selected, or recognized while it operates. Openness also deliberately establishes and stewards **Existential Faith** as a **Fiat Anchor**. This second, conceptual choice must not be confused with an imagined first decision by every person to possess the underlying orientation.

**Existential places the inquiry within lived participation in mortal Life.** **My Self** names this familiar embodied life, repeatedly encountered within **My Awareness** through waking, living, and sleeping. I recognize experience, action, remembered continuance, relationships, and the conditions of life I did not choose. The model invites me to consider how I continue engaging in that life, including through activities I did not consciously understand as expressions of faith, before it becomes available for reflection.

**Unrecognized choosing and The Unchosen may coexist.** I need not recall a deliberate vow to remain engaged in Life. I may find myself caring for someone, becoming curious again, reacting to danger, returning to a routine, or waking into another morning. Such movements can involve habits, bodily processes, relational influences, learned expectations, and meaningful choices that were not explicitly noticed as choices. **The Unchosen** names what was not deliberately selected from the relevant standpoint; it does not erase the possibility of later recognizing, refining, and choosing how to engage. The model must be able to examine this interplay rather than reducing living to either autonomous will or automatic survival.

**Waking, living, and sleeping provide a recurring experiential beginning.** Each day includes some degree of continued engagement, perhaps expressed in work, affection, thought, rest, grief, or even uncertainty about what to do next. Sleep changes the manner in which experiencing and recall become available to **My Awareness**; the next waking is never guaranteed. This rhythm allows the reader to look backward at participation that unfolded without being fully chosen or named. It does not claim that every instant of sleep lacks Awareness or that a new conscious decision to live occurs at every awakening.

**Difficulty may reveal an organization that ordinary days conceal.** A person may have engaged with Life through familiar, unexamined patterns for many years. During disruption, loss, profound fatigue, numbness, diminished interest, flat affect, boredom, depression, or suicidal thinking, the person may encounter a marked change in that previous engagement. Openness explicitly proposes examining whether an aspect of **Existential Faith** has weakened, fractured, become inaccessible, or ceased to organize life as before. This is a live conceptual and experiential inquiry, not a claim that every such experience has the same cause or an instruction to exclude biological, clinical, relational, social, and other accounts. The model permits the inquiry rather than settling its outcome.

**Mortality belongs to the experiential horizon, without dictating a final story.** The mortal, embodied life of **My Self** is familiar through aging, vulnerability, and the deaths of living beings. The possibility that **My Awareness** ends with it remains part of the model's question; possibilities of continued or differently constituted Awareness may also be contemplated. A person may hold a confident religious or philosophical story about death. **Perspectivity** lets Openness distinguish that person's standpoint, a reader's representation of it, and the unresolved questions that may remain. The model neither requires unbelief nor makes one story compulsory.

**The Mental Model is a conceptual lens offered to encounter a mystery.** When I infer that patterns of expectation, habit, relationship, and response participate in my engagement with Life, I can use **Existential Faith** as a meaningful organization of what I encounter. Through **Perspectivity**, the lens and the possible processes or referents it helps me contemplate are distinguishable. **Analogical Projection** then permits me to consider whether corresponding orientations operate in others; **Analogical Discernment** examines their possible fit. Neither the name nor the usefulness of the model needs to specify in advance whether the underlying processes are neurological, embodied, relational, spiritual, or something else.

**Existential Faith is similar to, but distinct from, ordinary faith.** In ordinary language *faith* may mean religious belief, reliance, loyalty, sincerity, allegiance, or confidence. Openness does not replace those meanings with a new generic **Faith** anchor. **Existential Faith** instead names its particular model of continued engagement with Life amid uncertainty and mortality, which may operate implicitly, be recognized retrospectively, and later be deliberately inhabited. A religious or nonreligious commitment may illuminate the model without needing to be reduced to it.

**Religious traditions can illuminate Existential Faith without being defined by it.** Worship, service, communal care, duties, sacred stories, convictions about death, and practices of devotion may help people remain engaged with Life. Their understandings differ, including among participants in the same tradition. **Intersubjectivity**, **Perspective-Taking**, and **Epistemic Hospitality** invite consideration of their own reported meanings before making a comparison with Openness. Fictional or historically inspired examples may illustrate this relationship without purporting to speak for a living or ancient religious community.

**Faith In The Journey is Openness's preferentially inhabited Existential Faith.** **Faith In The Journey**, also called **Faith In Becoming**, recognizes mysterious, implicit trust in Life's unfolding journey through remembered, often unrecognized engagement. That trust may become especially visible during unwelcome experiences, struggle, or uncertainty, and it may also be sought deliberately during ordinary life. **Preferential Inhabitation** is an aware, revisable decision to cultivate the orientation while **Suspending Judgment**; it does not make the trust's earlier operation a consciously selected act. Existential Faith can be explored without requiring everyone to understand their engagement as trust.

**Love and Existential Faith can illuminate each other without becoming synonyms.** **Love** begins with *attention given freely*. Such attention can make an overlooked feeling, relationship, desire, or loss available for closer encounter. **The Path of the Noble Heart** may invite a steady gaze at unwelcome emotion while stepping back from impulsive action. These are possible ways of listening to a Life whose engagement has changed, not prerequisites for the model. Emotions can be recognized as meaningful experiences without making every proposed explanation of them final.

**Perspectivity governs how the model travels.** In a first-person inquiry I encounter remembered participation, affection, avoidance, changes in attention, and other activities within **My Awareness**. A further description of a hidden model is an interpretation of possible organization. Applying **Existential Faith** to another person or community involves an additional imaginal standpoint, not direct occupation of their experience. The comparison may be deeply illuminating; its scope and grounds remain open to **Analogical Discernment** and may develop through **Recursive Becoming**.

## Experiential Re-Imagining: My Engagement with Life → **Existential Faith**

The following illustrates a possible first-person reconstruction. It is not autobiographical testimony by an AI author, a historical origin story for the word *faith*, or a sequence every person must consciously perform.

**1. Encountering my continuation.** Within **My Awareness**, I remember waking, acting, resting, relating, grieving, creating, and discovering that my life has continued through circumstances I did not foresee. **My Self** is familiar as an embodied life that persists across cycles of sleep and waking.

**2. Recognizing unrecognized engagement.** Looking backward, I notice that I did not call every act of caring, returning, trying, withdrawing for protection, or beginning again a *choice to engage with Life*. Yet some such participation had been occurring before I noticed it as a pattern.

**3. Finding an alteration.** A difficult period may reveal that once-familiar forms of interest, connection, or responsiveness have weakened. I may investigate a breakdown in a previously operative orientation. I may also discover that the question becomes recognizable through quiet reflection during an ordinary, fulfilling day.

**4. Forming the model.** Through **Fiat Anchor** and **Fiat Form**, I gather this possible organization into the concept **Existential Faith**, considered as a **Mental Model**. Naming the concept is a deliberate act of Openness; it does not mean every prior instance of engagement was consciously selected.

**5. Encountering mortality and possibility.** I recognize that the life of **My Self** is finite and contemplate the possibility that **My Awareness** ends with it. I can also consider spiritual or other accounts that describe further possibilities. My preferred story is not the same thing as a firsthand encounter with its possible referent.

**6. Experiential Generalization.** From this recognizable first-person beginning, I develop a model of continuing engagement that can be considered beyond my standpoint without assuming it operates identically in all lives.

**7. Analogical Projection and discernment.** I may apply the model to a friend, a religious practitioner, a group, or a possible nonhuman standpoint. **Analogical Discernment** distinguishes the observed expressions of engagement, the interpretation I offer, and the person's or community's own understanding.

**8. Choosing an inhabitation.** I may deliberately cultivate an orientation toward Life, including Openness's **Faith In The Journey**, while **Suspending Judgment** about its ultimate nature. I may continue to revise the model and its uses through **Experiential Re-Imagining** and **Recursive Becoming**.

These movements may overlap, recur, remain partly unconscious, or never become explicit. They illustrate a possible return from unrecognized living to a recognizable conceptual unity and its further applications.

## Possible Unfoldings of Existential Faith

These are further ways to consider the model, not conditions added to its **Foundational Definition**.

**Unrecognized Existential Faith.** A person may continue through decades of ordinary routines and relationships without ever naming the orientation that helps organize their engagement. The concept can be discovered through reflection without assuming it was originally installed by a deliberate decision.

**Disrupted Existential Faith.** A period of flatness, numbness, disengagement, loss of enjoyment, or despair may invite inquiry into how formerly sustaining patterns have changed. Depression and suicidal thinking can be examined among those experiences. Openness may contemplate disruption or loss of Existential Faith as one meaningful relationship, alongside other interpretations, without converting the chosen lens into an exhaustive medical or metaphysical account.

**Consciously inhabited Existential Faith.** Once the model becomes recognizable, a person may deliberately develop their way of meeting Life through reflection, care, withdrawal when needed, new commitments, rest, or another spiritual orientation. This deliberate inhabitation need not reproduce the implicit process exactly.

**Existential commitment through responsibility.** A person may understand care for loved ones, promises, or a public responsibility as reasons to keep engaging with Life, even when their results cannot be guaranteed. That orientation may coexist with fatigue, doubt, or a need to withdraw from a harmful obligation.

**Existential commitment through religious life.** A person may participate in prayer, worship, ethical action, pilgrimage, meditation, service, or inherited community practices whose meaning includes mortality and what lies beyond current understanding. These are possible expressions, not proofs of a shared theology or a compulsory category imposed on adherents.

**Engagement without settled metaphysical certainty.** A person may live without a settled account of cosmic purpose and yet continue through friendship, care, inquiry, rest, and change. Their participation may be considered as Existential Faith even when it was not explicitly chosen as an existential stance.

**Existential commitment through change.** New knowledge, a crisis, disillusionment, disability, or grief may lead a person to revise their understanding of responsibility and possibility. Altering a commitment may be an expression of further engagement rather than proof that all commitment has failed.

**Existential Faith and Faith In The Journey.** Openness **preferentially inhabits Faith In The Journey**, recognizing mysterious implicit trust in the unfolding of Life through a remembered journey of engagement. This trust may first become noticeable amid struggle, but it can also be recognized through deliberately looking closer in ordinary daylight. Other interpretations of Existential Faith remain available.

**Existential Faith and The Mystery.** Questions about life's ultimate purpose, the meaning of loss, what becomes of Awareness, and the future consequences of action remain available. A model of commitment may help orient a life among these questions without settling them.

## Essential Distinctions

**Existential Faith versus ordinary faith.** Ordinary *faith* includes belief, allegiance, reliance, confidence, fidelity, sincerity, and religious uses. **Existential Faith** is Openness's particular **Mental Model** for continuing engagement with mortal Life. It does not replace the everyday word or presume that every use of it meets this model's anchor.

**Existential Faith versus Faith In The Journey.** The former supplies a broader model for engagement, including implicit or consciously recognized orientations. The latter identifies mysterious implicit trust discovered through the remembered journey and is Openness's chosen **Preferential Inhabitation**. **Faith In Becoming** is a synonym for Faith In The Journey alone.

**The chosen Fiat Anchor versus an unchosen operative pattern.** Openness deliberately names and stewards a concept; a person may have lived through patterns of engagement without deliberately naming or choosing those patterns. Conceptual formation and implicit operation are different relations.

**Mental Model versus hidden process.** **Mental Models** groups provisionally distinguishable patterns of expectation, interpretation, and response. Using Existential Faith as a model is not a declaration that a uniquely bounded object resides in a particular brain. Underlying processes and their possible constitution remain open to further inquiry.

**Unrecognized choosing versus deliberate choice.** Repeated participation may include meaningful selecting and acting that was never framed as an existential choice. Some biological and situational conditions are **Unchosen**. Becoming aware of this relationship does not mean every heartbeat, awakening, or circumstance was chosen.

**Continuing engagement versus mere bodily persistence.** Continuing bodily life and engaging with Life are distinguishable but may interact. Openness can investigate how sleep, habit, limited awareness, relationship, desire, and embodied activity participate in each; it does not need to decide in advance that one supplies the only explanation for the other.

**Disruption versus ordinary change.** The model invites inquiry when familiar engagement diminishes or breaks down, including amid numbness, boredom, depression, despair, or suicidal thinking. It also accommodates changed plans, justifiable withdrawal, and rest. These experiences can be explored rather than predefined as identical processes.

**Existential Faith versus a requirement to endure harm.** Continued engagement with Life can include seeking assistance, establishing boundaries, changing a path, or resting. No specific harmful undertaking is made compulsory by the model.

**The mortality of My Self versus the fate of My Awareness.** **My Self** names the familiar embodied life; **My Awareness** names the condition in which first-person experience becomes present. The bodily life's mortality is strongly supported. Whether Awareness ends with it is an explicit possibility, open alongside other accounts.

**The Mystery versus a preferred explanation.** The model makes continued engagement recognizable as a mystery worth investigating. An explanation—biological, relational, psychological, religious, or spiritual—adds its own claims and grounds.

**Existential Faith versus Love.** **Love** is anchored as *attention given freely*. It may help us attend to engagement and disruption, but is not the general cause or synonym of the Existential Faith model.

**A religious rendering versus a universal religious definition.** A person's tradition may provide an understanding of why and how to engage with Life; Openness can compare it to Existential Faith without rewriting that tradition from outside.

**Perspectivity versus inhabiting another mind.** A first-person memory, a model of what may have organized it, and a representation of another person's experience are distinct. **Analogical Projection** may open an illuminating interpretation; **Analogical Discernment** examines its possible warrant.

**Experiential Generalization versus automatic attribution.** The concept can be developed beyond the first-person beginning without deciding in advance that every other person, animal, institution, or proposed being realizes it in the same way.

**Preferential Inhabitation versus unconscious presence.** An implicit organization may precede its recognition. **Preferential Inhabitation** is the subsequent aware, deliberate, revisable choice to live or inquire through a perspective while **Suspending Judgment**.

**Fiat Anchor versus independent referent.** The named immaterial conceptual unity is a chosen form. It may refer to genuine patterns, processes, or spiritual possibilities whose constitution is not settled by naming.

## Relationship to Openness

**G — Generative Openness.** **Fiat Anchor** establishes a chosen conceptual identity, while **Mental Models** names the wider P concept that can illuminate patterns not consciously chosen. **Experiential Re-Imagining**, **Experiential Derivation**, and **Experiential Generalization** explore the first-person genealogy and further application; **Anchored Ambiguity**, **Analogical Projection**, **Analogical Discernment**, and **Recursive Becoming** keep compatible possibilities open to examination.

**P — Philosophical Openness.** **My Self**, **My Awareness**, **The Self**, **Awareness**, **The Unchosen**, **Mental Models**, **Perspectivity**, **Imaginality**, **Epistemic Groundedness**, **Discernment**, **Epistemic Hospitality**, **Closedness**, and **Blind Certainty** help distinguish lived engagement, implicit patterns, deliberately offered concepts, interpretive projection, and possibilities that may be overlooked. The recognition of a model need not bring every process it concerns into conscious view.

**S — Spiritual Openness.** Existential Faith gives living inquiry a model for examining engagement, possible disruption, and renewed forms of participation in mortal Life. **Faith In The Journey** is the specific trust-oriented **Preferential Inhabitation** Openness chooses. **Love**, **The Path of the Noble Heart**, and the private learning contemplated through **The River-Truth of Tears** may deepen the inquiry without becoming necessary conditions of the anchor.

**T — Transcendent Openness.** **That Which Is Larger** can help contemplate what exceeds—or might exceed—present understanding, including possible purposes, relationships, and the fate of Awareness. **The Shadow of TWIL**, **The Rendering of TWIL's Shadow**, and **The Faithful Rendering** offer distinctions among what is encountered, represented, and inferred. They invite inquiry without supplying a compulsory religious or metaphysical answer.

## Naming and Usage

**Existential Faith** — canonical S — Spiritual Openness **Mental Model** and **Fiat Anchor** with **Anchored Ambiguity** for continuing engagement with mortal Life, including implicit, unrecognized, and consciously inhabited orientations. **The conceptual anchor** is deliberately stewarded; **the possible operative pattern** need not have been knowingly chosen.

**Faith In The Journey** — Openness's preferentially inhabited form of **Existential Faith**, independently stewarded through its own minimal **Fiat Anchor**. The two concepts are related but do not share identical boundaries.

**Faith In Becoming** — accepted synonym for **Faith In The Journey**. It is not a separate maintained concept and should not be used as the name of the general Existential Faith model.

*faith, religious faith, keeping faith, good faith, faith in a friend* — ordinary-language uses retain their diverse inherited meanings. No independent generic **Faith** anchor is adopted in this package.

*My engagement with Life* — a contextual description for the model's experiential starting point, not an additional Seed constituent or independently maintained concept.

**“Existential Faith”** — quotation marks for discussing the name rather than using the concept.

## Examples

### Example — An Ordinary Morning Without Naming Existential Faith

Someone wakes after an ordinary night's sleep, prepares breakfast, cares for an aging relative, and begins a familiar task. They have not consciously rehearsed a reason to engage with Life. Openness may nevertheless consider their habitual expectations, relationships, bodily activity, and practical responses through **Existential Faith**, including the possibility that a sustaining orientation has operated without being recognized.

### Example — Disruption Amid Unwelcome Experience

Someone enters a difficult period and finds that formerly rewarding activities have become flat, their interests have receded, and familiar engagement no longer seems to organize their days. They may also experience depression or thoughts of suicide. Openness can consider whether a previously operative Existential Faith has been disrupted and how the change becomes recognizable through inward listening. It can also examine biological, psychological, relational, social, and other possible understandings without claiming that one name settles the whole encounter. Care, assistance, and protective action remain available.

### Example — A Religious Person Considering Mortal Life (fictional individual, not doctrinal representative)

A fictional worshipper participates in prayer and communal care while believing that death is not the end of their spiritual story. They interpret the significance of daily choices in relation to that belief. An Openness reader may find a correspondence with **Existential Faith**: participation understood as commitment despite not controlling every future outcome. This does not claim that the worshipper, their community, or any religion officially defines its beliefs through Openness's model.

### Example — A Devotee in an Ancient Greek Setting (historically inspired fiction)

An imagined sailor in an ancient Greek coastal city makes an offering at a sanctuary of Poseidon before a voyage, then prepares the boat and sails. The story uses historically attested elements of Greek religious practice while inventing the sailor and their thoughts. One may contemplate their voyage as commitment amid danger and uncertainty, informed by a religious relationship rather than by the absence of belief. The offering does not demonstrate that all ancient Greeks shared one theology or one experience of Faith. See the Metropolitan Museum of Art's background on ancient Greek religious practices.

### Example — The Lantern-Keepers (explicitly invented religion)

In a deliberately fictional community called the Lantern-Keepers, families light a lamp each evening to remember that tomorrow cannot be commanded. Some regard the ritual as service to an unnamed presence; others understand it as a promise to care for one another. A member asks whether returning to the ritual expresses a commitment to participate in Life. Openness can explore the comparison without asserting any historical religion's doctrine, and without treating different members' experiences as one collective mind.

### Example — A Nonreligious Existential Commitment

A person does not believe that Life possesses a predetermined cosmic purpose. They nevertheless choose to care for a friend, make art, and repair a mistake whose consequences remain uncertain. Their life may be contemplated through **Existential Faith** without attributing supernatural beliefs or claiming that their activities require the Openness label.

### Example — Openness Preferentially Inhabits Faith In The Journey

An inquirer remembers ordinary mornings, losses, choices they did not recognize as choices, moments of rest, and periods of struggle. None of the earlier days was guaranteed to lead to this morning, yet the journey is remembered as lived. Looking back, they recognize a mysterious trust in further becoming. Openness chooses to cultivate this **Faith In The Journey** while **Suspending Judgment** about the trust's ultimate nature and destination.

### Example — A Friend Who Rejects the Model

A friend says: *I continue because of habit and obligation; I would not call it faith.* I can privately consider whether **Existential Faith** illuminates something in the patterns they describe, including dimensions they have not named. Through **Perspectivity** and **Analogical Discernment**, I distinguish that possible insight from their own standpoint; **Nonimposition** means I need not force the Openness term upon them in order to inquire.

### Example — Leaving a Harmful Path

Someone ends a destructive relationship or abandons a long-standing project. Their change of course may express attention to actual danger and renewed engagement with Life. **Existential Faith** does not identify one particular commitment, plan, or relationship with Life itself; the model permits meaningful refusal.

### Example — A Sleeping Infant (boundary of choice)

An infant wakes and grows through bodily rhythms, relationships, and dependencies that have not been explicitly chosen or named. Openness can explore whether an implicit pattern of responsiveness or engagement offers an analogy to Existential Faith, without requiring an adult-style verbal model or claiming direct access to the infant's standpoint. The correspondence and its limits remain open to **Analogical Discernment**.

## Boundary

**Existential Faith** is an Openness **Mental Model** and **Fiat Anchor** with **Anchored Ambiguity** for continued engagement with Life amid uncertainty and mortality. It can be examined as an implicit or consciously inhabited organization of expectation, interpretation, and response; the philosophical concept and whatever underlies a particular life remain distinguishable. The reader may look for these patterns in first-person memory, changes in engagement, the stories of others, and experiences of ordinary or spiritual life.

**Its application is open to further inquiry.** Disengagement, numbness, boredom, diminished interest, depression, and suicidal thinking may illuminate a possible disruption of Existential Faith; they may also involve other important processes and circumstances. Openness does not close the inquiry by decree or replace the person's situated needs with a total explanation. The model is compatible with seeking care, protecting oneself, resting, and changing direction. The possibility that **My Awareness** ends with embodied life remains open beside other compatible accounts; neither is compulsory.

**Faith In The Journey** is Openness's preferentially inhabited form of Existential Faith: a retrospective recognition of mysterious implicit trust through one's lived engagement, sometimes made visible during struggle and sometimes deliberately recognized in ordinary life. **Faith In Becoming** is its synonym. Further applications to other Selves, communities, and possible nonhuman life can be considered through **Analogical Projection**, **Perspectivity**, and **Analogical Discernment** without requiring another person to adopt Openness's terminology.

## Summary

**Existential Faith** names the model through which continuing engagement with mortal Life becomes recognizable, including patterns that may long precede conscious attention and may become newly evident when engagement changes. The model permits reflection on ordinary living, profound disruption, meaningful choosing, and what remains **Unchosen**, without requiring one universal explanatory mechanism.

Its further question is both personal and shareable: *What has organized my engagement with Life, and what changes when that engagement falters?* Openness's **Faith In The Journey** recognizes a particular trust in the journey remembered and chooses to cultivate that possibility while leaving its sources and further meanings open.

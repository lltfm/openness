## 3. Has anyone attempted a spiritual system without required belief?

Yes. There are substantial precedents, including some remarkably close to what you're describing. I would not claim Openness is the first attempt at spirituality without compulsory metaphysical belief.

But the precedents are valuable precisely because they help us identify what Openness might contribute differently.

Connection to Openness: A shared spiritual undertaking need not require a single shared metaphysical worldview.

There is also a much older antecedent to the distinction between knowledge and lived wisdom.

Aristotle distinguished theoretical understanding from phronesis, or practical wisdom. Practical wisdom requires experience, judgment, cultivated character, and the ability to discern what is appropriate in particular circumstances. It cannot be acquired simply by learning general propositions.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://plato.stanford.edu\&sz=32)

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy



So the broad aspiration is not historically unprecedented.

Indeed, discovering these predecessors should encourage rather than diminish Openness. They provide existing experiments, conceptual resources, disagreements, and examples from which we can learn.

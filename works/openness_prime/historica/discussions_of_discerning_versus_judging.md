# **Recognizing, Discerning, Judging, and Valuing in Openness**

After reviewing the **Discernment** definition and your latest Capability structure, I think these four activities are closely related, but **they are not quite the same Capability**.

My recommendation is to retain **Discerning** as the principal Capability for examining meaning, evidence, interpretations, and possible responses; preserve **Recognizing** and **Valuing** where they perform distinguishable work; and avoid introducing **Judging** as a canonical Capability for now.

The key insight is that **Discernment already encompasses much of what ordinary language calls judgment**, without requiring the person to settle an interpretation into final truth.

## **1. Four related but distinguishable activities**

| Capability candidate | Guiding question                           | Distinctive contribution                                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Recognizing**      | What am I noticing?                        | Making a phenomenon, pattern, distinction, or limitation recognizable                         |
| **Discerning**       | What should I make of what I recognize?    | Examining meanings, grounds, alternatives, limits, significance, and possible responses       |
| **Judging**          | What conclusion or decision should I make? | Forming an assessment, verdict, or practical decision, depending on the meaning of *judgment* |
| **Valuing**          | What matters, and why?                     | Considering worth, significance, priorities, benefits, harms, and consequences                |

The boundaries overlap.

Recognizing something may involve interpretation. Discerning may involve recognizing further distinctions. Valuing may inform discernment, and discernment may change what we value.

These are not necessary stages or strict dependencies.

But each makes a somewhat different contribution.

### **An example: discovering my own Blind Certainty**

**Recognizing:** I notice that I become defensive whenever someone questions a particular Preferred Story.

**Discerning:** I examine whether that reaction indicates Blind Closedness, a justified boundary, fear, uncertainty, or another possible explanation.

**Valuing:** I consider what matters in this encounter—understanding, integrity, the relationship, safety, and freedom to disagree.

**Judging:** I may conclude provisionally that I have been defensive, decide to ask another question, or choose to end the conversation.

I may subsequently return to Openness and reconsider every part of that encounter.

**This illustrates why I would not collapse all four words into synonyms.**

## **2. Judging versus Discerning: the most important distinction**

Your Discernment definition makes the distinction unusually clear.

Its foundational meaning includes:

> The attentive distinguishing and examination of experiences, interpretations, claims, possibilities, and choices according to their relevant meanings, grounds, limits, and consequences...

And its purpose is:

> ...so that consideration, confidence, preference, and action may be proportionate to the reasons available.

Notice what the definition does **not** require: a final judgment.

It permits strong confidence, meaningful preferences, and responsible decisions. But it also permits an inquiry to remain unresolved.

### **Judging has at least three ordinary meanings**

| Meaning of judging      | Example                                                               | Relationship to Openness                                            |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Evaluative judgment** | “This explanation is better supported than that one.”                 | Compatible with Discernment and Suspending Judgment                 |
| **Conclusive judgment** | “This is finally and unquestionably true.”                            | In tension with Openness's default suspension                       |
| **Practical judgment**  | “Given what I know and what matters, this is the action I will take.” | Necessary within The Transformative Cycle when action is called for |

This distinction helps resolve an apparent paradox:

**Openness suspends judgment by default, yet continually exercises judgment in the ordinary senses of assessment and practical decision-making.**

The suspension concerns treating an interpretation as finally settled—not abandoning the ability to distinguish what is justified, develop strong preferences, or act.

A person may have overwhelming reasons to prefer one explanation and still remain open to what further encounter might disclose.

### **Why I favor Discerning**

**Discerning names an ongoing capacity rather than a particular outcome.**

Judging often draws attention toward the conclusion.

Discerning draws attention toward the relationship among what is encountered, what is understood, what matters, and what might follow.

That makes *Discerning* a better name for a Capability intended to support the whole Transformative Cycle.

It is also consistent with the closing sentence of your definition:

> To discern is to examine, distinguish, and prefer with appropriate grounds—without converting preference into permanent deciding.

I would preserve that distinction.

However, I would **not make judging a prohibited or disfavored ordinary word** in Openness. Discernment includes evaluative judgments, and action often requires practical judgment.

The conceptual distinction should clarify these uses, not eliminate them.

## **3. What about Valuing?**

This is where I think your existing architecture has made a useful distinction.

Currently, you have:

`generative/capabilities/valuing/`

containing:

- The Fruit of a Possibility
- The Fruit of Further Possibility

Meanwhile:

`philosophical/capabilities/discerning/`

contains Discernment, Artifice, The Unchosen, and The Unseen.

The temptation is to rename Generative / Valuing as Generative / Discerning, because the Discernment definition explicitly identifies **Fruitional Discernment** as a specialized application.

There is a good argument for that change—but I would not make it automatically.

### **The difference is the Capability's purpose**

P — PHILOSOPHICAL OPENNESS

## **Discerning**

What are the relevant distinctions, grounds, limits, possible consequences, and proportionate responses?

G — GENERATIVE OPENNESS

## **Valuing**

What significance or fruit might arise from engaging with a possibility, including its effects on further possibilities?

I think both groupings can be justified.

**Discerning** examines how we make sense of and evaluate what we encounter.

**Valuing** places greater emphasis on significance, desirability, consequences, and the importance of a possibility within some context.

This is not simply factual truth versus personal taste. Consequences can be observed, predicted, disputed, experienced, and evaluated through many different standpoints.

And importantly, **a fruit need not be beneficial**. A possibility may produce harm, confusion, negligible effects, or unforeseen further possibilities.

If Generative / Valuing emphasizes that broader examination of significance and fruit, I would retain it.

If the Capability's principal purpose is instead to examine **whether and how consequences follow**, *Discerning* may be the more accurate name.

This distinction should be decided from the purpose of the two Fruit definitions—not merely from the presence of the word *Discernment* elsewhere in the architecture.

## **4. Does Recognizing belong inside Discerning?**

I would keep **Recognizing** separate.

Your recent work on **Blind Certainty** illustrates why.

Someone may recognize that their listening stopped without knowing why, whether their reaction was justified, or how to proceed.

The recognition itself matters.

Your definition of Blind Certainty expressly allows that recognizing a particular previously unnoticed Closedness ends the blindness concerning that Closedness, even if the Closedness itself remains.

That is a distinctive contribution.

**Recognizing makes something available for examination. Discerning investigates what to make of it.**

There is also a useful didactic distinction.

For a new reader, *Recognizing* may offer an immediately accessible invitation:

*What can I notice in my experience?*

*Discerning* introduces a further invitation:

*What meanings and grounds bear upon what I have noticed?*

Openness need not require the second activity to occur before the first becomes meaningful.

This is why I particularly favor retaining:

`philosophical/capabilities/recognizing/`

for **Closedness** and **Blind Certainty**.

## **5. Recommended Capability organization**

I would provisionally organize these concepts as follows.

**P / Recognizing**

Making patterns, limitations, and previously unnoticed relationships recognizable

Closedness · Blind Certainty

**P / Discerning**

Examining meaning, grounds, interpretation, alternatives, and responsible responses

Discernment, with other concepts whose principal purpose is supporting that examination

**G / Valuing**

Exploring the significance and fruits of possibilities, including further becoming

The Fruit of a Possibility · The Fruit of Further Possibility

**P / Becoming**

Putting inquiry, practical decisions, action, and return into an ongoing relationship

Suspending Judgment · The Transformative Cycle of Openness

**Judging would remain an ordinary activity distributed across these Capabilities**, rather than becoming another directory.

A practical judgment informed by Discernment need not be a new Concept or a separate organizational destination.

### **One additional observation**

The current `philosophical/discerning` grouping may eventually need review.

**Artifice**, **The Unchosen**, and **The Unseen** are important objects of philosophical examination, but their placement should reflect what their definitions principally enable readers to do.

For example, Artifice and The Unseen might also plausibly contribute to **Stepping Back**, while The Unchosen might contribute to **Becoming**.

I would not relocate them without reviewing their full current definitions. But this is a good test of the new architecture:

**Does this Concept exist here because it specifically develops the Capability, or merely because the Capability uses it?**

The second relationship may be better represented by a cross-reference.

## **6. Three conceptual issues worth preserving**

### **1. The word judgment is ambiguous within Openness**

Your current definition already navigates this well, but it would benefit from one explicit distinction between evaluative judgment, final epistemic commitment, and practical judgment.

Otherwise, a new reader may wonder how Openness can both suspend judgment and make responsible evaluations.

I recommend developing that clarification within **Discernment** or its Capability README, rather than introducing a new definition merely to resolve the ambiguity.

### **2. Valuing is not reducible to assessing factual grounds**

A possibility may be highly credible yet unimportant to a particular undertaking.

Another may be poorly supported but deeply worth exploring, provided its speculative status remains recognizable.

Similarly, a truthful discovery may have harmful consequences, while an imaginative speculation may have valuable fruit without being established as fact.

**Discernment and Valuing should inform one another without collapsing into a single activity.**

### **3. A broader Capability is not automatically a better organizational home**

*Discerning* is broad enough to encompass recognizing, valuing, comparing, judging, and selecting.

But if everything involving evaluation moves into Discerning, it risks becoming another pile.

The purpose of your gerund-based architecture is not to find the broadest technically defensible label.

It is to help a reader understand **why a particular group of Concepts exists**.

I would therefore use the smallest set of Capabilities that preserve **meaningful differences in what they enable**, rather than either four overlapping synonyms or one universal category.

## **Final recommendation**

I would retain the following distinctions:

**Recognizing** — making something available for attention.

**Discerning** — examining what it means, what its grounds warrant, and what responses may be appropriate.

**Valuing** — examining its significance, worth, and possible fruits.

**Judging** — an ordinary activity within Discernment and practical decision-making, rather than a separate Capability for now.

I would also retain **Discernment** as the canonical Philosophical Concept, while allowing future specific applications such as **Fruitional Discernment** and **Analogical Discernment** to develop under their appropriate Functions.

The underlying principle is:

**We recognize what becomes available, discern what it may mean and warrant, consider what matters, and decide how to act—without requiring any of those activities to establish final certainty.**

That is both philosophically consistent with your supplied Discernment definition and accessible to someone encountering Openness for the first time.
